# Monetization models for a video creator beyond brand sponsorships (2025-2026)

> Research date: November 20, 2026. US-based creator assumed.
> Provenance caveat for the report writer: direct page fetches were blocked in this session (network allowlist), so every finding below comes from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the full pages. Official pages (YouTube Help, Meta, Snap, X, Twitch, IRS, Patreon) are linked where the search surfaced them, but exact wording was not verified in the original. Third-party blogs, calculator sites and vendor marketing are labelled as such. Treat every threshold as "check in the platform dashboard before acting".

## 1. Platform ad-revenue programs: thresholds and real pay per 1,000 views

### Takeaway
Long-form YouTube is the only platform program that pays dollars rather than cents per 1,000 views (all-niche median about $2.30, gaming about $2.05 in a 300-channel dataset); Shorts, Reels and TikTok pay cents to about $1 per 1,000 and are getting harder to qualify for. Several programs changed materially in 2026: YouTube doubles its ad-revenue entry bar on February 1, 2027, X ended Creator Revenue Sharing in September 2026, TikTok RPMs reportedly fell after the January 2026 US ownership change, and Twitch opened subs/Bits to nearly all streamers.

### Cited Findings

**YouTube Partner Program (YPP)**
- Current two-tier structure: fan-funding/Shopping tier at 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 public watch hours (365 days) or 3 million public Shorts views (90 days); ad and Premium revenue tier at 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours (365 days) or 10 million Shorts views (90 days). The watch-hour and Shorts routes are alternatives. — [vidIQ YPP guide, 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/monetize-youtube-guide/); [YouTube Help: expanded YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en); [YouTube Help: YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)
- CHANGE ANNOUNCED August 10, 2026, effective February 1, 2027: new applicants for ad/Premium revenue need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 qualified watch hours in the prior 365 days, or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days (both double the current bar). The 1,000-subscriber requirement is unchanged. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/); [Tubefilter, Aug 10 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/); [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/new-youtube-creators-will-need-8000-watch-hours-to-start-earning-from-ads-double-the-current-bar)
- Current YPP members are grandfathered on long-form ad revenue and do not have to meet the new entry thresholds. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)
- Catch for existing members: a new ongoing Shorts threshold applies to them too. From February 1, 2027, Shorts ad revenue sharing requires at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days; members below the cutoff have Shorts ads paused and restarted automatically once back above it. Existing creators must review and accept updated modules before January 31, 2027. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/YouTube-s-8-000-hour-rule-also-has-a-catch-for-existing-channels.1366446.0.html); [vidIQ on the 2027 changes](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- Revenue split: creators keep 55% of long-form ad revenue and 45% of their allocated Shorts pool revenue. Shorts feed ad revenue is pooled monthly, part is diverted to music licensing, and a creator's allocation follows their share of engaged Shorts views. Shorts revenue sharing began February 1, 2023. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Long-form RPM, best dataset found: AIR Media-Tech analysed about 300 monetized channels from May 2025 to May 2026. All-niche median RPM about $2.30 per 1,000 views; Gaming median $2.05; Education & Science median $10.22 (P25-P75 $2.31 to $19.50); Business & Finance about $2.01 (flagged: fewer than ten channels, directional only); Kids & Teens $0.33. AIR is a creator-services vendor, but this is its own channel data. — [AIR Media-Tech, RPM data from 300 channels](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels); [AIR niche ranking](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- Other gaming long-form estimates from SEO guides range $2 to $8 per 1,000 views; one describes gaming as the largest category by views and among the lowest-paying by RPM. Low confidence, undisclosed methods. — [Fluxnote gaming RPM guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-rpm-gaming-niche-2026)
- Shorts RPM, creator-disclosed: six long-form creators told Digiday their Shorts RPMs were consistently under $0.20 versus $3 to $6 average long-form RPM. vidIQ reports its own channel's Shorts RPM at about $0.10 per 1,000. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/how-youtube-shorts-revenue-compares-to-long-form-video-revenue-for-creators); [vidIQ Shorts monetization](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/)
- Shorts RPM, dataset: an AIR Media-Tech study of 274 channels found Shorts RPM from $0.02 to $1.48 per 1,000 views (reported second-hand). Most blog estimates cluster at $0.04 to $0.10. — [Nerdbot reprint of AIR data](https://nerdbot.com/?p=311820); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/)

**TikTok Creator Rewards Program**
- Official framing (March 2024 launch): rewards "high-quality, original content" over one minute long, with a formula weighted on originality, play duration, search value and audience engagement. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program)
- Commonly reported eligibility (third-party, not confirmed on an official page in this session): 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in the last 30 days, age 18+, personal account (not business/political), eligible country; only original public videos longer than one minute earn. — [ttcalculator guide](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/creator-rewards-program/); [Daily Dot / Passionfruit](https://www.dailydot.com/passionfruit/tiktok-creator-rewards-program)
- RPM is paid on "qualified views", a filtered subset of public views. Third-party ranges conflict: $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views (most common), $0.50 to $2.00, or $0.80 to $1.20 after a claimed January 2026 update. TikTok publishes no rate. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-much-does-tiktok-pay-per-view); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/); [ttcalculator payout update](https://ttcalculator.net/blog/creator-rewards-payout-update-2026/)
- 2026 instability: after TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC took over US operations in January 2026, many creators documented sharp RPM drops, some citing as low as $0.04 per 1,000 in early 2026; TikTok has not publicly addressed it. Evidence is anecdotal (creator posts, blogs). — [EURweb](https://eurweb.com/tiktok-payout-drop/); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/tiktok-creators-rewards-programme-faces-drastic-pay-reductions-that-are-forcing-career-exit-plans-1773819); [TheWrap Creatorverse column](https://www.thewrap.com/commentary-analysis/columns/creatorverse-shortform-creators-payment-programs/)
- May 2026 reports describe creators having viral videos disqualified before payout with no explanation or working appeal. Anecdotal. — [Tech Issues Today](https://techissuestoday.com/tiktok-creators-cry-foul-as-viral-videos-get-disqualified-before-payday); [Baller Alert](https://balleralert.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-disqualification/)

**Facebook Content Monetization and Creator Fast Track**
- Meta says it paid nearly $3 billion to creators in 2025, up 35% year over year, and is adding an "Earnings Rate" metric showing approximate earnings per 1,000 qualified views plus a non-qualified views breakdown. — [Meta Newsroom, Mar 18 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/creator-fast-track-grow-your-audience-earn-money-on-facebook/); [Meta for Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/new-tips-to-help-you-earn-on-facebook-content-monetization)
- Creator Fast Track (launched March 18, 2026): guaranteed $1,000 per month for creators with at least 100,000 followers on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube, or $3,000 per month for those with over 1 million, for posting Reels on Facebook. — [Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/creator-fast-track-grow-your-audience-earn-money-on-facebook/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/18/facebook-launches-a-new-monetization-program-to-attract-popular-creators-from-tiktok-youtube/)
- Content Monetization (the 2024-2025 consolidation of in-stream ads, Reels ads and performance bonus) is reported by third-party guides as still invite-only, with invites "sent out periodically" and no published criteria. A third-party guide cites 10,000 followers, 600,000 minutes watched in 60 days and 5+ videos as thresholds; Meta has not confirmed these. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-make-money-on-facebook/); [ShortSync guide (vendor)](https://www.shortsync.app/resources/facebook-content-monetization-program-2026)
- Per-1,000 payout claims conflict wildly: $1 to $10 per 1,000 (one guide) versus $0.02 to $0.20 per 1,000 Reels views with a median near $0.05 to $0.10 (another). No verified creator disclosures found. — [ShortSync (vendor)](https://www.shortsync.app/resources/facebook-content-monetization-program-2026); [PostPlanify (vendor)](https://postplanify.com/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views)

**Instagram**
- Instagram has no open ad-revenue share for Reels; the Reels Play bonus ended in 2023 and remaining bonuses are invite-only and limited (one source: US, Japan, South Korea). — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-monetization/); [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-play-bonus-update/)
- Gifts on Reels: viewers buy Stars; Instagram pays $0.01 per Star, monthly, $25 minimum payout. Follower minimums conflict: a 2026 guide says 500 for Gifts and 10,000 for Subscriptions; an older Meta page says 5,000 followers for Gifts. Live Badges: creator gets badge price minus a 30% app-store fee, $100 payout minimum. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-subscriptions-gifts/); [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/giving-more-creators-around-the-world-ways-to-earn-money)

**Snapchat**
- Unified Monetization Program effective February 1, 2025 (Spotlight Rewards ended January 31, 2025): 50,000+ followers, 25+ posts per month to Saved Stories or Spotlight; creators "may be eligible for invitation". View-time threshold reported as 12,000 hours in 28 days by one outlet and 15,000 by another. — [Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/snapchat-new-creator-monetization); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/snapchat-launches-streamlined-creator-monetization-process/735668/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/12/16/snapchat-new-monetization-program-shutting-down-spotlight-rewards/)

**X**
- X closed Creator Revenue Sharing: new enrollments ended August 7, 2026 and the program retired September 7, 2026. Its replacement, "Original Content Rewards", opened September 8, 2026 and pays on qualified impressions of original content (reposts excluded); minimum payout $30; requires identity verification and a Stripe or X Money payout account. — [X Help Center](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/creator-revenue-sharing.html); [Tubefilter, Aug 11 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/11/x-monetization-original-content-rewards-program-creators/); [American Bazaar](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/08/08/x-ends-creator-revenue-share-program-final-payout-coming-in-september-486028/)
- The old program required 5 million organic impressions in three months and 2,000 active Premium-subscribed followers; thresholds for the new program were not confirmed in sources found. — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/de/article/news-x-clickbait-payouts-creator-program-problem/)

**Twitch (relevant if streaming)**
- "Monetization for All" (rolling out from May 13, 2026, US first): most streamers can turn on subs, Bits, emotes and Channel Points before Affiliate status; payouts still require Affiliate; $50 minimum payout. — [Twitch Blog, May 13 2026](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2026/05/13/monetization-for-all/); [Streams Charts](https://streamscharts.com/news/twitch-expands-monetization-access-all-streamers)
- 2026 guides list Affiliate at 25 followers, 4 hours streamed, 4 broadcast days, 3 average concurrent viewers (older guides say 50 followers / 500 minutes / 7 days). Standard sub split is 50%. — [streamer.guide](https://streamer.guide/guides/twitch-affiliate-requirements); [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/twitch-affiliate-vs-partner-requirements)

### Inferences
- Per 1,000 views, order of magnitude for a gaming/game-dev creator: YouTube long-form about $2 (AIR gaming median) versus Shorts roughly $0.04 to $0.20, TikTok roughly $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views (fewer than public views, and apparently lower in 2026), Facebook Reels unverified, Instagram effectively $0. Long-form is worth roughly 10x to 50x a short-form view in ad terms; short clips should be treated as audience acquisition, not income.
- Timing matters today: a channel that can reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours and get accepted before February 1, 2027 avoids the 8,000-hour bar. Whether an application must be approved, or merely submitted, before that date was not verified.
- From February 2027 the rolling 10M-Shorts-views requirement means small channels will earn no Shorts ad revenue at all, even inside YPP. This strengthens the case for non-ad monetization of short-form reach.
- Program rules changed on four of six platforms within 2026 alone; ad-program income is the least controllable revenue line.

### Gaps
- No Business Insider creator-disclosed RPM stories could be retrieved; the only first-hand RPM disclosures found were Digiday's six creators and vidIQ's own channel.
- No verified per-1,000 figures for Facebook Content Monetization, Snapchat or X's new program.
- Sources disagree on whether the 2027 Shorts threshold for existing members is exactly 10M per rolling 90 days; confirm on YouTube's own page.
- Game-development (devlog/tutorial) long-form RPM specifically was not found; education-style dev content may sit above the $2.05 gaming median but that is unconfirmed.
- TikTok's official current eligibility page was not surfaced; the 10k/100k thresholds are from third parties.

## 2. Reused / inauthentic content and originality rules when clipping your own long video

### Takeaway
Cutting clips from your own long-form video is not what YouTube's reused-content policy targets (it targets other people's material and mass-produced templated output), but reposted or duplicated short-form can lose reach, cross-platform watermarks can make your own content look reused, and TikTok's one-minute minimum plus opaque "originality" disqualifications make clip monetization unreliable there.

### Cited Findings
- July 15, 2025: YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content"; YouTube said the definition (mass-produced or repetitive content) was unchanged and that the separate reused-content policy was not affected. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/)
- YouTube's reused-content policy concerns repurposing someone else's content without significant original commentary or value; it applies even with the original creator's permission, and is judged at channel level, so monetization can be removed from the whole channel. — [YouTube Help: channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- A vidIQ guide lists your own footage reused across uploads among allowed examples; a less authoritative guide warns that uploads carrying another platform's watermark can look reused even when they are yours. — [vidIQ reused content guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/); [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/reused-content-youtube-guide/)
- YouTube changed Shorts recommendations so reposted clips get less distribution than originals; this is a reach change rather than a new monetization rule. — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774/); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/phones/youtube-cracks-down-on-reposted-shorts-with-a-new-recommendation-update/)
- Shorts longer than one minute that contain claimed (Content ID) content are blocked and ineligible for monetization. — [YouTube Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Meta, July 14, 2025: accounts that repeatedly reuse someone else's content lose Facebook monetization access for a period and get reduced distribution; "just stitching clips or adding a watermark isn't enough"; commentary, reactions and voice-over remain eligible; creators can see penalty risk in the Support home screen; Meta is testing links from duplicates back to originals. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/meta-says-its-cracking-down-on-facebook-creators-who-steal-content-203713569.html); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/meta-follows-youtube-in-crackdown-on-unoriginal-content/551096/)
- TikTok defines originality as quality content unique to the creator; third-party guides say Duets/Stitches, content with someone else's watermark, and lightly modified reposts are treated as unoriginal and excluded from earnings. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program); [InfluenceFlow guide (third party)](https://influenceflow.io/resources/master-guide-to-tiktoks-original-content-standards-in-2026/)
- X's new Original Content Rewards excludes reposts and centres on originality. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/11/x-monetization-original-content-rewards-program-creators/)

### Inferences
- Practical rules for self-clipping: export clean masters (no TikTok/CapCut watermark) for each platform; re-edit hooks/captions per platform rather than uploading byte-identical files; keep TikTok cuts over 60 seconds if Creator Rewards income matters; avoid music that triggers claims on Shorts over one minute.
- The platforms' rules all target third-party reuse, so hiring clippers to post your content on their own accounts (section 8) pushes those accounts, not yours, into "reused content" territory; the clips there are marketing, not monetizable inventory.

### Gaps
- No official statement was found from YouTube, Meta or TikTok that explicitly addresses a creator posting cut-downs of their own long video; the conclusion rests on policy wording aimed at "someone else's content" plus third-party guides.
- Whether TikTok's or Meta's duplicate detection penalises the same creator posting identical clips across TikTok, Reels, Facebook and Shorts was not established.

## 3. Affiliate marketing

### Takeaway
Affiliate income is available almost from day one (YouTube Shopping affiliate now opens at 500 subscribers, TikTok Shop at roughly 1,000 to 5,000 followers, Amazon Associates and SaaS programs need no audience minimum that was found), but rates for games on Amazon are only 1 to 2%, so recurring software commissions (about 20 to 30%) and seller-set TikTok Shop/YouTube Shopping rates (about 10 to 15%) are where the money is.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Shopping affiliate: on March 27, 2026 YouTube dropped the separate 10,000-subscriber requirement; it is now open to YPP creators at 500 subscribers (3 uploads in 90 days and 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views). Merchants set their own commission and attribution window per product. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shopping-expansion-500-subscribers/); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/annabel-burba/youtube-just-lowered-the-bar-for-affiliate-earnings-now-creators-with-500-subscribers-can-cash-in/91321914); [YouTube Help: Shopping affiliate eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13376398?hl=en)
- Amazon Associates (US) standard rates surfaced from the official statement: digital video games 2%; physical video games and consoles 1%. Third-party guides also cite 20% for "Amazon Games" and about 4% for "all other" categories; the 20% row was not confirmed on the official page. — [Amazon Associates commission statement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ); [Lasso guide (third party)](https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/)
- TikTok Shop affiliate (US): most third-party sources say 1,000 followers to join (a "pilot" tier), with 5,000 followers unlocking fuller access; some say 5,000 outright. Commissions are set by sellers: about 13% US average per one source, 5 to 15% for physical goods, 10 to 20% for beauty, overall 2 to 20%. All third-party; verify in TikTok Shop Creator Center. — [ManyChat](https://manychat.com/blog/tiktok-shop-affiliate-program/); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/ie/blog/tiktok-affiliate-marketing); [Advertise Purple](https://www.advertisepurple.com/understanding-tiktok-shop-affiliate-program-requirements/)
- SaaS/software recurring programs: typical commission 20 to 30% recurring (Rewardful average 24.16%; LinkJolt median 20% across 121 campaigns), commonly capped at 12 to 24 months. Click-to-purchase benchmarks 1.5 to 3% average (top decile 5 to 8%); click-to-free-trial 3.5 to 5%. Vendor-blog data, inconsistent between sources. — [Refgrow benchmarks 2026 (vendor)](https://refgrow.com/blog/affiliate-program-benchmarks-2026); [Supademo list (vendor)](https://supademo.com/blog/saas-affiliate-programs)
- Kajabi's survey reported affiliate marketing income down 36% year over year for creators (survey of 1,717, Dec 2024 to Jan 2025; Kajabi sells owned-product tooling). — [Tubefilter, Apr 18 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/04/18/creator-entrepreneur-revenue-stream-kajabi-state-of-creator-commerce/amp); [Kajabi](https://kajabi.com/state-of-creator-commerce-25)
- Game-specific affiliate: Epic's Support-A-Creator pays a developer-set share with a 5% minimum on Epic Games Store sales via creator code or link; Xsolla Partner Network runs key campaigns and revenue-share campaigns; Steam has no individual affiliate program (curators earn 0%). — [Epic Support-A-Creator overview](https://sac.epicgames.com/overview/); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/22/06/27/epics-support-a-creator-program-pays-out-only-5-of-game-content-makers-sales); [Xsolla docs](https://developers.xsolla.com/creators/campaigns-cc/); [UpPromote directory (third party)](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/steam/)

### Inferences
- Illustrative math (not sourced): 10,000 long-form views with a 1% click rate and 3% purchase conversion yields 3 sales; at a $60 Amazon game (1 to 2%) that is $2 to $4, while a $20/month dev tool at 25% recurring for 12 months is about $180. For a game-dev channel, tool/asset/course/hosting affiliates beat game-sale affiliates by an order of magnitude or more per click.
- Affiliate works at tiny audience sizes only when content has purchase intent (tutorials, tool comparisons, "what I use"); entertainment clips rarely carry it.

### Gaps
- No reliable benchmark was found for affiliate earnings per 1,000 YouTube views or for description-link click-through rates; only anecdote that YouTube viewers convert better than social clicks.
- Rates for Humble Partner, Nexus creator stores, Unity/Unreal/Fab asset-store affiliate programs and itch.io were not found.
- Amazon Associates' minimum-sales and account-approval rules were not retrieved.

## 4. Audience-direct revenue: memberships, tips, gifts, subscriptions, paid communities, newsletters

### Takeaway
Fan funding unlocks earlier than ads (YouTube at 500 subscribers; Twitch almost immediately; TikTok LIVE gifts at about 1,000 followers), but realistic conversion is low single digits or below: roughly 0.5 to 3% of YouTube subscribers for memberships and 1 to 3% free-to-paid for newsletters. The "1,000 true fans" model works through price, not volume.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube channel memberships: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, 3,000 watch hours (365 days) or 3M Shorts views (90 days), age 18+, Commerce Product Module accepted. Super Chat/Super Stickers/Super Thanks sit in the same fan-funding module and share one revenue split. — [YouTube Help: expanded YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en); [YouTube Help: Super Thanks](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10878910?hl=en)
- Creator share on Supers and memberships is widely reported as 70% (after app-store fees and taxes); this figure came from vidIQ, not an official page in this session. — [vidIQ Super Thanks guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-super-thanks/)
- YouTube membership conversion: third-party guides estimate 0.5 to 3% of subscribers, or 0.5 to 2% of active viewers; average membership price about $4.99/month. YouTube publishes no conversion or churn data. — [Fluxnote (third party)](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-memberships-earnings-guide); [YTGrowth (third party)](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-channel-memberships)
- Patreon: pages published after August 4, 2025 pay a flat 10% platform fee (legacy pages keep 5%, 8% or 11%) plus payment processing; Patreon reported passing $10 billion in cumulative creator payouts and 25 million paid memberships as of August 2025; Apple requires remaining legacy-billing creators to move to subscription billing by November 1, 2026, exposing iOS purchases to App Store fees. — [Patreon Help](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/36426991446797); [Bloomberg, Jun 16 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-16/patreon-raises-fees-for-new-creators-to-support-video-content); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/patreon)
- A 2026 blog estimates Patreon conversion at 3 to 6% of audience for $3 to $5 tiers and 1 to 3% for $8 to $12 tiers; methodology unverified, low confidence. — [EarnifyHub (third party)](https://earnifyhub.com/creator-economy/patreon-strategy-membership-income-2026)
- TikTok LIVE gifts: about 1,000 followers and age 18+ to receive gifts; creators keep roughly 50% (estimates of TikTok's cut range 50 to 70%). TikTok Subscriptions: base 70% share after store fees, up to 90% with a bonus that one source ties to 10,000 followers and 1M monthly views. All third-party. — [Tikfinity (vendor)](https://blog.tikfinity.com/how-to-make-money-on-tiktok-live-in-2025/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-expands-creator-subscription-payouts-us-canada/801996/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-us-creator-revenue-share)
- Instagram Subscriptions/Gifts/Badges: see section 1 (Gifts at $0.01 per Star; Subscriptions reportedly need 10,000 followers). — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-subscriptions-gifts/)
- Paid newsletters: Substack historically told writers to expect 5 to 10% free-to-paid; observed averages are about 3%, with 1 to 2% for broad publications, 1 to 3% for small-to-medium lists, and 4 to 10% for small specialised ones. — [Really Good Business Ideas](https://www.reallygoodbusinessideas.com/p/substack-average-paid-subscriber-conversion-rate); [Simon Owens](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/whats-a-realistic-conversion-rate)
- Skool (paid community host), 2026 pricing: Hobby $9/month plus 10% per sale; Pro $99/month plus 2.9%; median paid community price reported as $27/month. — [Uscreen review of Skool pricing (competitor)](https://www.uscreen.tv/blog/skool-pricing.md)
- Practitioner example: Jay Clouse's Creator Science reportedly made about $830,000 in 2024 with roughly 50% from its paid membership community; a separate source describes a 200-member community generating $324,000 ARR. Secondary reporting of his own disclosures. — [Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/jay-clouse-creator-science/); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/creator-science-breakdown)
- Framing: Kevin Kelly's "1,000 True Fans" posits 1,000 fans at about $100/year for about $100,000; Li Jin's 2020 "100 True Fans" argues 100 fans at $1,000/year is possible but requires a wholly different, higher-value product than patronage. — [Kevin Kelly, The Technium](https://kk.org/thetechnium/?p=2678); [Li Jin, a16z](https://a16z.com/2020/02/06/1000-true-fans-try-100/)

### Inferences
- Illustrative math: 5,000 subscribers at 1% conversion and $4.99 with a 70% share is about $175/month; 20,000 subscribers at 1% is about $700/month. Memberships become meaningful at roughly 10,000+ engaged subscribers unless priced high. A $27 to $50/month community with 50 members ($1,350 to $2,500/month) can out-earn YouTube ads on a small channel, which is the Li Jin point.
- Jay Clouse's membership economics (hundreds of members, four-figure annual price) illustrate the high-price/low-volume route; it depends on an audience of professionals with a business reason to pay, which a general gaming audience lacks but a game-developer audience may have.

### Gaps
- No platform-published conversion or churn data exists for YouTube memberships, Patreon or Discord paid roles; all percentages are third-party estimates.
- Super Thanks/Super Chat typical earnings per 1,000 views were not found.
- Discord server subscription terms and Substack/beehiiv fee details were not retrieved.

## 5. Owned products: digital products, merch, physical goods, software/games, services

### Takeaway
Surveys still show most creators depend on brand deals, but the evidence that exists (mostly from vendors of product tooling) says creators who sell their own products earn more and are less exposed to falling brand, affiliate and platform payouts; merch is the weakest owned product ($3 to $8 net per shirt), while courses, templates, software and services carry the margin.

### Cited Findings
- Kajabi 2025 State of Creator Commerce (1,717 respondents, Dec 2024 to Jan 2025): respondents reported brand-deal earnings down 52% year over year, affiliate down 36%, on-platform payouts down 33%; podcast and other owned-channel revenue up 47%; "entrepreneurial creators" reportedly earn about 25% more than social-first creators. Vendor survey of a platform's own customer base. — [Kajabi](https://kajabi.com/state-of-creator-commerce-25); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/04/18/creator-entrepreneur-revenue-stream-kajabi-state-of-creator-commerce/amp); [Drop Media](https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/?p=4345)
- Kit (ConvertKit) reports: 2022 edition found creators sold services and digital products more than subscriptions; 2023 edition found digital products were the income stream creators most planned to add. No 2025/2026 edition was surfaced. — [Kit, State of the Creator Economy 2022](https://convertkit.com/reports/creator-economy-2022); [Kit, 2024 report](https://convertkit.com/reports/creator-economy-2024)
- Print-on-demand merch: Printful says most sellers net $3 to $8 per shirt, with POD margins typically 20 to 40%; a worked example ($29.99 shirt, $9.95 base) gives 63% gross margin before marketing and fees. Vendor sources, likely optimistic. — [Printful: t-shirt profit](https://www.printful.com/blog/how-much-money-can-custom-t-shirts-make); [Printful: POD margins](https://printful.com/blog/what-is-a-good-profit-margin-for-print-on-demand)
- Justin Welsh (solo creator, courses/templates) reportedly reached $10M total revenue over 5 years 9 months at about 89% margin; figure quoted on a third-party page from his own retrospective, not verified at source. — [third-party breakdown page](https://no-ads-course-funnel.pages.dev/)
- Selling a game as the owned product: Thronefall (co-developed by game-dev YouTuber Jonas Tyroller, about 177k subscribers at the time) passed 1 million copies; earlier developer-shared data showed 315,000 Steam units and just over $1.5M gross. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-how-thronefall-went-minimal); [GamesMarkt](https://www.gamesmarket.global/indie-hit-thronefall-sells-over-one-million-copies-588b03986d04328e71544cbcb1d1b454/)

### Inferences
- An owned product converts the same views at far higher value than ads: at a $2 RPM, 100,000 views is $200 of ad revenue; 0.1% of those viewers buying a $20 product is $2,000 gross. This is arithmetic, not a sourced benchmark, but it is the mechanism behind the Kajabi/Kit findings.
- Merch needs volume (hundreds of orders) to matter and is best treated as a fan-identity product after an audience exists, not an early revenue stream.
- For a game-dev creator the highest-margin owned products are likely the game itself, paid assets/templates/tools, and courses; services (contracting, consulting) are the fastest to cash with no audience minimum.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) survey was found that breaks revenue mix down by earnings tier to prove owned products out-earn sponsors for small/mid creators; the claim is supported by vendor data and practitioner cases only.
- No data found on creator apps/software revenue, physical product lines or creator-led consumer brands at small scale.
- Course completion/refund rates and digital-product conversion benchmarks were not retrieved.

## 6. Email list / owned audience as an asset

### Takeaway
Practitioners treat the email list as the only audience the creator controls; Nathan Barry's rule of thumb is about $1 per subscriber per month, but only if you sell products to the list, and every benchmark found comes from people who sell email software or newsletters.

### Cited Findings
- Nathan Barry (Kit founder): creators should make about $1 per month per email subscriber, conditional on selling products; "Nobody can change the algorithm on your email list." Commercially interested source. — [BossBabe podcast transcript](https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/the-bossbabe-podcast/founder-of-kit-reveals-how-to-make-7-figures-off-a-small-email-list); [Membership.io interview](https://membership.io/blog/myb-nathan-barry)
- Kit claims creators on its platform earn about 3.5x what they spend on Kit from products sold through Kit. Company claim. — [Kit blog](https://kit.com/resources/blog/trends-in-creator-economy)
- Kajabi's 2022 report found email was the main nurturing tool for its customers, and argues list quality beats size (500 engaged subscribers out-earn 5,000 disengaged). Vendor view. — [Kajabi blog](https://www.kajabi.com/blog/email-marketing-for-creators)
- Justin Welsh built his audience by organic posting plus newsletter and started the newsletter because he already held buyer email addresses he was not using. Third-party summary. — [third-party breakdown page](https://no-ads-course-funnel.pages.dev/)
- Platform volatility that motivates list-building is documented in section 1: X ended its revenue program, TikTok payouts dropped after an ownership change, and YouTube raised entry thresholds, all in 2026. — [Tubefilter on X](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/11/x-monetization-original-content-rewards-program-creators/); [EURweb on TikTok](https://eurweb.com/tiktok-payout-drop/); [Tubefilter on YouTube](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)

### Inferences
- For a game developer, the functional equivalents of the email list are the Steam wishlist (section 9), a mailing list and a Discord; all three are reachable at launch without an algorithm.
- A list of 1,000 at the $1/subscriber/month heuristic is about $1,000/month, comparable to the ad revenue of roughly 500,000 monthly long-form views at a $2 RPM. This comparison is illustrative and rests on Barry's unverified heuristic.

### Gaps
- No independent revenue-per-subscriber benchmark, opt-in rate from video viewers to email, or email-to-purchase conversion benchmark was found.
- Jay Clouse's and Justin Welsh's own statements on email were not retrieved first-hand; the Welsh figures come from an unreliable third-party page.

## 7. UGC creation, content licensing, speaking, selling a channel

### Takeaway
UGC work pays about $150 to $500 per video with no audience required and is the most direct way to turn video skill into cash at zero followers; licensing viral clips and selling a channel exist but are opportunistic and poorly benchmarked.

### Cited Findings
- UGC rates (brand buys the deliverable, not your audience): Influee median about $175 per video; Fueler US average about $212 for 30 to 60 seconds; Collabstr average UGC collaboration $154; Influencer Marketing Hub's 2026 marketer survey found about 80% of reported UGC costs under $500 per video; beginner guidance $75 to $300. Paid-ad usage rights/whitelisting typically add about 30% of base fee per month. All marketplace/agency sources. — [Influee (marketplace)](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-price); [Fueler](https://www.fueler.io/blog/ugc-creator-rates-in-the-us-pricing-guide); [hoo.be summary of Collabstr/Billo/IMH data](https://hoo.be/blog/ugc-creator-rates-2026)
- Viral-clip licensing: Jukin Media (acquired by Trusted Media Brands, August 2021) either buys clips for a flat fee (reported $200 to $10,000+) or splits ad/licensing revenue (often 50/50; some creators got 70%); Jukin had paid over $20M to amateur video creators by December 2018; licensing can restrict where the clip may be embedded. — [Tubefilter, 2018](https://www.tubefilter.com/2018/12/20/jukin-media-20-million-creator-royalties-youtube/); [Wikipedia: Jukin Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jukin_Media); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/jukin-media-building-empire-peoples-viral-videos)
- Selling a channel: Flippa markets valuations of 12x to 36x monthly profit (buyer guide: most at 18x to 24x), but its own 2026 sold-deal data reports an average profit multiple of 1.26x (median 0.92x, top quartile 2.93x), apparently on an annual-profit basis; the bases are not reconciled. — [Flippa valuation guide](https://flippa.com/blog/how-much-is-my-youtube-channel-worth-a-complete-valuation-guide/); [Flippa 2026 multiples](https://flippa.com/blog/youtube-channel-multiples-what-channels-are-actually-selling-for-in-2026/)
- Events as revenue: Jay Clouse's first offline event grossed just under $17,000 in ticket sales (June 2025). — [Starter Story summary of Creator Science podcast](https://www.starterstory.com/creator-science-breakdown)

### Inferences
- If Flippa's sold-deal multiple is annual, realised prices are roughly 11x to 35x monthly profit at the top quartile and about 11x to 15x at the median, i.e. about one year of profit; personality-driven channels are hard to transfer and likely sell at the low end or not at all.
- UGC is labour income (trading time for money) rather than leverage on viewership, but it funds the early period and builds a paid-ad portfolio.

### Gaps
- No independent survey of UGC creator annual earnings was found; no data on stock-footage income, white-labelling, or speaking fees for small creators.
- Current (2025-2026) Jukin/TMB terms were not found; licensing figures are 2015 to 2018 vintage.

## 8. Clipping economy

### Takeaway
Clipping bounties run at roughly $1 per 1,000 views (median across 50 live Whop campaigns in September 2026), which makes paying clippers a cheap distribution channel for a creator with a product to sell, and a poor income source for the clipper.

### Cited Findings
- Whop Content Rewards: average about $1 per 1,000 views; a count of 50 live campaigns on September 28, 2026 found a $1.00 median; most campaigns pay $0.50 to $1.50; outliers from $0.20 to $6 (one music campaign paid $0.30). Pay is only on approved submissions and verified views, usually with a campaign budget cap. Sources are clipping platforms and tool vendors. — [Built by Foundry campaign count](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/what-is-clipping-1-dollar-per-1000-views-50-campaigns); [OpusClip on Whop Content Rewards (vendor)](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards); [Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards)
- Competing marketplaces advertise up to $3 per 1,000 views (Vyro, Clipping.net). Vendor claims. — [OpusClip comparison (vendor)](https://www.opus.pro/blog/vyro-vs-whop)
- Trade press describes clipping of viral social video as a large business in its own right. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/?p=7986214)

### Inferences
- As a buyer: $1,000 buys roughly 1 million clip views on third-party accounts. That only pays back if the views lead somewhere that earns (long-form channel, Steam page, product), since the clipper's account, not yours, holds the audience and any platform payout.
- As a seller: at $1 per 1,000, a clipper needs 1 million verified views to make $1,000, competing against many other clippers for a capped budget; it is not stronger or more reliable than the creator's own channel.
- Clipper accounts posting someone else's footage fall squarely under the reused/unoriginal-content rules in section 2.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) data on clipper earnings distribution, fraud/bot-view rates, or measured downstream conversion from paid clips to subscribers or sales.

## 9. Gaming and game-development niche: the game as the product

### Takeaway
For a game-dev creator the largest upside is selling the game itself, with the channel feeding Steam wishlists; documented cases (Thronefall, Zero Sievert) show a dev channel seeding early players, but Chris Zukowski warns devlogs are a near-full-time time sink that mostly reach other developers, and festivals plus streamer coverage usually drive the big wishlist spikes.

### Cited Findings
- Zukowski's dataset: comparing 34 titles released 2020-2022 with 126 released 2023-2025, sales per wishlist at six months fell from 0.82 to 0.43, attributed partly to Next Fest-driven low-intent wishlists; wishlists still predict launch performance (R² 0.77 vs 0.72 across eras; launch-day wishlists vs gross revenue R² 0.786). — [Inven Global summary](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27273/steam-wishlist-inflation-value-of-1-wishlist-cut-in-half-but-predictive-power-remains-unchanged); [HowToMarketAGame survey](https://howtomarketagame.com/salesSurvey/)
- Zukowski on YouTube devlogs: YouTube can drive real traffic only if treated as close to a full-time job on a fixed schedule; most developers lack the on-camera charisma; he calls it a time sink. He suggests about 7,000 wishlists before launch, with 0 to 40 wishlists a week underperforming and 200+ "awesome". — [HowToMarketAGame FAQ](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/03/14/frequently-asked-question-about-marketing-your-game/)
- Zukowski on streamers: giving the game to a good streamer can bring many wishlists, and about 20% of those may convert at the next discount (single-interview estimate). — [Unity blog interview](https://unity.com/blog/tips-strategies-for-marketing-indie-games)
- Thronefall: co-developer Paul Schnepf said the team "received a lot of the early players from the devlog Jonas had posted", but the big spike came from a Steam Next Fest demo with YouTuber pickup; 315,000 units / $1.5M gross early, later over 1 million copies. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-how-thronefall-went-minimal); [GamesMarkt](https://www.gamesmarket.global/indie-hit-thronefall-sells-over-one-million-copies-588b03986d04328e71544cbcb1d1b454/)
- Zero Sievert: earliest traffic sources were led by a YouTube devlog, ahead of the Kickstarter trailer and Reddit. — [HowToMarketAGame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/18/how-a-pixel-art-shooter-achieved-a-million-dollar-launch-the-zero-sievert-story/)
- Peglin: 76,000 wishlists at launch, 80,204 units in week one. — [HowToMarketAGame](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/05/04/how-peglin-made-1049413-in-1-week/)
- Counter-evidence: one developer reports that YouTube traffic was a minority of Steam page views even when coverage videos hit hundreds of thousands of views, though wishlists spiked whenever a YouTuber played the game; a Game Developer blog argues devlog readers are other devs and superfans who already wishlisted, and that posts with tens of thousands of views produced no sales. Anecdotal. — [Green Pixels devlog on itch.io](https://greenpixels.itch.io/gamblers-table/devlog/945763/wishlists-0-to-10000-in-just-three-weeks-); [Game Developer: "Indie marketing is a fool's errand"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/indie-marketing-is-a-fool-s-errand)
- itch.io is a weak sales channel next to Steam: one game sold about 113,000 copies on Steam versus 414 sales ($706.73) from 17,000 page views on itch.io. — [itch.io devlog](https://itch.io/devlog/1142956/itchio-sales-reality-check-and-why-it-doesnt-mean-your-game-is-bad.amp)
- Kickstarter: in 2024, 441 successful video-game campaigns raised $26M (up 28% and 9% respectively from 2023); all-time video-game average about $68.2K per campaign with roughly a 32% success rate (third-party tracker), versus about 80% for tabletop. — [Kickstarter](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-biggest-platform-for-games/); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before)
- Ad rates for gaming content are low (AIR median $2.05 RPM), and Amazon pays 1 to 2% on games; see sections 1 and 3. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm); [Amazon Associates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)

### Inferences
- Wishlist arithmetic using Zukowski's post-2023 ratio: 7,000 launch wishlists x 0.43 is about 3,000 copies in six months; at a $15 price that is about $45,000 gross before the store's cut, refunds and taxes. A modest wishlist count therefore out-earns years of small-channel ad revenue, which is the core argument for "channel as marketing for the game".
- The devlog audience skew toward other developers is a weakness for selling the game but a strength for selling developer-facing products (tutorials, courses, assets, tools, SaaS affiliates). A game-dev channel can monetise both sides: players via the game, developers via education and tools.
- Short clips of gameplay (TikTok/Shorts/Reels) plausibly reach players rather than developers; the DREADMOOR and Escape from Duckov cases in search results credit short-form and creator coverage for wishlist spikes, but none isolates the effect.

### Gaps
- No controlled or large-sample data isolates how many wishlists or sales a developer's own YouTube channel produces per 1,000 views.
- Cases such as Dani (Muck) and Choo-Choo Charles were not retrievable with figures; Choo-Choo Charles's breakdown lists YouTube among channels without numbers.
- Steam's revenue share, Humble/Nexus key-affiliate rates, publisher sponsorship rates for small game channels, and full-year 2025 Kickstarter video-game figures were not retrieved.

## 10. Income distribution reality check

### Takeaway
Roughly half of surveyed creators earn under $10,000 to $15,000 a year and only about 4 to 6% clear six figures; medians for self-identified full-time creators cluster around $45,000 to $50,000, and the trend from 2023 to 2025 is toward more creators at the bottom.

### Cited Findings
- Goldman Sachs (2023): about 50 million creators globally, a $250B market projected to reach $480B by 2027; about 4% are "professionals" earning over $100,000 a year. No newer Goldman update found. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027)
- NeoReach 2025 Creator Earnings Report (3,000+ creators): 50.71% earn under $15,000 a year (up from 48.10% in 2023); 56.55% of full-time creators earn below a $44,000 living-wage threshold (48% the prior year); 5.69% earn over $200,000 (7.2% two years earlier). Average annual income by platform: Instagram $81,700, YouTube $62,400, TikTok $44,250, Twitch $25,600. — [NeoReach](https://neoreach.com/creator-earnings-report); [Tubefilter, Jul 14 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/14/creator-economy-monetization-earnings-report-gap-neoreach/amp/); [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/over-half-of-full-time-creators-earn-below-us-living-wage-despite-250b-industry-neoreach-finds/)
- Influencer Marketing Factory (January 2026 survey): 48.7% of American creators earn under $10,000 a year and about 5.8% clear six figures; TheWrap's headline on a 2026 study says 67% earn under $10,000 (the two figures conflict or refer to different cuts). — [Marketing Dive press release](https://www.marketingdive.com/press-release/20260223-the-influencer-marketing-factory-releases-2026-creator-economy-report-highl/); [TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/creators-annual-salary-2026-study)
- CreatorIQ (300 creators): average reported income $44,293; 11% earn six figures; brand payment data shows a median of $3.0k per creator against an $11.4k average. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/industry-trends/creatoriq-creator-compensation-report)
- Linktree (9,500 users, older survey): only 12% of full-time creators make over $50,000 a year; 46% make under $1,000. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2302999)
- Median for full-time creators: about $50,000 (Kit 2025, via a secondary blog) and $48,000 (Epidemic Sound 2025, via the same secondary source); part-timers about $15,000. Not verified at source. — [SelfEmployed.com summary](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/creator-middle-class-income-2026/)

### Inferences
- "Audience first, sponsors later" is the path most creators are on, and it is the path producing these distributions. Surveys of full-time creators overstate typical outcomes because of survivorship and self-selection (network members, platform customers).
- NeoReach's platform averages are means dominated by top earners; they are not what a new creator should expect.

### Gaps
- No source was found reporting median earnings by audience size (for example 1k, 10k, 100k subscribers).
- The 2025/2026 Kit and Linktree reports were not surfaced; the Linktree figures are from an older (circa 2022) survey.

## 11. Revenue diversification and typical revenue mix

### Takeaway
Brand deals remain the primary income for roughly three quarters of surveyed creators, yet brand-deal income is flat or falling for many; practitioners who publish their numbers (for example Jay Clouse) show mixes led by memberships and products rather than ads or sponsors.

### Cited Findings
- Influencer Marketing Hub survey (2,000 creators): brand deals are the main income source for 77%, named three times more often than all other sources combined; sample skews small (43.8% under 100k followers). Later's analysis puts brand partnerships as primary income for 82%. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://www.influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report/); [Later](https://later.com/blog/2026-creator-income-diversified-monetization/)
- August 2026: more than 50% of creators said their brand-deal earnings rose only slightly or stayed flat from 2025 to 2026, and creators worry about sponsor/viewer tension. — [Tubefilter, Aug 16 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/16/creators-rely-on-brand-deals-but-worry-about-the-tension-between-sponsors-and-viewers-and-just-15-say-they-fully-trust-sponsored-content-from-other-creators/)
- CreatorIQ: total dollars paid to creators rose 59% in 2025 while the number of creators in campaigns grew 183%, implying thinner pay per creator. — [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/industry-trends/creatoriq-creator-compensation-report)
- A survey of 1,000 US creators found product/merch sales plus affiliate marketing make up 21.2% of creator income. — [Archive.com](https://archive.com/blog/creator-diversification-strategy-statistics)
- Jay Clouse / Creator Science: about $830,000 revenue in 2024, roughly half from the membership; around $70,000/month top line referenced in mid-2025; seven income streams described (membership, courses, sponsorship, affiliate, royalties, events). Secondary reporting. — [Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/jay-clouse-creator-science/); [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/creator-science-breakdown)
- Kajabi frames the shift as from "social-first" to "entrepreneurial" creators with owned products and channels. Vendor framing. — [Kajabi](https://kajabi.com/state-of-creator-commerce-25)

### Inferences
- A sequencing framework consistent with the evidence, by audience size (thresholds are from sections 1 to 4; the staging itself is an inference):
  - 0 followers: UGC work, services/freelance, building an email list and Steam wishlist, Twitch subs/Bits, SaaS and Amazon affiliates.
  - About 500 YouTube subscribers: memberships, Supers, YouTube Shopping affiliate.
  - About 1,000 to 5,000 followers: TikTok LIVE gifts, TikTok Shop affiliate, first digital product or paid community to a small list.
  - 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours (8,000 for new applicants from February 2027): YouTube long-form ads.
  - 10,000+ followers: TikTok Creator Rewards, Instagram Subscriptions, larger product launches.
  - 50,000 to 100,000+: Snapchat program, Facebook Creator Fast Track, meaningful merch, larger sponsors.
- Ad programs and sponsors are the streams with the highest thresholds and the least control; products, services, affiliates and fan funding unlock earlier and depend on audience fit rather than size.
- 183% more creators competing for 59% more brand dollars suggests sponsorship rates per creator are under pressure at the small end.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue-mix breakdown for typical mid-size (50k to 500k subscriber) YouTube channels was found; Colin and Samir and Publish Press material was not surfaced.
- Jay Clouse's first-hand 2025 monthly "retro" numbers were not opened.

## 12. US tax and business basics (brief)

### Takeaway
All creator income is taxable self-employment income whether or not a form arrives; 2026 reporting thresholds rose (1099-NEC/MISC to $2,000, 1099-K back to $20,000 and 200 transactions), which means fewer forms but the same tax, and an S-corp election generally only pays off somewhere above roughly $40,000 to $100,000 of net profit.

### Cited Findings
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) restored the Form 1099-K threshold to more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions; the IRS states reporting thresholds do not affect whether income is taxable. — [IRS newsroom](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000); [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/express/questions/whats-new-for-the-2026-tax-season)
- The same law raised the 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC threshold from $600 to $2,000 for payments made in calendar year 2026. — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html)
- Estimated tax: individuals including sole proprietors generally must pay quarterly if they expect to owe $1,000 or more; 2026 due dates are April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15, 2027. — [IRS estimated tax FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax/individuals); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/taxes/quarterly-taxes-estimated-payments)
- Self-employment tax is 15.3% (Social Security plus Medicare) and applies from $400 of net self-employment earnings (the $400 figure came from a secondary source, not an IRS page in this session). — [Gusto](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/taxes/s-corp-reduce-self-employment-taxes); [QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/taxes/estimated-taxes/)
- S-corp election limits self-employment tax to a "reasonable salary"; break-even estimates range from $40,000 to $60,000 net profit to about $80,000 to $100,000, against roughly $1,500 to $2,500 a year of added payroll and filing overhead; it does not reduce income tax. Vendor/advisor blogs. — [SelfEmployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/s-corps-and-self-employment-tax-comprehensive-guide/); [Karat](https://www.trykarat.com/blogs/s-corp-benefits-creators-influencers); [Collective](https://www.collective.com/blog/s-corp/how-to-reduce-self-employment-tax-s-corp/)

### Inferences
- A new creator can operate as a sole proprietor, track all income and expenses from the first dollar, set aside a share of net income for tax, and revisit an LLC (liability separation) or S-corp election (tax) once profit is consistently in the tens of thousands. Platform payouts (AdSense, TikTok, Patreon, Steam) may no longer generate forms at small amounts in 2026, so self-kept records matter more.
