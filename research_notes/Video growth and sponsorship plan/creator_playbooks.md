## 1. MrBeast: the leaked production memo (2024) and public interviews

### Takeaway
The memo reduces "what makes a video viral" to three metrics (CTR, AVD, AVP) plus an untrackable "wow factor", and assigns a job to every stretch of the runtime: first minute, minutes 1-3, a re-engagement around minute 3, minutes 3-6, a second re-engagement around minute 6, and a back half that must never signal the ending. Title and thumbnail come first because they set the expectation the first minute has to match. In March 2024 MrBeast publicly reversed his own fast-cut style, which is the single most important "dated advice" flag in this file.

### Cited Findings

Provenance and status of the document
- The document is a roughly 36-page internal onboarding memo titled "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production"; it leaked in September 2024. Two anonymous former MrBeast producers confirmed its authenticity to Passionfruit, per Net Influencer's coverage. Other coverage cautions that circulating copies may be incomplete or edited. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/mrbeast-productions-leaked-internal-document-rosanna-pansino/); [Simon Willison's notes, 15 Sep 2024](https://simonwillison.net/2024/Sep/15/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/) (not fetchable this session); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- The verbatim quotes below were read from a third-party text copy of the memo (a personal blog repository that interleaves a few of the blogger's own comments; I quoted only memo text that also matches other summaries). — [Memo text copy on GitHub](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)

Goal and metrics
- Stated goal: "Your goal here is to make the best YOUTUBE videos possible." The memo explicitly says this is not the best-produced, funniest, best-looking or highest-quality video. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- "The three metrics you guys need to care about is Click Thru Rate (CTR), Average View Duration (AVD), and Average View Percentage (AVP)." — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- CTR is defined with a worked example: 100,000,000 thumbnail impressions and 10,000,000 clicks is a 10% CTR, and "This is what dictates what we do for videos." — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)

Idea, title and thumbnail first
- Idea-level leverage: "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" is "lame and you wouldn't click it", while "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" takes similar time and effort but is "easily 100x more viral". — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Title wording: swapping "I Spent" for "I Survived" adds intrigue; "In general the more extreme the better" ("I Don't Like Bananas" vs "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth"). — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Why production staff must know the packaging: the title and thumbnail "set the expectations for the viewer". The memo's bouncy-castle example: thumbnail shows a giant yellow castle next to huge buildings, the video shows a red one in a field, so the viewer feels lied to and clicks off. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- A thumbnail sketch and the written creative are named as the things needed before production work on a video can start. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)

The minute-by-minute structure
- First minute: "the first minute has the most loss (go look)" and "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video." The listed failure modes are a badly lit opening, not matching the expectations of the clickbait, not pre-planning what is said, and not front-loading interesting material. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- First-minute loss, as a number: summaries of the memo's retention example say that of 60 million viewers who clicked, 21 million left in the first minute, and the memo treats that as a comparatively good result. (Secondary; the figure sits beside a retention chart in the memo, which the text copy I read renders as an image.) — [Structured summary of memo ch. 1](https://github.com/virgiliojr94/book-to-skill-library/blob/HEAD/books/mrbeast-production/chapters/ch01-what-makes-a-video-viral.md); [OMR (German coverage)](https://omr.com/de/daily/mrbeast-doc-pdf-leak)
- Minutes 1-3: "transition from hype to execution (generally). Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." The named tactic is "crazy progression". — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- "Crazy progression" explained (secondary): rather than spending the first three minutes on day one of a multi-day survival story, cover several days quickly so the viewer is already invested. — [Daniel Scrivner's summary](https://www.danielscrivner.com/how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production-summary/)
- Re-engagement around minute 3: "content that is highly interested that fits the story and makes people genuinely impressed", also described as a segment that "only MrBeast can do", placed there "because they could get bored of the story and click off." — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Minutes 3-6 are "the next most important part": the most exciting content that is also very simple, "lots of quick scene changes and highly stimulating simple content that reflects the story", with the aim of making viewers "fall in love with the story, the people in the video". The memo adds that viewers who watch the first half are very likely to finish. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Second re-engagement: "Usually at the 6 minute mark we will include another re-engagement" that needs a little more explanation and pushes the story into the back half. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Back half: after six minutes viewers are in a "lull", "watching the video without even realizing they are watching a video." Weaker content and long explanations can live here, but the memo also warns that many videos were killed by a bad back half. — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Endings: "Don't ever signal the end of the video unless it's to build hype for the prize or payoff at the end of the video." — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)

Wow factor and retention formats
- "Wow factor" is the non-trackable element: a video can tick the CTR and AVD boxes and still underperform. The example is "100 days in the circle": the contestant's house is brought in by crane 30 seconds in, which added nothing to the title or thumbnail and is "arguably from a data standpoint illogical", but "Anytime we do something that no other creator can do, that seperates us in their mind". — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- Retention formats named in the creative chapter (secondary summaries): payoff-at-the-end formats such as "last to leave", and "stair stepping", meaning each step is progressively cooler than the last (the firework video escalating from $1 up to a world-record firework). — [Gist summary of the memo](https://gist.github.com/cognitivetech/55870132c63d8f456f8b550d0d6127a3); [Structured summary, creative chapter](https://github.com/virgiliojr94/book-to-skill-library/blob/HEAD/books/mrbeast-production/chapters/ch03-creative.md)
- Other memo practices relevant to a solo creator (secondary summary): "no dull moments"; run content past as many people as possible; watch the channel's last ~50 videos to get up to speed; integrate brand deals into the content because the retention chart dips less than with a conventional ad read; "creativity saves money" (a Doritos prize was cheaper and funnier than a $20,000 cash prize); protect "critical components" and schedule a backup day. — [Gist summary of the memo](https://gist.github.com/cognitivetech/55870132c63d8f456f8b550d0d6127a3)

Public statements
- Lex Fridman Podcast #351 (January 2023): advice to beginners was to make 100 videos, improve something each time, and not expect views on those first 100; he also argued it is easier to get 10 million views on one excellent video than 100,000 views on each of 100 mediocre ones, because great videos keep being recommended. (Third-party episode notes.) — [Podcast Notes](https://podcastnotes.org/lex-fridman-podcast/mrbeast-future-of-youtube-twitter-tiktok-and-instagram-lex-fridman-podcast-351/)
- Same interview, packaging patterns as summarised: clear, uncluttered thumbnails and titles under about 50 characters. (Secondary; the 50-character figure was not checked against the transcript.) — [Podcast Notes](https://podcastnotes.org/lex-fridman-podcast/mrbeast-future-of-youtube-twitter-tiktok-and-instagram-lex-fridman-podcast-351/)
- Reversal on pacing, 3 March 2024 (X post): "This past year i've slowed down our videos, focused on story telling, let scenes breathe, yelled less, more personality, longer videos, etc" and "our views have skyrocketed!" He told creators to drop the "ultra fast paced/overstim era of content" and added "It doesn't even work." — [Tubefilter, 4 Mar 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/24/03/37448542/mrbeast-reveals-secret-sauce-to-skyrocketing-youtube-views-slower-longer-videos-could-be-the-future)
- Tubefilter's count: a March 2023 MrBeast video had 38 cuts in its first 60 seconds; "I Survived 7 Days In An Abandoned City" had 23, with longer, wider shots and normal speaking volume. — [Tubefilter, 4 Mar 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/)
- A former editor (named Yates in coverage) said the earlier hyper-fast style came from analytics, with number-crunchers concluding that contestants' personal stories did not matter. — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/youtube-editing-mrbeast-54202/)
- Diary of a CEO (episode covered by Fortune, March 2025): the coverage found was about personal strain ("The average person does not want to live the life I live"), not craft. — [Fortune, 6 Mar 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/03/06/mrbeast-youtube-empire-miserable-ceo-stress)

### Inferences
- The memo's structure is a spacing rule, not a fixed timestamp rule: front-load, switch from telling to showing early, place a genuinely impressive beat where boredom is expected (about a quarter and a half of the way into a 12-15 minute video), and hide the ending. For a shorter video the same beats would scale proportionally; the memo itself does not say this.
- The memo (written before the leak, describing the older house style: "quick scene changes and highly stimulating simple content") and the March 2024 post ("let scenes breathe") are in tension. The consistent part across both is story investment and no dull moments; the part that changed is cut rate and volume.
- "Wow factor" for a small channel translates to "one thing in the video that viewers cannot get from a similar channel", since the memo defines it relative to competitors, not to budget.

### Gaps
- I could not open Simon Willison's notes, Time, or Business Insider coverage (hosts blocked), so their specific framing is not represented.
- No verified craft detail from the Colin and Samir interviews or Diary of a CEO episode with MrBeast was retrieved; anything attributed to those should be re-researched.
- The memo's retention chart (the 60M/21M example) was only seen through summaries.
- Coverage mentions MrBeast's later experiments with closed-mouth thumbnails (2024), but I found no results data.

## 2. YouTube strategists: Galloway, Eves, Thomas, Lawrence, Blackman, Alter, Colin and Samir, Jay Clouse

### Takeaway
Strategists converge on an order of operations: idea first, then title and thumbnail, then an opening that confirms the click, then segment-level tension. They put most of a video's upside in the idea and packaging (Galloway: clients make 10+ and sometimes 50+ thumbnails per video) and recommend modelling proven outliers rather than inventing from scratch.

### Cited Findings

Paddy Galloway (strategist; clients reported to include MrBeast, Jesser, Ryan Trahan, Noah Kagan)
- Time allocation: "Don't spend 5% of your time on something that's 50% of the game." He says his clients often make 10+ thumbnails per video, sometimes 50+. (X post, "5 Principles That Helped Me Generate 50 Billion Views"; date inferred from the post ID as roughly early November 2026.) — [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/2117334892375896413)
- "The algorithm = the audience" is lesson 1 of his "5 key lessons" thread on 10 billion+ views (X, about October 2023). — [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1711401542576660774)
- Ideas set the ceiling; "The vast majority of your growth (90%+) will come from one idea, be an ideation machine", and successful channels turn that outlier into a repeatable format. (X thread, 1 January 2025.) — [Thread Reader copy](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1874513765762531734.html)
- Leverage claim: "A 20% better title could mean double, triple, quadruple" the views. (Creator Science interview.) — [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway-2/)
- Three sources of ideas: internal ideas, external ideas, and packaging (starting from a title/thumbnail concept). — [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway-2/)
- Idea funnel "0-100-10-1": generate 100+ ideas, filter to 10, develop 1. (Secondary podcast summary; appears in one source only.) — [Startup Ideas Podcast episode](https://share.transistor.fm/s/d34a7e0e)
- "CCN fit" is an idea filter he says eliminates a large share of candidate ideas. (Transcript extract; the expansion of the acronym was not confirmed in this session, see Gaps.) — [Creator Science podcast #152](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway/)
- Thumbnail "3 element rule" (X, October 2021): "the best thumbnails on YouTube have 3 or less major elements (e.g text, face, subject)". — [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1450463472907657220)
- "Glance test" (X, about July 2024): "can you process everything in just a quick glance? Viewer gets just milliseconds to view it as they are scrolling." Same post: judge title and thumbnail as one unit; do they complement each other or contradict/repeat? — [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951)
- Related rules reported from his interviews: at most 3 "focus areas", under five words of thumbnail text, and at least three thumbnails per video tested against each other at launch. (Secondary.) — [Only Happy News summary](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/)
- Format transfer: adapt proven formats from unrelated niches. (Colin and Samir, "The New Rules of YouTube", 2025.) — [Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway); [edited transcript](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)
- Outlier analysis as described by third parties: ignore a channel's average videos and study those that beat its own baseline by 5x-15x. This is a tool vendor's description, not his wording. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/paddy-galloway-growth-strategy/)
- Scale he cites (2025 interview): about 10 billion client views a year, approaching 50 billion over his career. — [Edited transcript, Colin and Samir 2025](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited)

Derral Eves ("The YouTube Formula", 2021; "The YouTube Formula 2.0", September 2026)
- Core position: audience before algorithm ("Find your audience and speak to them. Create for them. When something isn't working, look at your data and make smart adjustments."). (Secondary summary.) — [Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/summary/the-youtube-formula-summary-derral-eves)
- The working loop is Try > Fail > Analyze > Adjust. (Secondary summary.) — [Summaries.com](https://summaries.com/blog/the-youtube-formula)
- Workflow order: from concept to capturing thumbnail images before recording the video. (Single secondary summary.) — [Shortform PDF summary](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/the-youtube-formula-pdf-derral-eves)
- Tubefilter (28 September 2026) reports that the original book's core advice is still actionable and that Eves released an updated "2.0" edition. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/28/derral-eves-youtube-formula-book/)

Jake Thomas (Creator Hooks newsletter)
- Three "click worthy emotions": curiosity, fear, desire; the best titles are "usually curiosity plus fear or curiosity plus desire." — [Creator Science #125](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas/)
- Building curiosity: know the audience's assumptions and preconceived notions, then challenge them. — [Creator Science #125](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas/)
- His dataset: across nearly 1,000 analysed titles, 60% used curiosity, 46% desire, 39% fear. — [Creator Science #193](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas-2/)
- Title and thumbnail should carry different emotions (desire in the title, fear or curiosity in the thumbnail); repeating the title in the thumbnail is "a missed opportunity". — [Creator Science #193](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas-2/)
- Method: "model what has proven to work, either in your niche or adjacent niches." — [Net Influencer interview](https://www.netinfluencer.com/creator-hooks-jake-thomas-code-on-clickable-youtube-titles/)

Ed Lawrence (Film Booth)
- Roughly 70% of his process is planning: settle thumbnail and title until he believes people will click, then make the content, then write the intro and hook. — [Passionfroot interview](https://www.passionfroot.me/creators-on-air/mastering-youtube-strategies-with-ed-lawrence)
- Titles: "you either steal the title or you tweak it slightly", then look for thumbnail patterns that worked in and around the niche. — [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/ed-lawrence/)
- Retention (X thread, July 2022): keep viewers wondering what happens next; giving away the most valuable content at the start leaves little reason to stay. — [Ed Lawrence on X](https://twitter.com/Ed_FilmBooth/status/1549104575990349824)
- Intro: script an intro that "undoes the clickbait", i.e. connects the title and thumbnail to the opening. (Secondary summary.) — [Create Grow Profit](https://creategrowprofit.com/how-to-find-amazing-youtube-video-ideas-ed-lawrence-filmbooth/)

George Blackman (YouTube scriptwriter)
- Setup-tension-payoff is a per-segment loop, not a whole-video arc, and he uses it to diagnose retention graphs; a workshop recap puts a loop every 1-2 minutes. (Secondary.) — [Vidpros profile](https://vidpros.com/insiders/george-blackman-youtube-scriptwriting/); [workshop recap](https://recapio.com/digest/guest-workshop-with-george-blackman-by-creator-crew-workshops)
- Writing order from the workshop recap: all payoffs first, then setups, then the hook, then the tension. (Secondary.) — [workshop recap](https://recapio.com/digest/guest-workshop-with-george-blackman-by-creator-crew-workshops)
- Hook formula: educational videos use Target, Transformation, Stakes; entertainment videos use Character, Concept, Stakes. One recap suggests three sentences to raise the stakes. (Secondary summaries of his method.) — [1of10 interview write-up](https://1of10.com/blog/10-000-hours-of-youtube-scriptwriting-in-1hrs-9mins-george-blackman/); [Write With AI teardown](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/write-a-killer-youtube-script-like)
- Common failure: paying off instantly. A teardown notes a segment with "not a single part" of tension because the payoff came immediately. — [Write With AI teardown](https://writewithai.substack.com/i/142862792/setup-tension-payoff)
- From his own retention reviews: in one reviewed video the first 3:30 builds toward a payoff and retention stays very consistent while the tension builds. — [Retention Review #2](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-2-payoff-outro-core)
- Pre-script step (X, about February 2025): find videos with packaging similar to yours, pick one that performed badly and one that performed well, and look for clues in the difference before writing the hook. — [George Blackman on X](https://x.com/GeorgeBlackman_/status/1892212177781600301)
- Signposting: remind the viewer where they are in the structure at each new segment. (Secondary.) — [1of10 interview write-up](https://1of10.com/blog/10-000-hours-of-youtube-scriptwriting-in-1hrs-9mins-george-blackman/)

Aprilynne Alter
- VidCon 2025: "In the first five seconds, if there's too much of a difference between what your title and thumbnail promise versus what the first five seconds of your video are, people are going to get confused." A video must immediately meet two promises, a "plot promise" and a "format promise". — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/aprilynne-alter-reveals-how-to-make-a-killer-youtube-intro/)
- Same talk: a highly polished thumbnail on a video whose production value does not match undermines trust. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/aprilynne-alter-reveals-how-to-make-a-killer-youtube-intro/)
- Her MrBeast study (X, August 2023): the opening sentence matches the title and the first shot matches the thumbnail; she also identifies "input bias" (showing the effort invested) in the first 20 seconds. — [Aprilynne Alter on X](https://x.com/AprilynneAlter/status/1688598816801656832)
- Earlier notes (X, March 2022): plan title and thumbnail before filming; keep thumbnail text to five words or fewer; do not repeat the title in the thumbnail; a "HIVES" structure (Hook, Intro, Value, End screen, Sales pitch). — [Aprilynne Alter on X](https://x.com/AprilynneAlter/status/1498725968218710018)
- She reads CTR in the first three hours after release and runs A/B/C tests on three thumbnail concepts. (X, July 2024.) — [Aprilynne Alter on X](https://twitter.com/AprilynneAlter/status/1814427203922473310)
- Thumbnail hierarchy (one main character, the rest supporting) is a topic of her Creator Science episode. — [Creator Science #196](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/aprilynne-alter/)

### Inferences
- A reusable pre-production gate falls out of these sources: (1) list many ideas and filter hard (Galloway); (2) write title options using curiosity plus fear or desire, modelled on proven outliers (Thomas, Lawrence); (3) design at least three thumbnails that pass the glance test with three or fewer elements and under five words (Galloway); (4) only then script, opening with a line and shot that restate the packaging (Alter, Lawrence, MrBeast memo).
- "Do not repeat the title in the thumbnail" (Thomas, Galloway, Alter) and "the first shot should match the thumbnail" (Alter on MrBeast) are compatible: packaging elements should complement each other, while the opening should confirm both.
- Blackman's loops and the MrBeast memo's re-engagements are the same mechanism at different scales: scheduled reasons to keep watching.

### Gaps
- Colin and Samir's own production rules and Jay Clouse's own YouTube lessons were not retrieved (search budget exhausted). They appear here only as interviewers.
- "Patrick Rizzo" and other thumbnail designers: no results obtained; I cannot confirm who he is or what he teaches.
- The "1 idea, 100 titles" practice could not be attributed to a named person. The closest documented items are Galloway's "0-100-10-1" idea funnel (one secondary source) and his "10+, sometimes 50+ thumbnails" figure.
- CCN is, to my recollection, "Core, Casual, New" (an idea should appeal to all three viewer groups), but this was not confirmed by any source retrieved here.
- Eves's specific AVD/AVP guidance and the contents of the 2026 "2.0" edition were not retrieved.

## 3. Long-form creators on craft

### Takeaway
Top long-form creators describe craft as story and feeling rather than tricks: a clear goal with stakes that is restated often (Trahan's team), a click-worthy wrapper around substance (Rober), emotion shown rather than narrated (Hillier-Smith), and three-act structure even in vlogs (Neistat). Veritasium's contribution is the argument that honest, intriguing packaging is necessary and should be tested.

### Cited Findings

Ryan Trahan and editor Zach Levet (penny series, 2022 onward)
- Levet on restating the mission: without the goal of delivering a penny to MrBeast, the series "would just be a daily vlog of Ryan making money." — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/game-changer)
- The recurring "game plan" segment emerged on day four and became part of every episode; Levet said it gave context for what Ryan was about to do and the value of each piece. — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/game-changer)
- Built-in jeopardy: a $50,000 donation forced a restart at one penny ("The Great Reset"). — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/penny)
- Production cadence on the series: edit all day in a van, review the finished video as a team, post; episodes went out while the challenge was still running. Trahan told Colin and Samir "It's such a fun motivation because it's alive." — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/game-changer)

Mark Rober
- "Hide the vegetables": a clickbait-style wrapper such as a 15-ton Jell-O pool draws people in, and they end up learning chemistry and the scientific method. — [TED / ReThinking transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/how-mark-rober-hides-science-vegetables-in-viral-videos-transcript)
- Advice to beginners in the same interview: make 50 videos and do not expect anyone to see any of them. — [TED / ReThinking transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/how-mark-rober-hides-science-vegetables-in-viral-videos-transcript)
- Stanford talk, May 2025: "Focus on the feeling" and "You just have to be authentic. Full stop." — [The Stanford Daily, 19 May 2025](https://stanforddaily.com/2025/05/19/youtuber-and-former-nasa-engineer-mark-rober-draws-full-crowd-speaks-to-scientific-storytelling/)

Veritasium / Derek Muller
- His 2021 video (uploaded as "We Need To Talk About Clickbait", later retitled "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective") argues that title and thumbnail largely decide a video's success, and separates honest intrigue ("legitbait") from deception ("clicktraps"). — [Veritasium](https://www.veritasium.com/videos/2021/8/17/we-need-to-talk-about-clickbait); [Gigazine summary, 30 Aug 2021](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)
- He describes swapping titles and thumbnails after publishing and keeping the better performer. (Secondary summary.) — [Gigazine summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective)
- In 2019 ("My Video Went Viral. Here's Why") he argued the recommendation system, more than subscriber preference, drives views, and that click-through rate was the key metric at that time. — [Veritasium](https://www.veritasium.com/videos/2019/5/23/my-video-went-viral-heres-why)
- Sources disagree on who coined "legitbait": Gigazine credits Veritasium; a Habr write-up credits another YouTuber. — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective); [Habr](https://habr.com/post/574422)

Hayden Hillier-Smith (editor; formerly Logan Paul's editor)
- "You have to understand the difference between storyshowing and storytelling. If you can make me feel the story rather than tell me the story, that's compelling." He asks how little voiceover or explicit dialogue an edit can use. — [Musicbed interview](https://www.musicbed.com/articles/filmmaking/masterminding-viral-edits-with-hayden-hillier-smith/)
- Pacing tool: cutting to b-roll lets the viewer's attention rest while the audio thread continues. — [Musicbed interview](https://www.musicbed.com/articles/filmmaking/masterminding-viral-edits-with-hayden-hillier-smith/)
- Curiosity tool: show an image slightly too briefly; "You don't quite have enough time to see the image … and then that adds a sense of curiosity." — [Musicbed interview](https://www.musicbed.com/articles/filmmaking/masterminding-viral-edits-with-hayden-hillier-smith/)
- Music: short recurring cues (leitmotifs) to set tone quickly. — [Musicbed interview](https://www.musicbed.com/articles/filmmaking/masterminding-viral-edits-with-hayden-hillier-smith/)
- Positioning: the Creator Science episode description says he "isn't focused on spectacle, he's not even focused on retention". — [Creator Science #129](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/hayden-hillier-smith/)
- Jon Youshaei's Forbes profile (10 March 2023) gives three lessons from his career and describes how the daily-vlog time crunch produced a style with "very little breathing room" before he shifted toward asking how to make the work great. (Only the introduction was visible.) — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jonyoushaei/2023/03/10/how-hayden-hillier-smith-redefined-what-it-means-to-be-an-editor-on-youtube/)

Casey Neistat
- Applies a three-act structure even to spontaneous vlogs, shooting with the structure in mind; treats how a story is told as more important than the subject. (2016 analysis, dated.) — [No Film School, Aug 2016](https://nofilmschool.com/2016/08/7-storytelling-techniques-you-can-learn-filmmaker-youtube-star-casey-neistat)
- Deliberate imperfection (hand-drawn titles, cutouts) to keep work human. (Course notes, secondary.) — [Course notes](https://notes.nicolevanderhoeven.com/sources/Course/Filmmaking+&+Storytelling+with+Casey+Neistat/Filmmaking+&+Storytelling+with+Casey+Neistat)

Dhar Mann

Johnny Harris, Marques Brownlee
- Johnny Harris is cited as holding that only certain stories suit video and others work better as text. (Thin, secondary.) — [Medium profile](https://medium.com/@invinciberry/a-story-about-a-storyteller-8386fa8f982e)

## 4. Short-form specialists

### Takeaway
Jenny Hoyos is the best-documented source: decide the last line before filming, hook in about a second, foreshadow in two lines (three seconds or less), and replace "and" with "but/so" so every line creates or resolves conflict. Hormozi's hook-retain-reward and Vaynerchuk's pillar-to-micro repurposing model describe volume systems rather than single-video craft.

### Cited Findings

Jenny Hoyos (Shorts)
- Ending first: she writes the hook, then the last line, so she always knows the final beat before filming. — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- Structure: "I do a hook, And then I foreshadow, which is 2 lines, and that is usually 3 seconds or less." — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- Hook window: "I really do think you have one second to hook someone, especially on Shorts". (Reported in a secondary write-up of her interviews; other write-ups summarise her rule as hooks under three seconds, so sources differ on one versus three seconds.) — [Mode Digital Media summary](https://modedigitalmedia.com/videos/what-jenny-hoyos-s-shorts-playbook-says-about-hooks-rewatchability-and-platform-fit); [Recall summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/entrepreneurship/the-formula-to-break-100-million-views-on-shorts-ft-jenny-hoyos)
- Pace at the line level: she replaced "let's get started" with "so I cooked illegally" because the first version broke pace. — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- But/so: replace "and" with "but" to add conflict, e.g. "I was walking but then it started raining, so I had to find an umbrella." (Secondary summary of her talk.) — [Recall summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/entrepreneurship/the-formula-to-break-100-million-views-on-shorts-ft-jenny-hoyos)
- Length and ending: a summary of her interview with Colin and Samir says the last second of a 34-second video is crucial because that is where the payoff lands; in the Creator Science episode she describes a retention graph that dropped sharply in the final second. — [Leaderonomics summary](https://www.leaderonomics.com/videos/box-of-chocolates/how-to-make-great-youtube-shorts-lessons-from-jenny-hoyos); [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- Her own numbers as transcribed: "my average scroll through rate is 85%, but my average retention is 95%." (The phrase "scroll through rate" is as transcribed; she likely means the share of viewers who chose to view rather than swipe away.) — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- Her caveat: retention is over-weighted; some Shorts with excellent retention stall while others with weaker numbers keep climbing. — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- Tubefilter, September 2023: a weak hook is her first suspect when a Short fails; she tries to raise several questions in the hook although the Short answers one; she asks whether a viewer would rewatch; production takes about 20 hours per Short, 10 at minimum. — [Tubefilter, 27 Sep 2023](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/09/27/creators-on-the-rise-jenny-hoyos/)
- The Creator Science episode title states her average as 10 million views per Short. — [Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)

Alex Hormozi
- Content unit from "$100M Leads" (2023): Hook (stop the scroll), Retain (keep the body engaging), Reward (pay off the hook's promise). (Book summary.) — [Business Book Club summary](https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-100m-leads-by-alex)
- Attributed stance: there is no such thing as too long, only too boring. (Third-party analysis.) — [OutlierKit analysis](https://outlierkit.com/resources/alex-hormozi-growth-strategy/)
- Volume figures conflict and are all third-party: about 35,000 pieces a year (sourced to his LinkedIn posts and a former media director), 250+ pieces a week (a course page), a YouTube catalogue of about 5,300 videos with 91% of views from Shorts. — [Contentporary](https://contentporary.io/blog/alex-hormozi-content-strategy); [OutlierKit analysis](https://outlierkit.com/resources/alex-hormozi-growth-strategy/)
- Clip selection as described by an analysis of his operation: cut clips from the moments where the long-form retention graph spikes, because those are pre-validated hooks. (Third-party.) — [CaptureFlow playbook](https://captureflow.ai/playbooks/alex-hormozi)

Gary Vaynerchuk
- The "GaryVee Content Model": one long-form "pillar" piece (keynote, podcast, vlog) is cut into "micro" pieces; his team's write-up says one session became 30+ pieces distributed across 6-10 platforms. (The model deck dates from about 2019.) — [garyvaynerchuk.com](https://garyvaynerchuk.com/content-marketing-strategy/); [Biteable explainer](https://biteable.com/blog/gary-vees-content-framework-one-video-30-content-pieces/)
- "Document, don't create": document the journey instead of manufacturing content; a mindset against perfectionism rather than a production method. (Secondary.) — [Repurposito explainer](https://www.repurposito.com/blog/gary-vaynerchuk-document-dont-create-framework)

Zach King
- Third-party description only: a simple situation followed by an impossible twist within seconds, with clips short enough that viewers rewatch to work out the trick. — [JoinBrands guide](https://joinbrands.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices/)

Platform statements relevant to Shorts
- YouTube's Shorts lead (Creator Insider, 2024, reported by Benzinga): posting many Shorts a day does not by itself affect performance; the algorithm does not favour creators by upload frequency. — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532)

### Inferences
- Hoyos's method is directly reusable as a script template: last line, hook (about one second), two foreshadow lines (three seconds or less), body written as but/so beats, hard stop on the payoff.
- Her 20-hours-per-Short figure contradicts the idea that short-form is a volume game by nature; Hormozi and Vaynerchuk represent the opposite, volume-through-repurposing school. A playbook should present these as two distinct strategies: few, engineered originals versus many clips cut from long-form.
- Hormozi-style clipping from retention spikes only works if long-form already exists; it is a distribution step, not an alternative to making the long video.

### Gaps
- No reliable source retrieved for clipping operations (clipper networks, pay-per-view clipping programmes) or for Zach King's and Dhar Mann's own first-hand advice beyond the YouTube Blog piece.
- The "30-34 second target" attributed to Hoyos is only partly confirmed here (a 34-second example); I did not retrieve her reasoning for a target length or any fifth-grade-reading-level rule.
- No Jon Youshaei interview with Hoyos was found; the documented long interviews are with Jay Clouse (Creator Science) and Colin and Samir.
- YouTube changed how Shorts views are counted in 2025 (my recollection: views began counting on play or replay, with "engaged views" retaining the old definition). Not verified this session; verify before comparing Shorts numbers across years.

## 6. Common concrete frameworks and how practitioners read retention

### Takeaway
The frameworks reduce to a short checklist: packaging that passes a one-glance test, an opening that confirms the click within about five seconds, scheduled re-engagements or setup-tension-payoff loops, an ending that is never announced, and post-publish testing judged on watch time rather than CTR alone.

### Cited Findings

Hook formulas
- Opening line matches the title, first shot matches the thumbnail; show effort early (Alter's reading of MrBeast, 2023). — [Aprilynne Alter on X](https://x.com/AprilynneAlter/status/1688598816801656832)
- Confirm the click in the first five seconds with a plot promise and a format promise (Alter, VidCon 2025). — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/aprilynne-alter-reveals-how-to-make-a-killer-youtube-intro/)
- Target + Transformation + Stakes (educational) or Character + Concept + Stakes (entertainment) (Blackman). — [1of10 write-up](https://1of10.com/blog/10-000-hours-of-youtube-scriptwriting-in-1hrs-9mins-george-blackman/)
- Hook, then two-line foreshadow in three seconds or less (Hoyos, Shorts). — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)
- Front-load, pre-plan the words, light it well, match the clickbait (MrBeast memo). — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)

Title and thumbnail rules
- Three or fewer major elements; glance test; under five words; title and thumbnail complement rather than repeat (Galloway 2021, 2024). — [X 2021](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1450463472907657220); [X 2024](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951)
- Curiosity plus fear or desire; split emotions across title and thumbnail (Thomas). — [Creator Science #193](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas-2/)
- More extreme wording raises CTR, bounded by what the video actually delivers (MrBeast memo). — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- YouTube's own guidance: a title must accurately represent the video or viewers stop watching, which hurts discovery; track CTR separately for subscribers and new viewers. — [YouTube Help: thumbnail and title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)

Re-hooks and loops
- Re-engagement near minute 3 and minute 6 of a long video (MrBeast memo). — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- A setup-tension-payoff loop every 1-2 minutes (Blackman, per workshop recap). — [workshop recap](https://recapio.com/digest/guest-workshop-with-george-blackman-by-creator-crew-workshops)
- Stair stepping: each step cooler than the last, payoff at the end (MrBeast memo, secondary). — [Structured summary](https://github.com/virgiliojr94/book-to-skill-library/blob/HEAD/books/mrbeast-production/chapters/ch03-creative.md)
- Restate the goal and preview the plan each episode (Trahan's editor). — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/game-changer)
- Unattributed short-form rule of thumb found in vendor guides: one deliberate "interrupt" every 10-15 seconds in clips over 30 seconds. No named practitioner source; treat as lore. — [Shortzly guide](https://shortzly.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-strategies)

Endings
- Never signal the end except to build hype for the payoff (MrBeast memo). — [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- On Shorts the payoff sits in the final second, and the graph can fall sharply right there (Hoyos). — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)

Post-publish testing and reading the data
- YouTube's "Test & Compare" lets a creator test up to three titles and/or thumbnails; the winner is chosen by watch time share, not CTR. YouTube's stated reason is that CTR-only tools may pick a different winner and that watch time better supports growth. If there is no clear winner, the first uploaded option is kept. Results are labelled Winner, Performed Same, or Inconclusive. — [YouTube Help: A/B test titles and thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)
- Title testing rolled out globally in 2025; tests run for up to two weeks. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-title-a-b-testing-rolls-out-globally-to-creators/562571/)
- Practitioner testing habits: at least three thumbnails per video at launch (Galloway, secondary); A/B/C testing of three concepts with CTR read in the first three hours (Alter, 2024); a thumbnail swap worth 300k to 1.1M views (Abdaal, 2023). — [Only Happy News](https://onlyhappynews.com/happy-studio/mr-beasts-secret-youtube-consultant-has-worked-on-50-billion-views-here-are-his-actual-rules/); [Alter on X](https://twitter.com/AprilynneAlter/status/1814427203922473310); [Abdaal on X](https://x.com/AliAbdaal/status/1727976482968854624)
- Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison, 2024 Q&A) on what counts as a good CTR: better than your last video. — [Tubefilter, 26 Aug 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- Todd Beaupré (YouTube, Creator Insider, 1 September 2026, per third-party write-ups): if a video does badly with existing subscribers, look at the topic and title before blaming distribution; YouTube learns from subscribers, non-subscribers and new viewers separately. — [Creator Insider video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY); [write-up](https://thekingofsearch.com/signals/youtube-reframes-recommendation-mechanics)
- Blackman reads retention graphs segment by segment against the script's setup, tension and payoff points, and publishes these as "Retention Reviews". — [Retention Review #2](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-2-payoff-outro-core); [Retention Review #4](https://georgeblackman.substack.com/p/retention-review-4-second-payoff)
- Diagnostic pairing found in vendor material (unattributed): high CTR with low retention means fix the first 30 seconds and the promise mismatch; strong retention with low CTR means rework title and thumbnail. — [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/tips-creating-youtube-gaming-channel/)

### Inferences
- A defensible post-publish routine from the sourced items: compare CTR and early retention to your own previous video (Ritchie), split results by subscribers versus new viewers (YouTube Help, Beaupré), map each retention dip to the script segment that caused it (Blackman), and run a three-variant Test & Compare whose winner is judged on watch time (YouTube).
- Because YouTube's own test picks by watch time share, a thumbnail that raises clicks but attracts the wrong viewers can lose. This supports the practitioners' "match the promise" rule with platform mechanics.

### Gaps
- YouTube's official explanation of retention-graph shapes (intro percentage, spikes, dips, "typical" comparison) was not retrieved; search budget ran out.
- No named practitioner source was found for a specific "re-hook every N seconds" number in long-form, or for "remove dead air" as a quantified rule. The MrBeast memo's "no dull moments" is the closest attributable statement.
- No attributable source for "1 idea, 100 titles".

## 7. Publishing cadence, consistency, and time to traction

### Takeaway
Practitioners who give a number say 50 to 100 videos before expecting results (Rober 50, MrBeast 100), with each video improving on the last. YouTube staff say upload frequency is not itself rewarded, while large correlational datasets show higher-frequency channels growing faster, with the authors warning against reading that as cause.

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast (Lex Fridman #351, January 2023): make 100 videos, improve something each time, then reassess; one great video beats 100 mediocre ones. — [Podcast Notes](https://podcastnotes.org/lex-fridman-podcast/mrbeast-future-of-youtube-twitter-tiktok-and-instagram-lex-fridman-podcast-351/)
- Mark Rober: do 50 videos and do not expect anyone to see any of them. — [TED / ReThinking transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/how-mark-rober-hides-science-vegetables-in-viral-videos-transcript)
- Galloway, as summarised in a podcast write-up: expect 12 months minimum before real returns, and 50+ videos to find your groove. (Secondary; approximate.) — [Startup Ideas Podcast episode](https://share.transistor.fm/s/d34a7e0e)
- Galloway: 90%+ of growth comes from one idea, which argues for many attempts at ideas rather than many uploads of the same idea. — [Thread Reader copy, 1 Jan 2025](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1874513765762531734.html)
- Hoyos: about 20 hours per Short (minimum 10), an explicit quality-over-quantity position in short-form. — [Tubefilter, 27 Sep 2023](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/09/27/creators-on-the-rise-jenny-hoyos/)
- YouTube (Todd Beaupré, September 2026, via write-ups): the algorithm does not punish infrequent posting or trying new things; YouTube does not decide a video's fate in the first hour, and many videos get most of their views a month or more after upload. — [write-up](https://thekingofsearch.com/signals/youtube-reframes-recommendation-mechanics); [Creator Insider video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY)
- vidIQ study of 10,210,278 channels with 1,000+ subscribers (April 2025 to March 2026, long-form and Shorts combined), as reported by Vlix: every upload-frequency tier grew faster than the one below; moving from 1-3 to 4-7 videos a month lifted median monthly view growth by about 50%. The authors state the data "does not prove that weekly uploads cause growth". — [Vlix](https://www.vlix.me/digital-marketing/quality-vs-quantity-on-youtube)
- OverseerOS vendor analyses: among 189 channels with a 5x breakout, the median was 35 uploads per 90 days, but breakouts occurred at every frequency tier; an analysis of 2,565 upload gaps across 59 channels found no significant effect of gap length. — [OverseerOS breakout study](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-youtube); [OverseerOS gap study](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/does-posting-more-on-youtube-help)
- AIR Media-Tech's 18,000-channel study (vendor): more uploads add subscribers until about 40+ a month, where quality slips and results drop. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/does-posting-more-on-youtube-help-we-studied-18000-channels-to-find-out)
- YouTube director statement reported by PPC Land: subscribers skip about 90% of uploads in their feed. (Headline claim; article body not read.) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

### Inferences
- The 50-100 video numbers are framed by their authors as practice volume for skill, not as an algorithmic threshold; YouTube's statements are consistent with that reading.
- A reasonable synthesis for a small channel: choose the highest cadence at which each video still gets a deliberate idea filter, three packaging options and one named improvement over the last video. The correlational studies support moving from 1-3 to about weekly; nothing sourced here supports daily long-form.

### Gaps
- No rigorous data on "number of videos before traction" was found; all figures are personal rules of thumb or vendor correlations.

## 8. Widely repeated tips that practitioners or YouTube now call wrong or outdated (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
The best-sourced corrections are: hyper-fast "overstimulating" editing (retracted by MrBeast himself in 2024), optimising thumbnails for CTR alone (YouTube's test picks by watch time), tags and posting frequency as ranking levers (dismissed by YouTube staff), and the idea that the first hour or first day decides a video's fate (dismissed by YouTube in September 2026).

### Cited Findings
- "Cut faster, be louder" is outdated: MrBeast, 3 March 2024, told creators to drop "ultra fast paced/overstim" content and said "It doesn't even work", crediting slower pacing, storytelling and longer videos for higher views. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/)
- "Maximise CTR" is incomplete: YouTube's Test & Compare chooses winners by watch time share because CTR-only optimisation "may determine a different 'winner'". — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)
- "Tags drive discovery" is wrong: Rene Ritchie said tags have minimal impact; titles, thumbnails and descriptions matter more. (2024.) — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-insights-from-creator-liaison-renee-ritchie/493901/)
- "The algorithm rewards frequent or scheduled uploads" is wrong as a mechanism: YouTube's growth team says viewer behaviour matters, not the schedule; the Shorts lead said posting volume does not by itself affect performance (2024); Beaupré repeated in 2026 that infrequent posting is not punished. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/); [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532); [write-up of Sept 2026 video](https://thekingofsearch.com/signals/youtube-reframes-recommendation-mechanics)
- "The first hour/24 hours decides everything" is wrong: per write-ups of the 1 September 2026 Creator Insider video, YouTube does not decide a video's fate in the first hour and many videos earn most views a month or more later. — [write-up](https://thekingofsearch.com/signals/youtube-reframes-recommendation-mechanics)
- "Experimenting gets your channel punished" and "shadowbans": Beaupré said creators are not punished for trying new things; Ritchie said channels he checked after shadowban claims still received most views from recommendations, and that AI labels alone do not affect recommendations. (Third-party write-ups of the same 2026 video.) — [write-up](https://thekingofsearch.com/signals/youtube-reframes-recommendation-mechanics); [AIR Media-Tech 2026 tracker](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-algorithm-in-2026-month-by-month-changes-that-affect-your-views)
- "Watch time is the goal" is incomplete: Beaupré says YouTube uses surveys because it does not assume all watch time is valuable, and that signal importance varies by context (for example TV versus mobile); YouTube has published no single weighting. — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/youtube-still-cares-about-watch-time-more-about-returning-viewers-their-satisfaction-91300); [write-up](https://thekingofsearch.com/signals/youtube-reframes-recommendation-mechanics)
- "Put the title text in the thumbnail": Thomas calls repeating the title a wasted opportunity; Galloway and Alter say the same. — [Creator Science #193](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas-2/); [Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951)
- "You have three seconds to hook": Hoyos argues it is closer to one second on Shorts. — [Mode Digital Media summary](https://modedigitalmedia.com/videos/what-jenny-hoyos-s-shorts-playbook-says-about-hooks-rewatchability-and-platform-fit)
- "Retention percentage is everything": Hoyos says retention is over-weighted; Hillier-Smith positions himself as not focused on retention; the MrBeast memo itself says a video can hit CTR and AVD and still underperform without a "wow factor". — [Creator Science transcript](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/); [Creator Science #129](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/hayden-hillier-smith/); [Memo text copy](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)
- "Give the value up front": Lawrence warns that handing over the most valuable content at the start removes the reason to stay (2022), whereas Blackman's storytelling notes describe hooking with the most explosive points and then joining the dots. This is a live disagreement, not a settled correction. — [Ed Lawrence on X](https://twitter.com/Ed_FilmBooth/status/1549104575990349824); [George Blackman newsletter](https://www.georgeblackman.com/write-on-time/rr-18---this-traditional-storytelling-technique-is-perfect-for-youtube)

### Inferences
- The corrections share a direction: away from proxy metrics and tricks (cut rate, tags, schedules, CTR alone) and toward whether the viewer got what the packaging promised and was glad they watched.
- Advice dated before 2024 that prescribes very fast pacing should be treated as superseded; the MrBeast memo's structural advice (minute roles, re-engagement, hidden ending) is not contradicted by anything later, only its implied cut rate is.
