## 1. What thresholds do brands and sponsorship marketplaces use, and at what size do sponsorships become realistic?

### Takeaway
Official platform marketplaces set low or no hard audience bars (YouTube: Partner Program membership; TikTok One: 10,000 followers; Instagram: roughly 1,000 followers in practice), and third-party marketplaces either start around 1,000 followers or publish no minimum at all. No source found supports the idea of a single industry "threshold" based on summed cross-platform views; eligibility is per platform, and real selection is by fit and per-video performance.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube BrandConnect: Google's help page lists the minimum eligibility as being 18 or older and being in the YouTube Partner Program with eligibility for ad revenue sharing; the snippet retrieved states no separate subscriber number. — [YouTube Help: Get started with YouTube BrandConnect](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9385307)
- Older third-party guides cite a 25,000-subscriber minimum for BrandConnect (it launched in 2020 as a US-only programme for channels of 25,000+); a more recent guide says there is no separate bar beyond the Partner Program, which starts at 500 subscribers. These conflict; the 25K figure appears outdated. — [Hire Influence BrandConnect guide](https://hireinfluence.com/blog/youtube-brandconnect-guide/) (via search summary); [DEV/Spansa: Who's actually eligible for YouTube brand deals in 2026](https://dev.to/tryspansa/whos-actually-eligible-for-youtube-brand-deals-in-2026-7ka) (via search summary)
- BrandConnect is being rebranded/replaced by "Creator Partnerships" in a number of markets; eligibility beyond Partner Program membership was not explicitly detailed in the announcement. — [PPC Land: YouTube Creator Partnerships replaces BrandConnect in 7 markets](https://ppc.land/youtube-creator-partnerships-replaces-brandconnect-in-7-markets/); [PPC Land: expands to four more countries](https://ppc.land/youtube-expands-creator-partnerships-to-four-more-countries/)
- YouTube opened its Shopping affiliate program to creators with 500 subscribers (date not captured in the summary; 2025-2026 coverage). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-opens-shopping-affiliate-program-to-creators-with-500-subscribers/)
- TikTok One (successor to TikTok Creator Marketplace): TikTok's support page lists a minimum of 10,000 followers, age 18+, and at least 1,000 post views in the last 30 days, and says the requirement may differ by region. — [TikTok Support: TikTok One](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/tiktok-one/tiktok-one)
- TikTok's ads help page (last updated July 2026 per the search summary) lists three sign-up conditions: TikTok One has launched in your country, you are at least 18, and you meet the Community Guidelines and Branded Content Policy. — [TikTok Ads Help: How creators can sign up for TikTok One](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-creators-can-sign-up-for-tiktok-one)
- Some third-party guides add "100,000+ video likes in 28 days" and "3 posts in the last 30 days" for TikTok; the likes figure could not be confirmed against TikTok's own pages. — [StackInfluence](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/tiktok-marketplace-requirements) (via search summary, unverified)
- Instagram Creator Marketplace: requires a professional (creator or business) account, age 18+, and residence in a supported country. One 2026 guide says Instagram lists no official follower minimum and the functional minimum is about 1,000; another, citing the Instagram Help Centre, gives a 1,000-follower minimum on a public professional account. — [FluxNote: Instagram creator marketplace eligibility](https://fluxnote.io/guides/instagram-creator-marketplace-eligibility); [Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/instagram-creator-marketplace-requirements) (via search summary)
- Brands on the Instagram marketplace must have an Instagram account linked to a Facebook Page that is eligible for partnership ads and branded content on Instagram and Facebook. — [Meta developer docs: Creator Marketplace API](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/creator-marketplace)
- Third-party marketplaces with low stated bars: Collabstr 1,000+ followers/subscribers; Aspire a 1,000-subscriber minimum to join and browse campaigns. — [vidIQ: 16 Companies for YouTube Sponsorships & Brand Deals (2026)](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/companies-matching-youtubers-with-brand-deals/) (via search summary)
- Agentio publishes no minimum; a tool-review site says thresholds are not public, the platform targets established creators, and audience thresholds are a drawback for smaller creators. — [Creator Economy Tools: Agentio](https://creatoreconomytools.com/tool/agentio)
- ThoughtLeaders publishes no creator minimum; its "minimum views" concept is a brand-side contractual view guarantee. — [ThoughtLeaders glossary: view guarantee](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/sponsorship-glossary/view-guarantee)
- Passionfroot publishes no minimum audience; it matches creators with B2B brands by audience, niche and platform, and took a 15% fee on deals through its network as of October 2024. — [Passionfroot creators page](https://passionfroot.me/creators); [TechCrunch, 2024-10-21](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/21/passionfroot-is-building-a-platform-for-brand-and-creator-colloboration-with-a-focus-on-b2b-sector)
- Tier definitions are not standardized: impact.com puts nano under 15K and micro at 15K-75K; OpenSponsorship puts nano under 10K and micro at 10K-50K. — [inBeat: How much should an influencer post cost in 2026?](https://inbeat.agency/blog/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report)
- TikTok rate guides commonly use: nano 1K-10K followers, then micro, mid-tier, macro and mega bands. — [Influencer Marketing Hub: TikTok Influencer Rates in 2026](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-rates/tiktok-influencer-rates/)
- Influencer Marketing Hub's 2026 benchmark survey: 51.43% of respondents plan to expand nano-creator use (secondary citation; not confirmed on the primary report page); the report itself describes a continued shift toward nano/micro and UGC-style production. — [inBeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report); [IMH Benchmark Report](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/)
- Creator-education guides commonly recommend about 1,000 subscribers as the minimum before sponsor outreach, with paid offers easier at 10,000+. — [Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/sponsorships-for-youtubers/) (via search summary); [FluxNote: brand deals for small YouTube channels](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel)

### Inferences
- The realistic entry point for paid sponsorship is roughly 1,000-10,000 followers on one platform in a defined niche, not a large aggregate number. Every threshold found is measured per platform (subscribers, followers, recent post views); none counts summed cross-platform views.
- The official marketplaces are passive listing channels: being eligible does not mean being selected. Brand search filters in these tools work on audience, niche and performance, so a threshold-triggered bot does not match how selection occurs.
- Platforms that publish no minimum (Agentio, ThoughtLeaders, Passionfroot) are curated; they likely screen on long-form average views and niche rather than headline size.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm current official wording for BrandConnect/Creator Partnerships or Instagram Creator Marketplace from the primary help pages (fetch blocked).
- No data found for Facebook-specific partnership-ads creator thresholds, Grapevine, Upfluence or Creator.co minimums.
- No independent statistic found for the share of deals by tier or the median channel size at first paid deal.

## 2. Which metrics matter most to sponsors, and why are summed cross-platform short-form views a weak selling point?

### Takeaway
Sponsors price on expected views of the specific sponsored video (projected about 30 days out), audience geography/age/gender, niche fit and sponsor track record, not on lifetime or summed view totals. Short-form views are discounted heavily: vendor benchmarks put a sponsored Short at roughly 30-60% of the same creator's long-form integration, because sponsor airtime is seconds long and views expire within days.

### Cited Findings
- ThoughtLeaders' platform gives brands projected 30-day views for upcoming videos and each channel's sponsorship history, including whether brands return to it (vendor source). — [ThoughtLeaders: key YouTube influencer metrics for brand sponsorships](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/what-are-the-key-youtube-influencer-metrics-for-brand-sponsorships) (via search summary)
- Brands are advised to request audience demographics (age, geography, gender split) during outreach evaluation. — [ThoughtLeaders: find the right YouTube creator for your brand](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/find-right-youtube-creator-brand) (via search summary)
- ThoughtLeaders' "sponsorship score" rises for channels with repeat sponsors; channels with 5-10 "regulars" score higher, and brands use it in rate negotiation. — [ThoughtLeaders: sponsorship score](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/why-brands-should-look-at-a-youtubers-sponsorship-score)
- The standard pricing formula is recent average views x niche CPM / 1,000, using the average of recent uploads rather than subscriber count. — [OutlierKit: YouTube Sponsorship Rates 2026](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)
- A sponsorship "resume" should be built on average views per video over the last 60 days, not subscriber totals. — [Toptal Creator: how to get brand deals on YouTube](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-get-brand-deals-on-youtube) (via search summary)
- A channel with 3,000 engaged subscribers can often secure better deals than one with 50,000 passive viewers (creator-education source, not measured data). — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel) (via search summary)
- Shorts are priced at about 30-50% of the same creator's long-form rate per one vendor and 40-60% per another; example: a $5,000 60-second long-form integration versus $2,000-$3,000 for a dedicated Short. — [InfluencerFee: YouTube Shorts sponsorship pricing](https://influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-pricing/); [FluxNote: YouTube Shorts sponsorship rates](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-rates)
- Reasons given: a 60-second Short offers about 5-15 seconds of usable sponsor time versus 60-120+ seconds in a 10-minute video, and a Short's view lifespan is about 24-72 hours versus months for long-form (vendor estimates). — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-pricing/) (via search summary)
- A rate calculator applies a 0.3-0.5x multiplier for Shorts, 2.5-3x for dedicated videos, and a 25-50% premium for a US audience. — [YTMoneyCalculator: YouTube sponsorship rates](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/) (via search summary)
- The common structure is a Short bundled onto a long-form deal: add-on of 20-30% of the long-form fee, or bundle priced at 130-160% of long-form alone. — [FluxNote: YouTube Shorts brand deals 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-brand-deals-2026) (via search summary)
- On TikTok, 80-90% of views come from the For You Page, which makes follower-based pricing unreliable (vendor source). — [TTCalculator: brand deal rates by followers](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/brand-deal-rates-by-followers/)
- eMarketer has published analysis headlined "Long-form YouTube is becoming a serious TV-equivalent media buy," and ThoughtLeaders' CEO David Tintner is on record that long-form still wins for sponsorships (headlines only; article bodies not read). — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/long-form-youtube-becoming-serious-tv-equivalent-media-buy); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/thoughtleaders-bet-on-youtube-sponsorships-and-why-ceo-david-tintner-thinks-long-form-content-still-wins/)

### Inferences
- "X million combined views across platforms" answers a question sponsors do not ask. The buyer's question is "how many of the right people will see my integration in this video, for how long, and will they act?" A summed figure mixes 3-second algorithmic swipes with 10-minute watch sessions, hides geography and age, and says nothing about a single video's expected reach.
- A total dominated by short-form implies low per-view value (seconds of airtime, 1-3 day shelf life, feed-driven non-follower audiences), so the same headline number can be worth several times less than a long-form-led total.
- A stronger pitch metric set is: median views per long-form video at 30 days over the last 10 uploads, top-country share, age band, niche, and any click/conversion or repeat-sponsor evidence.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) dataset was found that quantifies how brands value a Shorts/TikTok view against a long-form view; the 30-60% ratios are vendor rules of thumb.
- No sourced benchmark for the US-audience percentage or engagement rate that brands treat as a cut-off.
- The Digiday and eMarketer articles were not readable in this session, so their figures are not included.

## 3. What are current pricing benchmarks and deal structures?

### Takeaway
YouTube integrations are priced by CPM on expected views, with published 2026 ranges spanning roughly $3-$15 for gaming up to $20-$80 for finance, B2B SaaS and tech, and sources disagree by 2x or more. Marketplace transaction data shows that typical small-creator deals are modest: Collabstr's 2026 report puts average paid collaborations at $255 (YouTube), $193 (Instagram) and $186 (TikTok).

### Cited Findings
- 2026 integration CPM by niche, guide A: finance $20-$55, business/SaaS $20-$45, gaming $6-$15 (vendor source). — [DEV/Spansa: finance sponsorship rates 2026](https://dev.to/tryspansa/youtube-finance-sponsorship-rates-2026-the-20-55-cpm-tier-57ch); [DEV/Spansa: gaming sponsorship rates 2026](https://dev.to/tryspansa/youtube-gaming-sponsorship-rates-2026-the-6-15-cpm-niche-13nb)
- Guide B: finance $40-$80, tech $20-$40, health $15-$30, beauty $8-$20, gaming $3-$12 (vendor calculator). — [YTMoneyCalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)
- Guide C: B2B SaaS and developer tools $40-$80; overall 2026 rates cluster around $15-$80 CPM; Reddit-sourced anecdotes put general lifestyle at $15-$25 and tech/finance at $30-$60. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)
- Guide D: technology $30-$60, health and wellness $25-$45. — [SponsorRadar: YouTube sponsorship rates](https://sponsorradar.com/insights/youtube-sponsorship-rates-what-brands-should-pay)
- Format multipliers: dedicated video 2.5-3x an integration; brief 15-30 second mention 0.5-0.8x a 60-second integration; Shorts 0.3-0.5x. — [YTMoneyCalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/) (via search summary)
- Small-channel flat fees: nano (1K-10K subscribers) $100-$500 per integration and $250-$1,000 per dedicated video in one guide; $50-$250 per video for 1,000-5,000 subscribers in another; a third suggests a realistic small-channel CPM of $20-$50. — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel); [Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/sponsorships-for-youtubers/) (via search summary)
- Collabstr 2026 report (published January 2026; 21,000+ collaborations, 472,000+ listed packages): average asking price $311 on YouTube, $214 on Instagram, $182 on TikTok; average actually paid $255, $193 and $186. — [Collabstr 2026 Influencer Marketing Report](https://collabstr.com/2026-influencer-marketing-report); [Collabstr: asking prices vs what brands pay](https://collabstr.com/blog/influencer-costs-2025); [Hello Partner, 2026-01-13](https://hellopartner.com/2026/01/13/industry-report-reveals-the-7-influencer-marketing-trends-defining-2026)
- Collabstr's prior-year (2025) data showed average spend of about $418 on YouTube, $212 on Instagram, $200 on TikTok and $178 for UGC; not directly comparable with 2026. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-much-does-influencer-marketing-cost)
- TikTok per-post flat fees, 2026: nano (1K-10K) $50-$200; micro $200-$1,000; mid-tier $1,000-$5,000; macro $5,000-$15,000; mega $15,000-$50,000+. — [TTCalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/brand-deal-rates-by-followers/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-rates/tiktok-influencer-rates/)
- Per-follower rule of thumb on TikTok: about $10-$20 per 1,000 followers before adjustments (calculator heuristic, not market data). — [TTCalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/tiktok-sponsorship-rates-followers/)
- Whitelisting/paid usage: about 25% of the base creation fee per 30-day boosting window (IMH); 15-25% per 30 days in another guide; base fee plus 20-30% per additional month for longer runs. — [Influencer Marketing Hub: organic vs paid usage rights](https://influencermarketinghub.com/organic-vs-paid-usage-rights/); [Lumanu](https://www.lumanu.com/blog/how-influencers-charge-whitelisting-usage-rights) (via search summary)
- Exclusivity: +25% to +100% on the creator fee depending on category tightness and duration; usage rights, whitelisting and exclusivity together typically add 25-100% or more. — [Influencity](https://influencity.com/blog/en/negotiating-influencer-content-rights) (via search summary)
- Creator-side advice is to itemize content fee, exclusivity premium and usage-rights fee as three separate lines; perpetual usage should cost substantially more. — [Toptal Creator: usage rights for creators](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/usage-rights-for-creators)
- Brands can ask for contractual view guarantees (a minimum view count for the sponsored video). — [ThoughtLeaders glossary](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/sponsorship-glossary/view-guarantee)
- Affiliate programs are open regardless of subscriber count and are the usual first step for small channels, alongside product/gifting deals. — [Podia](https://www.podia.com/articles/youtube-sponsorships-and-brand-deals) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- Worked example from the formula: a channel averaging 20,000 views per long-form video in a $25 CPM niche would quote about $500 per integration (20,000 x 25 / 1,000); the same channel in gaming at $8 CPM would quote about $160. Niche moves the price more than size at small scale.
- Collabstr's paid averages ($186-$255) show that most marketplace deals are small; a bot sending thousands of emails to chase deals of this size has poor economics even before legal and deliverability costs.
- For short-form-led creators, flat fees, affiliate commission and hybrid (small flat fee plus commission) are the realistic structures; CPM-priced integrations depend on long-form.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on typical payment terms (net 30/60/90) or the share of deals that are hybrid/performance-based; searches returned nothing citable.
- No Instagram Reels tier table was captured beyond Collabstr's 2025 note that Reels asking prices ($288) ran about 32% above TikTok videos ($217) — [Collabstr](https://collabstr.com/blog/instagram-influencer-marketing) (via search summary).
- Business Insider rate-card coverage, Goldman Sachs, CreatorIQ and Linqia reports were not reached.
- All niche CPM tables are vendor-published with undisclosed samples; treat as ranges, not benchmarks.

## 4. What does effective sponsor outreach look like, and what reply rates are realistic?

### Takeaway
Practitioners teach brand-specific pitches to a short list of well-matched companies, especially brands already sponsoring similar creators, and the available data shows small personalized campaigns outperform large generic ones by about 2-3x on reply rate. Mass templated pitches are described as ignored on sight.

### Cited Findings
- Justin Moore (Creator Wizard; sponsorship coach, ex-agency, full-time creator since 2014, $5M+ in brand partnerships generated) teaches the ROPE pitch: Relevant (show you researched the brand's current campaigns), Organic (link content you already made about the brand), Proof (past collaborations/results), Easy to execute (promise only what you can deliver). — [Creator Science podcast: Justin Moore sponsorship strategy](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/justin-moore-sponsorship-strategy/); [Smart Passive Income, session 631](https://www.smartpassiveincome.com/session631)
- Moore's position per Entrepreneur: landing deals "isn't about follower count or flashy media kits" but understanding what the brand needs; every campaign has a different goal (awareness, direct sales, or repurposable content) and the pitch should identify it. — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/sponsorship-expert-shares-advice-for-creators/489127)
- Moore's core fix is to make the pitch "them-focused instead of you-focused." — [Side Hustle Nation: Your Path to $1k+ Brand Deals, Even With a Small Audience](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/brand-deals/) (via search summary)
- Target selection: look at comparable shows/channels in your niche with similar audience size and note which brands keep recurring; repeat sponsors are the strongest prospects. — [Circle: podcast sponsorship guide (Justin Moore)](https://circle.so/de/blog/podcast-sponsorship-guide) (via search summary)
- Tools exist to see who sponsors whom: ThoughtLeaders exposes sponsorship histories and repeat sponsors; SponsorRadar publishes sponsorship rate and sponsor data. — [ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/why-brands-should-look-at-a-youtubers-sponsorship-score); [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/insights/youtube-sponsorship-rates-what-brands-should-pay)
- If you already use a product on camera, pitch that brand first. — [vidIQ: How to Get Brand Sponsorships on YouTube in 2026](https://vidiq.com/tr/blog/post/brand-deal-youtube) (via search summary)
- One 2026 guide calls cold outreach the most reliable first-deal channel for channels under 100K subscribers. — [DEV/Spansa: How to get your first YouTube sponsorship in 2026](https://dev.to/tryspansa/how-to-get-your-first-youtube-sponsorship-in-2026-2f4b) (via search summary; vendor)
- Media kit: 2-3 pages with analytics; have it ready but do not lead with it. — [Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-to-get-sponsored-on-youtube-80469/) (via search summary)
- Brand-side voice: Vin Matano (Creatorbuzz founder) said generic compliments are among his biggest frustrations with pitches and advised roughly three sentences that state exactly what you want. — [Influencer Marketing Hub: Pitching Brands via Email — What Actually Works in 2026](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pitching-brands-via-email/) (via search summary)
- "Brands can usually tell immediately when creators mass-send the same template to dozens of companies"; mass-produced pitches "are often ignored before they're even opened." — [Influencer Marketing Hub: PR email for influencers](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pr-email-for-influencers); [IMH: Pitching Brands via Email](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pitching-brands-via-email/)
- Follow-up norms: one polite nudge after 5-7 business days; stop after three unanswered messages. — [Influencer Marketing Hub: PR email for influencers](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pr-email-for-influencers) (via search summary)
- General cold-email baselines (all vendor datasets, B2B sales, not creator pitches): Instantly's 2026 benchmark averages about 3.4% reply across 100M+ emails; a February 2026 Sales.co analysis of 2M+ emails found 2.09%; Apollo calls 3-6% healthy. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-greeting-benchmarks-2026-industry-standards-for-reply-rates-open-rates/); [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-a-good-benchmark-for-reply-rates-in-cold-outreach); [Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics/) (via search summary)
- Personalization effect: Instantly reports 3% without versus 7% with personalization; Woodpecker reports about 9% versus 18% (upper-bound vendor figure). — [Woodpecker: cold email statistics](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Campaign size effect: campaigns under 50 recipients average 5.8% reply versus 2.1% for 500+ recipients (Belkins analysis of 16.5M emails, cited by Woodpecker); 3-5 follow-up steps yield 8.3% versus 4.1% with none. — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Lemlist markets creator-to-brand pitch templates as having achieved a 67% reply rate (headline vendor claim; sample not seen). — [Lemlist](https://lemlist.com/blog/brand-pitch-email-template)

### Inferences
- The numbers point one way: fewer recipients and more personalization raise replies (5.8% vs 2.1%; 7% vs 3%). A "blast to as many companies as possible" design selects the worst-performing cell on both axes.
- A pitch that satisfies ROPE cannot be produced by a threshold-triggered bulk mailer, because three of the four elements (brand research, organic prior content, a deliverable sized to that brand) are specific to each recipient.
- A realistic lead list for a small creator is tens of brands (those sponsoring 5-10 comparable channels plus products already used on camera), not thousands.

### Gaps
- No measured reply or close rate specific to creator-to-brand cold pitches was found; the cold-email figures are B2B sales benchmarks from software vendors, and the Lemlist 67% is an unverified headline.
- Reed Duchscher, Colin and Samir, and Business Insider media-kit coverage were not reached.
- Could not verify YouTube "includes paid promotion" search techniques or Social Blade workflows from a source in this session.

## 5. What legal constraints apply to bulk automated email from a US sender to companies in the US and abroad?

### Takeaway
In the US, unsolicited commercial email is legal on an opt-out basis but every message must meet CAN-SPAM, with exposure of up to $53,088 per non-compliant email and aggravated liability for automated address harvesting. Outside the US the rules flip toward consent: Canada's CASL requires consent with penalties up to C$10 million, and Germany generally requires prior consent even for B2B, so one global blast is unlawful in several major markets.

### Cited Findings
- CAN-SPAM: each separate email in violation is subject to penalties of up to $53,088; the Act is not limited to bulk email and covers commercial messages generally. — [FTC: CAN-SPAM Act — A Compliance Guide for Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- The $53,088 figure is attributed to an FTC inflation adjustment dated January 17, 2025 (per a vendor blog; date not confirmed on FTC's page and a later adjustment may exist). — [Adaptive Security](https://www.adaptivesecurity.com/blog/can-spam-act-requirements) (via search summary)
- Enforcement example: the largest known CAN-SPAM civil penalty is $2.95 million against Verkada, August 2024 (per a compliance vendor). — [Adaptive Security: email compliance penalties](https://www.adaptivesecurity.com/blog/email-compliance-penalties)
- CAN-SPAM section 7704(b) lists four aggravated practices: address harvesting, dictionary attacks, automated creation of multiple email accounts, and relaying through unauthorized access. — [Cornell LII: CAN-SPAM problematic spamming techniques](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/inbox/can-spam_act_problematic_spamming_techniques); [15 U.S.C. 7704](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7704)
- These are aggravators attached to an underlying violation, not stand-alone bans; harvesting is defined around automated collection from a website or online service that has a notice/policy against sharing addresses, though a treatise describes it more broadly as scanning web pages for posted addresses (sources differ). — [Cornell LII: address harvesting](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/address_harvesting); [vLex treatise section 9.05](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/-9-05-can-941805553)
- Aggravated violations allow treble damages, lifting the $2,000,000 cap on standard statutory damages to $6,000,000; enforcement is by the FTC, state attorneys general and internet access providers. — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/inbox/can-spam_act_problematic_spamming_techniques)
- Canada (CASL): senders must obtain consent, identify themselves and provide an unsubscribe mechanism; maximum administrative penalties are C$1 million for individuals and C$10 million for organizations. — [CRTC: CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm); [Substance Law: CASL compliance guide](https://substancelaw.ca/casl-compliance/) (via search summary)
- CASL "conspicuous publication" implied consent is narrow: the address must be conspicuously published, carry no statement refusing unsolicited messages, and the message must be relevant to the recipient's role; the CRTC has held that mere public availability of an address is not enough, and addresses reproduced or sold by a third party do not create implied consent. — [Bennett Jones, 2016](https://www.bennettjones.com/insights/blogs/2016/11/crtc-assesses-conspicuous-publication-basis-of-implied-consent-in-casl-enforcement-decision)
- CASL added address-harvesting prohibitions to PIPEDA, enforced by the Privacy Commissioner. — [Torys, 2016](https://www.torys.com/our-latest-thinking/publications/2016/05/canadas-privacy-commissioner-enters-the-casl-enforcement-ring)
- UK (PECR): the electronic-mail marketing consent rule does not apply to corporate subscribers (companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships, some government bodies), so B2B marketing email to them is permitted; sole traders and some partnerships are individual subscribers and need consent or soft opt-in. — [ICO: Business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- UK GDPR still applies when emailing named employees at corporate addresses, and the ICO suggests keeping a "do not email" suppression list of corporate subscribers who object. — [ICO: Business-to-business marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)
- EU: GDPR governs the data, but national ePrivacy implementations govern the email channel. Germany (UWG) generally requires prior express consent even for B2B cold email and lets competitors sue directly; a law-firm guide says Germany, Hungary and Poland apply B2C rules to B2B email. — [Morrison Foerster: A lawyer's guide to marketing compliance, 2023](https://media2.mofo.com/v3/assets/blt5775cc69c999c255/blte030b4975e725a10/642305842cf9c710750b34cb/230328-a-lawyers-guide-to-marketing-compliance.pdf); [Overloop](https://overloop.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-germany-gdpr-compliance) (vendor)
- France (CNIL) permits B2B cold email under legitimate interest if it relates to the recipient's professional role and includes an opt-out; France, the Netherlands and Spain are generally rated moderate risk with documented legitimate-interest assessments (vendor summaries, not legal authority). — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/gdpr-cold-email-b2b); [Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/gdpr-cold-email-b2b)

### Inferences
- A sponsorship pitch offering paid promotional services is plausibly a "commercial electronic mail message," so the creator should assume CAN-SPAM applies: accurate headers and subject, a valid physical postal address, a working opt-out honored promptly, and responsibility for anything a tool or contractor sends on their behalf. (These seven requirements are the well-known content of the FTC guide; the page could not be re-read this session.)
- The plan's most likely implementation (a bot that scrapes contact addresses from many company sites and mails them all) combines the two things the statutes single out: automated harvesting and volume. Per-email penalties make volume the multiplier of risk: 2,000 defective emails is 2,000 potential violations.
- A bot that registers multiple mail accounts by script to spread volume would touch a second listed aggravator (automated creation of multiple accounts).
- Practical enforcement risk against a single small creator is low in the US but not zero; the realistic near-term penalties are the non-legal ones in sections 6 and 7. In Canada and Germany the mail is unlawful by default without consent, so those recipients should be excluded or approached through inbound forms and marketplaces.

### Gaps
- Could not read the FTC guide, the statute text, the CRTC or ICO pages directly; exact wording, the current-year penalty figure and the precise harvesting definition need primary verification.
- No authoritative ruling found on whether a creator's one-to-one sponsorship pitch counts as commercial email under CAN-SPAM or as a "commercial electronic message" under CASL; treat as likely yes.
- Country-by-country EU rules come mostly from vendor blogs and conflict on Austria and Switzerland.
- This is research, not legal advice.

## 6. What deliverability constraints apply, and why does "blast to as many companies as possible" fail?

### Takeaway
Mailbox providers now enforce authentication and complaint limits that make bulk cold mail self-defeating: Gmail and Yahoo (February 2024) and Microsoft (May 2025) require SPF, DKIM and DMARC for senders above 5,000 messages a day and cap spam complaints at 0.3%. Cold-email practitioners keep volume to about 30 (at most 50) emails per inbox per day on separate, warmed-up domains.

### Cited Findings
- Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules took effect in February 2024; Gmail treats senders of about 5,000 or more messages per day as bulk senders, and Yahoo uses its own volume criteria. — [Google Workspace Admin Help: Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) (not fetched; via summaries); [Bird: Gmail and Yahoo sender requirements](https://bird.com/docs/knowledge-base/deliverability/gmail-yahoo-requirements)
- Spam-complaint ceiling is 0.3% for both Gmail and Yahoo, and Google recommends staying under 0.1%. — [Bird: What are the Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements?](https://bird.com/explained/deliverability/what-are-the-gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sender-requirements)
- One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) is required for bulk marketing mail, and unsubscribe requests must be honored within two days. — [Mailgun: Yahoogle bulk senders](https://mailgun.com/resources/research/yahoogle-bulk-senders); [Bird](https://bird.com/explained/deliverability/what-are-the-gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sender-requirements)
- Microsoft: for domains sending over 5,000 emails per day to Outlook.com/Hotmail/Live, SPF, DKIM and DMARC (at least p=none, aligned with SPF or DKIM) are required, with enforcement from May 5, 2025. — [Microsoft Tech Community: Outlook's new requirements for high-volume senders](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/exchange/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlooks-new-requirements-for-high-volume-senders/4399730)
- Microsoft's original plan routed non-compliant mail to Junk first; an April 29, 2025 update changed this to rejecting messages that fail the checks, with error "550; 5.7.515 Access denied, sending domain does not meet the required authentication level." Mailjet describes May 5 as the start of Junk routing, so secondary summaries conflict. — [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/exchange/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlooks-new-requirements-for-high-volume-senders/4399730); [Mailgun: Microsoft sender requirements](https://www.mailgun.com/blog/deliverability/microsoft-sender-requirements/); [Mailjet](https://www.mailjet.com/blog/email-best-practices/microsoft-sender-requirements/)
- Microsoft also recommends valid, reply-capable From/Reply-To addresses and a functional unsubscribe link; whether one-click is mandatory at Microsoft is disputed between summaries. — [Proofpoint](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/email-and-cloud-threats/microsoft-new-email-authentication-requirements) (via search summary)
- Per-inbox volume (vendor guidance): Instantly recommends 30 cold emails per inbox per day; Smartlead puts the ceiling at 30-50 and suggests 3 mailboxes per secondary domain, max 50 per mailbox per day; other vendors say 25-30. — [Instantly: slow ramp plan for new cold email domains](https://instantly.ai/blog/slow-ramp-plan-for-new-cold-email-domains-deliverability/); [Smartlead: how many cold emails per day](https://smartlead.ai/blog/how-many-cold-emails-per-day)
- Warm-up: at least 2 weeks for new inboxes and 4 weeks for brand-new domains before production sending. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/slow-ramp-plan-for-new-cold-email-domains-deliverability/)
- All practitioner sources say to keep cold volume off the primary domain, because "one failed campaign can damage your company's transactional email, marketing sends, and brand reputation at the same time." — [Instantly: cold email software mistakes to avoid](https://www.instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-software-mistakes-to-avoid-12-costly-errors-that-tank-deliverability-roi)
- Larger campaigns reply worse: 2.1% for 500+ recipients versus 5.8% under 50 (Belkins, 16.5M emails). — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)

### Inferences
- A 0.3% complaint ceiling means roughly 3 "report spam" clicks per 1,000 delivered emails puts a sender over the line. Unrequested templated pitches to marketing inboxes are exactly the kind of mail that gets reported, so a blast can exceed the threshold within the first few hundred sends.
- Staying under 5,000 a day does not create a safe harbor: the complaint thresholds and authentication expectations are how providers judge reputation at any volume, and a new or personal domain has no reputation to absorb complaints.
- Volume arithmetic: at the practitioner limit of 30 per inbox per day, 3,000 companies would take 100 inbox-days after 2-4 weeks of warm-up. "As many as possible, immediately" is not achievable without multi-domain infrastructure, which costs money and moves the creator into aggravated-practice territory if automated.
- The damage lands on the creator's own identity. If the blast goes out from the address used for real sponsor conversations, invoices and platform notices, later legitimate replies to brands may go to spam.

### Gaps
- Google's sender-guideline page could not be fetched; exact wording on what applies to all senders versus bulk senders, whether bulk status is permanent, and 2025 enforcement escalation is unverified here.
- Gmail and Google Workspace per-account daily sending caps were not verified in this session.
- Per-inbox limits are vendor recommendations with a commercial interest, not independent measurements.

## 7. Is there reputational risk in a small industry: do brand managers blacklist or dismiss mass-templated pitches?

### Takeaway
Evidence supports dismissal, not blacklisting: brand-side commentators say mass templates are recognized immediately and ignored, but no source found documents shared blacklists of creators. The reputational cost is real but mostly takes the form of a burned first impression and spam-filter history rather than a formal ban.

### Cited Findings
- Generic outreach, unclear proposals and mass-produced pitches "are often ignored before they're even opened." — [Influencer Marketing Hub: Pitching Brands via Email (2026)](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pitching-brands-via-email/)
- "Brands can usually tell immediately when creators mass-send the same template to dozens of companies without understanding the product, audience, or niche." — [Influencer Marketing Hub: PR email for influencers](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pr-email-for-influencers)
- Vin Matano (Creatorbuzz) named generic compliments as one of his biggest frustrations with pitches received. — [Influencer Marketing Hub: Pitching Brands via Email](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pitching-brands-via-email/) (via search summary)
- Guides advise against hollow praise such as "Love your products!" because it adds nothing. — [Toptal Creator: how to pitch to brands](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-pitch-to-brands) (via search summary)
- Brands themselves track creators who sponsor well: ThoughtLeaders scores channels on repeat sponsors and warns brands away from "overexposed, off-brand, or underperforming" creators. — [ThoughtLeaders: the ultimate playbook for running YouTube sponsorships](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/the-ultimate-playbook-for-running-youtube-sponsorships) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- The durable harm is technical and personal rather than institutional: a recipient who marks the pitch as spam trains their company's filter against the sender's domain, and the manager who saw a template is unlikely to open a later, better pitch from the same name.
- Many brands outsource creator buying to a small set of agencies and platforms (ThoughtLeaders, Agentio, marketplace tools). One blast can reach the same buyer through several client inboxes, which makes the duplication obvious.
- Each brand can only be pitched cold for the first time once. Spending that attempt on a template sent at an arbitrary threshold wastes the best prospects before the channel has its strongest numbers.

### Gaps
- No first-hand testimony from brand influencer managers about shared blacklists was found; the claim that blacklists exist is unsupported by this research.
- Sources here are creator-education and vendor articles, not surveys of brand-side behavior.

## 8. What do FTC endorsement rules and platform disclosure tools require, and which contract terms should a small creator watch?

### Takeaway
Any sponsored content must carry a disclosure that is "difficult to miss" and easily understood; the FTC's 2023 revision says a platform's built-in paid-partnership tool may not be sufficient on its own, and a disclosure only in the description is not enough. Contract attention should go to usage rights, whitelisting, exclusivity and view guarantees, each of which should be scoped and priced separately.

### Cited Findings
- The FTC finalized revised Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255) in mid-2023; since July 2023 a "clear and conspicuous" disclosure is defined as one that is "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers." — [Troutman Pepper Locke, 2023-07-10](https://www.troutman.com/insights/locke-lord-quickstudy-new-ftc-endorsement-guides-what-advertisers-and-influencers-need-to-know/); [FTC Endorsement Guides hub](https://ftc.gov/endorsementguides)
- The 2023 update says a platform's built-in disclosure tool "might not be an adequate disclosure" by itself; practitioners treat Instagram's "Paid Partnership" label and TikTok's sponsored toggle as supplements, not replacements. — [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/what-to-know-now-that-the-ftc-has-updated-its-endorsements-guides/); [Termly](https://termly.io/resources/articles/ftc-requirements-for-influencers/) (via search summary)
- A disclosure placed only in the description, comments or accompanying text of a video is not enough; for live streams the disclosure should be repeated periodically. — [Backstage: FTC guidelines every influencer needs to know](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/ftc-guidelines-for-influencers-80272/) (via search summary)
- The FTC evaluates what the audience sees and understands, not what the platform technically allows. — [Traverse Legal](https://www.traverselegal.com/blog/ftc-influencer-guidelines/) (via search summary)
- TikTok One participation requires compliance with TikTok's Branded Content Policy. — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-creators-can-sign-up-for-tiktok-one)
- Contract terms and typical pricing: paid usage/whitelisting about 15-25% of base fee per 30 days; exclusivity +25% to +100%; perpetual usage substantially more; itemize content, exclusivity and usage as separate lines. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/organic-vs-paid-usage-rights/); [Toptal Creator: influencer exclusivity clause](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/influencer-exclusivity-clause)
- Brands may write a view guarantee into the contract, obliging the creator to deliver a minimum view count. — [ThoughtLeaders glossary](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/sponsorship-glossary/view-guarantee)

### Inferences
- A compliant sponsored video uses both layers: the platform's paid-promotion toggle and a spoken or on-screen disclosure near the start of the integration in plain words ("sponsored by").
- For a small creator the highest-cost contract mistakes are unpriced perpetual usage rights, broad category exclusivity that blocks other sponsors, and view guarantees with make-good obligations on a channel whose views vary widely (as short-form-led channels do).
- Because disclosure failures are judged as deceptive practices, the creator and the brand both carry responsibility; a contract that asks the creator to omit disclosure should be refused.

### Gaps
- Penalty exposure for creators under the Endorsement Guides was not found in search results; the Guides are interpretive and enforcement runs through FTC Act deception cases, but no figure is sourced here.
- YouTube's "includes paid promotion" checkbox requirement and Meta's branded-content tool rules were not verified from primary help pages this session.
- No source captured on payment terms, kill fees, approval rounds or indemnity norms.

## 9. What would a better outreach system look like?

### Takeaway
Automation is useful for research, tracking and follow-up scheduling, not for the sending decision or the message body. The evidence supports a small, targeted, semi-automated pipeline: a lead list of brands already buying in the niche, one personalized pitch per brand, low daily volume from an authenticated domain, one or two follow-ups, and CRM tracking, alongside inbound and marketplace listings.

### Cited Findings
- Personalized structure, templated skeleton: "structure is templated; the hook is personalized," and "templated blast campaigns rarely work; tailored outreach at scale (using a CRM) does." — [Favikon: how to do influencer outreach](https://www.favikon.com/blog/how-to-do-influencer-outreach-templates-and-tips-that-actually-get-replies) (via search summary; written for brands, principle transfers)
- Lead sourcing from repeat sponsors of comparable channels. — [Circle: podcast sponsorship guide (Justin Moore)](https://circle.so/de/blog/podcast-sponsorship-guide); [ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/why-brands-should-look-at-a-youtubers-sponsorship-score)
- Volume discipline: about 30 cold emails per inbox per day, 2-4 weeks of warm-up, and a separate sending domain. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/slow-ramp-plan-for-new-cold-email-domains-deliverability/); [Smartlead](https://smartlead.ai/blog/how-many-cold-emails-per-day)
- Small campaigns and follow-ups raise replies: under 50 recipients 5.8% versus 2.1% at 500+; 3-5 follow-up steps 8.3% versus 4.1% (vendor data). — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Follow-up cadence: one nudge after 5-7 business days, stop after three messages. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pr-email-for-influencers) (via search summary)
- Pitch content: ROPE (relevant, organic, proof, easy to execute), about three sentences, explicit ask. — [Creator Science podcast](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/justin-moore-sponsorship-strategy/); [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pitching-brands-via-email/)
- Parallel passive channels with low thresholds: TikTok One at 10,000 followers, Instagram Creator Marketplace at about 1,000, Collabstr and Aspire at 1,000, YouTube Shopping affiliate at 500 subscribers. — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/tiktok-one/tiktok-one); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/instagram-creator-marketplace-eligibility); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/companies-matching-youtubers-with-brand-deals/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-opens-shopping-affiliate-program-to-creators-with-500-subscribers/)

### Inferences
- Suggested design, derived from the findings above:
  1. Lead list: automate collection of which brands sponsor the 20-50 closest channels in the niche (sponsor mentions in descriptions, ThoughtLeaders/SponsorRadar data); rank by repeat count; add products already used on camera. Expect a list of roughly 30-150 brands.
  2. Contact discovery: use the brand's published partnership or creator-program address or form; do not scrape addresses in bulk; tag each lead by country and exclude or route Canada and Germany to forms and marketplaces.
  3. Draft: generate a per-brand draft from a template with a researched hook (their current campaign, the creator's existing organic mention, a specific deliverable and price range); a human approves every send.
  4. Send: 5-20 per day from an authenticated custom domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), with real name, postal address and an opt-out line; keep a suppression list.
  5. Follow up once or twice at 5-7 business-day intervals, then stop.
  6. Track in a simple CRM: brand, contact, date, status, reply, rate quoted, outcome, renewal date.
  7. Trigger: use milestones to refresh the media kit and re-pitch warm contacts, not to fire a blast.
- Expected yield, using the vendor ranges above for small personalized campaigns (about 6-18% reply): 100 well-chosen brands could produce roughly 6-18 conversations. A 2,000-company blast at about 2% with spam-folder losses could produce fewer usable conversations while consuming every prospect and the sending domain.
- The existing bot effort is better redirected to the list-building and CRM stages, where automation carries no legal or deliverability risk.

### Gaps
- No source provides a tested end-to-end creator outreach pipeline with measured results; the design above is synthesized from separate findings.
- Close rates from reply to signed deal for small creators were not found.

## 10. When should a creator start pitching: wait for a big threshold or start early?

### Takeaway
Waiting for one large cross-platform viewership threshold is not how the market works. Sources consistently place the start of outreach at about 1,000 engaged subscribers in a defined niche, beginning with affiliate and product deals and moving to paid integrations as per-video long-form views become predictable.

### Cited Findings
- Common guidance is a minimum of about 1,000 subscribers before seeking sponsors; some guides say small channels land brand deals at 1,000-5,000 subscribers; paid offers get easier at 10,000+. — [Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/sponsorships-for-youtubers/); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel) (via search summary)
- Justin Moore's stated view is that deals do not depend on follower count but on understanding and solving the brand's need; he has taught "$1k+ brand deals, even with a small audience." — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/sponsorship-expert-shares-advice-for-creators/489127); [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/brand-deals/)
- Affiliate programs accept creators at any size, and product sponsorships, affiliate and gifting are the usual first deals. — [Podia](https://www.podia.com/articles/youtube-sponsorships-and-brand-deals) (via search summary)
- Brands are shifting toward smaller creators: 51.43% of surveyed marketers plan to expand nano-creator use in 2026 (secondary citation of the IMH survey). — [inBeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report)
- Nano-tier rates are modest: $100-$500 per YouTube integration at 1K-10K subscribers; $50-$200 per TikTok post at 1K-10K followers. — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel); [TTCalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/brand-deal-rates-by-followers/)
- Repeat sponsors are a signal brands look for, so early small deals build the track record used in later pricing. — [ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/why-brands-should-look-at-a-youtubers-sponsorship-score)

### Inferences
- A staged trigger fits the evidence better than a single threshold: join affiliate programs immediately; list on marketplaces as each platform's bar is met (1,000 followers; 10,000 on TikTok); begin targeted pitching of 10-30 closely matched brands once the last 10 long-form videos show a stable median view count; raise prices and widen the list as that median and the repeat-sponsor count grow.
- If the creator's reach is mostly short-form, the first investment that raises sponsorship income is long-form output and audience data, since that is what CPM-priced integrations are sold on.
- Early small deals have value beyond the fee: they supply the "proof" element of later pitches and the repeat-sponsor history that brand tools score.

### Gaps
- No dataset found on the median audience size at which creators land a first paid sponsorship, or on how niche changes that point.
- The "start at 1,000 subscribers" guidance comes from creator-education and vendor sites with an interest in onboarding small creators; it is consensus advice, not measured outcome data.
