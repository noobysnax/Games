Research date: November 20, 2026. US-creator focus.

## 1. How does each platform's algorithm rank content as of 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
All five surfaces rank per-viewer on predicted watch behavior plus satisfaction-type signals, not on upload volume or follower count; none publishes weights. Two view-count redefinitions (Shorts on March 31, 2025; all YouTube formats on August 24, 2026) mean public view counts are now "starts," so clip performance must be judged on engaged views, retention and viewed-vs-swiped, not headline views.

### Cited Findings

YouTube long-form
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] In a 2025 interview with YouTube creator liaison Rene Ritchie, Todd Beaupre (YouTube's head of growth and discovery) described recommendations as "pulling" videos for an individual viewer rather than "pushing" videos out broadly. CTR and watch time remain inputs, but the system also uses satisfaction measured through direct viewer surveys and other feedback; quoted: "We don't want to just assume that all time is valuable." — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] Per-viewer ranking draws on watch history, current context, time of day and device. The PPC Land headline reports a YouTube director saying subscribers skip about 90% of uploads in their feed (i.e., subscriber count does not guarantee views). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/youtube-algorithm/)
- [TRADE] Many "2026 guide" blogs claim satisfaction has *replaced* watch time as the primary signal; that is stronger than what the coverage of the Beaupre interview supports. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/); contrast [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-algorithm), [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/understanding-youtube-algorithm)
- [TRADE/OFFICIAL help page as reported] Starting August 24, 2026, YouTube counts a public view the moment a video starts to play across all formats (long-form, live, podcasts), aligning them with Shorts. The older stricter count survives as "engaged views" in YouTube Analytics Advanced Mode. YPP eligibility and earnings stay tied to engaged Shorts views / engaged watch hours; CTR, retention and average view duration are still computed on engaged views. Pre/post-August-24 public view counts are not comparable. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-how-it-counts-views-on-long-form-live/586166/); [Forbes, Aug 17, 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/08/17/youtube-changes-how-it-counts-views-handing-marketers-two-numbers-instead-of-one/); contradicted by [Watchfiwn](https://watchfiwn.com/blogs/youtube-view-count-change-engaged-views), which says long-form counting did not change (minority view; help-page-based sources support the change)
- [DATA, modeled] Agentio's analysis of 35,800 creator videos estimates public long-form view counts will run roughly 30% above engaged views under the new method. Third-party estimate; YouTube does not publish the engaged-view threshold. — [Agentio](https://www.agentio.com/blog/youtube-view-vs-engaged-view)

YouTube Shorts
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] Effective March 31, 2025, a Shorts view is counted every time a Short starts to play or replays, with no minimum watch time. The previous metric was renamed "engaged views" (Analytics > Advanced Mode). YouTube said earnings and YPP eligibility are unaffected because they remain tied to engaged views. — [TechCrunch, Mar 26, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Tubefilter, Mar 26, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/)
- [OFFICIAL feature / VENDOR interpretation] YouTube Studio reports "Viewed vs. swiped away" for each Short (Reach tab). Creators treat it as a hook check: a high swipe rate points to a weak opening. Secondary coverage reports Todd Sherman (Shorts product lead) calling it a great metric, but YouTube has not said this exact number is the ranking signal and does not publish ranking weights, test-batch sizes or viewed-percentage thresholds; vendor claims of specific "seed audience" batch sizes are unsupported. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Buffer (summary of YouTube's own Q&A)](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary coverage] YouTube's position per coverage "in YouTube's words": there is no ideal Shorts length, posting time or daily quota. Deleting and re-posting a Short risks being read as spam. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary coverage; attribution not isolated] Beaupre is reported as saying no single metric drives Shorts: clicks, absolute and relative watch time, likes, comments and survey responses are all used, with weights varying by viewer and device. I could not isolate which page in the result set carried this (candidates: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/), [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)).

TikTok
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok's recommendation-system page says the system builds a prediction score per video from user signals; highest-scoring videos are selected for the For You feed; user interactions "which may include time spent watching a video" are generally weighted more heavily than other factors. Signal groups: user interactions, content information, device/account settings. — [TikTok: Introduction to the recommendation system](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/recommendation-system)
- [VENDOR/TRADE, estimates] Third parties rank completion rate / watch time as the primary signal with shares, replays, saves and comments secondary. TikTok publishes signals but not weights. Search ranks mainly on query match, so keywords in captions and on-screen text matter more there. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/); [Sorank](https://www.sorank.com/blog-geo-seo/tiktok-algorithm)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok separates "allowed on platform" from "eligible for For You feed." FYF-ineligible categories include "reproduced or unoriginal content imported or uploaded without any new or creative edits, such as content with someone else's visible watermark or superimposed logo," and low-quality content such as extremely short clips. — [TikTok For You feed Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update) (note: URL is the 2024 H1 version; secondary sources cite a September 2025 guideline update — re-verify current text)
- [TRADE] US TikTok has operated under TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC since January 2026; TikTok said the US algorithm will be "retrained" on US user data and that US creators remain discoverable globally. No timeline or completion date published. Claims of 20–40% reach drops or of new completion-rate weighting are unverified blog claims; TikTok has confirmed no ranking changes. — [Al Jazeera, Jan 23, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/1/23/who-controls-tiktoks-us-platform-under-new-deal); [Keywords Everywhere](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/social-algorithm-updates/tiktok-algorithm/); [FastClips "facts vs rumours"](https://fastclips.app/learn/tiktok-us-algorithm-joint-venture)
- [TRADE/VENDOR] Creator Rewards Program evaluates four metrics: originality, play duration, search value, audience engagement; pays only on original videos longer than one minute. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-revamped-creator-rewards-program-incentivize-longer/710639/)

Instagram (Reels)
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] Adam Mosseri has named the top three ranking signals as watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach; he singled out sends per reach (of everyone who saw a post, how many sent it to a friend in DM) as one of the most important signals, especially for reaching non-followers. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389/); [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/whats-working-on-instagram-right-now-carousels-and-sends-says-adam-mosseri)
- [VENDOR, unverified] Numbers such as "sends are worth 3–5x a like" or "original posts get 40–60% more distribution" appear only in secondary blogs; no primary source found. — [Buffer guide (context)](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)
- [TRADE] Originality is a standing ranking principle (favoring original creators over reposters since 2022) and was tightened in 2024 (Reels) and April 30, 2026 (photos/carousels) — see section 4. — [Engadget, 2022](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [Tubefilter, Apr 30, 2026](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)

Facebook
- [OFFICIAL] June 17, 2025 (Meta for Creators): all videos uploaded to Facebook will be shared as Reels going forward; Reels will have no length or format restrictions after the change; the Video tab is renamed the Reels tab; previously posted videos stay as they are; rollout "gradually" to profiles and Pages globally over the following months. — [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/making-it-easier-to-create-videos-on-facebook); [TechCrunch, Jun 17, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/17/facebook-announces-that-all-videos-on-its-platform-will-soon-be-shared-as-reels); [Tubefilter, Jun 17, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/06/17/meta-facebook-reels-all-videos-update/)
- [OFFICIAL] Meta Transparency Center page on the Facebook Reels AI system (last updated June 19, 2026, per search summary): a lightweight model selects roughly 10–100 candidate reels per viewer; inputs include how many times the reel has been viewed by others, reels the viewer recently liked/watched/shared/commented on, the author, reel length, similarity to other reels, and match to what the viewer tends to engage with. The page says models change frequently. — [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-reels/)
- [VENDOR reports of an OFFICIAL engineering post; primary not retrieved] In January 2026 Meta's Reels team reportedly described a survey-based interest score (viewers rate 1–5 how well a video matches their interests) now feeding Reels ranking, calling likes, shares and watch time "noisy." Reported in secondary Facebook-algorithm guides in the result set (e.g. [nbksocial](https://www.nbksocial.com/journal/how-facebook-decides-which-reels-to-show/), [petalie](https://petalie.ai/learn/facebook-algorithm)); I could not confirm the original Meta post.
- [VENDOR, do not use] Claims in SEO blogs about "RankNet-7," "4.2 trillion ranking decisions per day," "247 updates in 2025," or "Friend Bubbles" could not be traced to Meta. — e.g. [amraandelma](https://www.amraandelma.com/top-facebook-algorithm-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Every one of these systems scores each clip on its own merits with each viewer. A 60-second slice that opens mid-thought gets swiped (Shorts), fails completion (TikTok), earns no sends (Instagram), and scores poorly on interest surveys (Facebook, YouTube). The algorithms contain no mechanism that rewards "being part of a complete set."
- Because YouTube now reports first-frame "views" everywhere, a flood of Shorts will produce large public view numbers that say little; engaged views and viewed-vs-swiped are the diagnostic numbers for a clipping test.
- The convergence on satisfaction surveys (YouTube and, reportedly, Facebook) penalizes clips that hold attention by withholding the payoff ("watch part 2") more than older watch-time-only systems did.

### Gaps
- No official numeric thresholds exist for any platform (e.g., "good" viewed-vs-swiped %, completion %). Vendor benchmarks were not collected because none are official.
- Could not confirm from a primary source whether the TikTok US retraining has measurably changed ranking as of November 2026.
- A Metricool 2026 Instagram figure (average Reel watch time 8.5 seconds, up 118% year over year, from ~24.4M posts) surfaced in a search summary but I could not isolate the source page; treat as unverified.

## 2. Does mechanically chopping a long video into consecutive 60-second segments perform well, versus selecting standalone moments?

### Takeaway
No independent study directly tests "consecutive chunks vs. curated moments," but every source found — platform guidance, clipping practitioners, and the few data points — points the same way: clips must stand alone with their own hook, context and payoff, and context-dependent "Part N" clips lose most viewers; mechanical chunking is also the pattern that sits closest to the platforms' "mass-produced/low-variation" and "low-quality" language.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR/practitioner consensus] Clip guides agree each clip must make sense on its own: "if someone needs to have watched the full video to understand a 45-second clip, it won't perform"; "if a viewer needs the prior two minutes to follow it, it will not travel." Hook within the first 2–3 seconds; add a short line of context; leave a beat after the punchline before cutting. (Quotes come from the practitioner guides in this result cluster; exact page per quote not isolated.) — [Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/how-to-repurpose-long-form-video-into-clips); [Clipping Agency](https://clippingagency.co/how-to-grow-a-youtube-channel-with-shorts-the-clipping-strategy/); [Emerging AI Review workflow](https://www.emerging-ai-review.com/turning-long-videos-into-high-performing-short-clips-a-practical-workflow/); [Quso](https://quso.ai/blog/short-form-content-strategy)
- [VENDOR] The best-performing clips "usually come from the middle third of your video, not the intro." — [Katto](https://katto.tech/blog/how-to-make-youtube-shorts-from-long-videos-2026-guide)
- [VENDOR] Rule of thumb on series vs. standalone: use standalone clips for isolated insights or timely reactions; use a series only when the angle/format/promise can repeat "without confusing the viewer." — [Quso](https://quso.ai/blog/short-form-content-strategy)
- [VENDOR/practitioner] "A series that requires heavy context to understand Part 1 will never recruit new viewers from the For You page." — [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/tiktok-series-content-strategy) (attribution to this page vs. a sibling guide in the same result set not fully isolated)
- [Anecdotal data] One creator's Part 1 drew about 80K views in a day while Part 2 had 21K; the guide attributes much of the gap to Part 2 being written as if everyone had seen Part 1. Suggested fixes: one-line recap, part number on screen, one callback, resolve the Part 1 cliffhanger immediately. — [DEV Community guide](https://dev.to/agentworkshop/how-to-plan-the-next-part-of-a-tiktok-series-so-part-2-viewers-arent-lost-2026-recap-callback-e1a)
- [VENDOR, unverified] A claim that multi-part series drive a "2.8x higher return viewer rate" than standalone videos has no traceable source. — [smmnut](https://smmnut.com/blog/tiktok-mini-series-strategy-2025/)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's own blog on converting Shorts views into long-form growth frames the Short as a promise: the first 5–10 seconds of the linked long video should deliver on the topic the Short promised. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok lists "low-quality content, such as extremely short clips" and unoriginal content "without any new or creative edits" as For You-ineligible. — [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update)
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (renamed July 15, 2025) targets mass-produced or repetitive, templated low-variation content and is applied at the channel level. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- [VENDOR, hands-on test] AI clippers' own "virality scores" are unreliable (a 92-score clip can be worse than a 64-score clip), i.e., even automated moment *selection* needs human review, let alone no selection at all. — [Javis Review](https://javisreview.com/review/opusclip-review-2026/); [Techsy](https://techsy.io/en/blog/opusclip-vs-vizard)

### Inferences
- A 20-minute video cut into consecutive 60-second chunks yields ~20 clips, of which roughly 19 start mid-thought and most end without a payoff. On every platform in section 1 the first seconds decide whether the clip is watched or swiped, so the expected outcome is that the majority of chunks fail the initial test audience, and the account's averages (viewed-vs-swiped, completion, sends per reach) are dominated by the weak chunks.
- "Part 37"-style labeling tells a cold viewer at second one that they lack context; the 80K→21K anecdote suggests severe drop-off even at Part 2. Sequential parts work only where each part is itself a complete beat (story formats, countdowns), which is curated editing, not mechanical chunking.
- Mechanical chunking of your own footage is unlikely to violate originality rules (see section 4), so the risk is low reach and weak account-level signals rather than a policy strike — with one caveat: dozens of near-identical-looking sequential uploads per video is the pattern closest to YouTube's "mass-produced/repetitive" wording, and YPP reviews judge the whole channel.
- Better variation supported by the evidence: pick 3–8 moments per long video that each have hook + context + payoff (often from the middle third), re-edit each natively (reframe, captions, new first line), and treat the full-video chunk dump as unnecessary.

### Gaps
- No independent, controlled data study comparing consecutive mechanical chunks vs. curated standalone clips (retention, follow-through to Part 2, subscriber conversion) was found. This is the single most important missing piece; a channel-level A/B test would be needed.
- No official platform statement specifically about "Part 1/Part 2" clip series was found.
- Named clipping strategists' statements (specific agencies/individuals with track records) were not retrievable; the practitioner sources found are mostly tool/agency blogs.

## 3. Current length limits and sweet spots per platform

### Takeaway
Limits as of late 2026: YouTube Shorts up to 3 minutes; Instagram Reels 3 minutes (announced Jan 18, 2025; some sources report longer uploads on some accounts); TikTok up to 10 minutes recorded / 60 minutes uploaded, with a >1-minute requirement for Creator Rewards; Facebook has no Reel length cap since June 2025. Data studies show shorter clips win on engagement rate while longer clips (1–3 minutes) win on median views on TikTok; Reels reach appears best around 30–60 seconds; Shorts data is inconclusive (duration explains very little).

### Cited Findings
- [TRADE/VENDOR] YouTube raised the Shorts maximum from 60 seconds to 3 minutes in October 2024 (vertical or square video). — [Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/youtube-shorts-now---long); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)
- [VENDOR, unverified, flag] One source says that from September 24, 2026 a Content ID claim no longer automatically blocks a new 1–3-minute Short, while noting YouTube's US English help page still says it does. Not confirmed against a YouTube announcement. Relevant if clips contain claimed music/footage. (Surfaced in the Shorts-length result cluster, e.g. [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026), [CreateSocial](https://createsocial.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-length); exact page not isolated.)
- [OFFICIAL] Mosseri, Threads, January 18, 2025: "We're now supporting reels up to three minutes long on Instagram. We still are focused on short form video over long form, but hopefully this gives creators a bit more room to be creative." Previous cap was 90 seconds. — [Mosseri on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DE-efFqyStv?hl=en); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-instagram-reels-3-minutes-us-tiktok-ban/)
- [TRADE, conflicting] Medianama reports an Instagram-authored slideshow saying Instagram only recommends videos of 90 seconds or less to unconnected (non-follower) audiences; whether that still holds after the 3-minute change is unverified. A 2026 blog asserts 90 seconds is still a non-follower "reach cliff" without a primary source. — [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-instagram-reels-3-minutes-us-tiktok-ban/); [moonb.io](https://www.moonb.io/blog/instagram-reel-length)
- [VENDOR, conflicting] Some 2026 guides say Reel uploads can now be up to 20 minutes on some accounts and that Instagram rolls out length changes gradually; Sprout Social notes posts scheduled through Meta's Reels publishing API still follow a 3–90 second limit. — [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/how-long-can-an-instagram-reel-be); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/?p=105118)
- [VENDOR] TikTok: in-app camera limit 10 minutes; upload limit 60 minutes; options can vary by account and region. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-long-should-a-social-media-video-be/); [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/social-media-video-aspect-ratios-and-sizes-the-2025-guide/)
- [TRADE/VENDOR] TikTok Creator Rewards Program: pays only on original public videos longer than one minute; account needs 10,000 followers and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days; 18+. One guide adds Duets, Stitches, Photo Mode and sponsored content are excluded (single source). Third-party payout estimate $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views (estimate, varies). — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-creator-next/); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program); [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/creator-rewards-program/)
- [OFFICIAL] Facebook: after the June 2025 change Reels have no length or format restriction; the earlier Reel cap was 90 seconds (raised from 60 in March 2023). — [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/making-it-easier-to-create-videos-on-facebook); [SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/how-long-can-a-facebook-reel-be/)
- [VENDOR] Snapchat Spotlight videos need to run longer than one minute to qualify for monetization (see section 9). — [Storika](https://www.storika.ai/guides/snapchat-creator-monetization-2026)
- [DATA] Socialinsider, 6M+ brand TikTok videos, January–June 2026: median views rise with length from about 1,000 (15–30 s) to 11,136 (120–180 s); engagement rate peaks at 6.00% for 15–30 s but ranges only 4.20%–6.00% across all length buckets. — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-are-tiktok-videos/)
- [DATA, secondhand] Buffer, 1.1M TikTok videos (published March 2025): median reach 194 views for 5–10 s clips vs. 432.5 for videos over one minute. A different write-up says Buffer found 3–10-minute videos had the highest average views; the two summaries don't agree — check Buffer's original. — [SocialRails summary](https://socialrails.com/blog/best-tiktok-video-length-maximum-engagement)
- [DATA] Socialinsider 2026 Reels: around 30–60 seconds reaches the most people (5.60% reach rate), falling to 3.50% for Reels over two minutes; another summary of Socialinsider says 45–60 second Reels get the most views (~10.3K average). — [Socialinsider video statistics](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)
- [DATA/VENDOR, mixed] Shorts: Adobe survey — 46% of creators say under 30 seconds is ideal; an analysis of 574 Shorts from the most-subscribed channels found median 40 s / mean 43.9 s (what top channels post, not what performs best); other sources cite 50–60 s; Channel Makers reportedly found duration explains only about 2% of the variation in views. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study); [Mark Studios](https://www.markstudios.com/research/youtube-shorts-length-study)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary coverage] YouTube says there is no ideal Shorts length. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- "Exactly ~1 minute" is a defensible length only on TikTok, where a clip must exceed 60 seconds to earn Creator Rewards and longer clips show higher median views. On Reels the data favors 30–60 s and non-follower recommendation of >90 s clips is uncertain; on Shorts length is nearly irrelevant relative to the hook.
- Since Shorts, Reels and Facebook all allow 3 minutes or more, a self-contained 75–150 second segment (one complete idea) is now postable everywhere unedited in length, which removes the main historical reason for cutting at an arbitrary 60-second mark.
- Clip length should be set by the natural boundaries of the moment, with a slight push past 60 s for TikTok monetization only when the content holds.

### Gaps
- Could not verify current official help-page text for Instagram's maximum Reel length (3 minutes vs. longer) or whether >90 s Reels are recommended to non-followers in late 2026.
- No official TikTok maximum-length page was retrieved; figures are from secondary guides.
- No large independent Shorts dataset on performance by duration for 2025–2026 was found.
- No Facebook Reels performance-by-length study was found.

## 4. Cross-posting penalties and originality rules: does posting your own clip natively everywhere count as unoriginal?

### Takeaway
All four platforms' originality rules target re-uploading *other people's* content or mass-produced/templated content; posting your own clips natively is not what any of them target. The concrete, documented cross-posting risk is another app's watermark/logo (Instagram since 2021, TikTok's For You eligibility standards, Meta's 2025 guidance), plus channel-level "mass-produced/repetitive" judgments on YouTube.

### Cited Findings

YouTube
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] On July 15, 2025 YouTube renamed its YPP "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" to better identify mass-produced and repetitive content. YouTube and creator liaison Rene Ritchie described it as a minor clarification of a long-standing rule — such content was already ineligible for monetization — and said reaction, clips and commentary channels are not specifically targeted. The separate "reused content" policy did not change. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/); [TechCrunch, Jul 9, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- [TRADE] The policy applies at channel level: violations can remove monetization from the whole channel. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- [OFFICIAL community FAQ, as summarized] "Reused content" is content that is not clearly your own original creation; examples flagged include compilations with little narrative, short videos compiled from other social platforms, and content copied from another source without substantive changes; reviewers judge the channel as a whole; permission/licensing does not make reused content eligible. YouTube's inauthentic-content examples reportedly allow a shared intro/outro when the bulk of each video differs, and disallow templated low-variation videos. — [YouTube Community FAQ: Reused Content & YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/community-guide/271248162/%F0%9F%94%8E-faq-reused-content-youtube%E2%80%99s-partner-program?hl=en); [Hypertube explainer](https://hypertube.io/blog/youtube-partner-program-reused-content-policy-explained)
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] Ritchie has advised streamers who lack reach to "clip those up into Shorts" (about livestream clips). — [Tubefilter, Aug 26, 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)

Instagram
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary coverage] Since February 2021 Instagram recommends Reels with visible watermarks or logos from other apps less often. Mosseri later said your own logo doesn't hurt reach; other apps' logos do (per an October 2024 summary). Some sites tie a watermark statement to January 2025 — unverified date. — [dot.LA](https://dot.la/instagram-tiktok-content-2657200198.html); [RepostIt](https://repostit.io/does-cross-posting-hurt-reach/)
- [TRADE] 2022: Mosseri said Instagram would favor original content and be less likely to recommend accounts that aggregate and reshare others' content. 2024: Reels change replaced reposts with the original in recommendations. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [Tubefilter, Apr 30, 2026](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)
- [TRADE] April 30, 2026: Instagram extended its originality rules from Reels to photos and carousels. Accounts that primarily post unoriginal content become ineligible for recommendations to non-followers (followers still see posts). Evaluation is over roughly a month of an account's posts rather than single posts. Re-uploading someone else's work "without adding meaningful creative input to enhance the original, such as just adding a border, watermark, subtitles, or a credit in the captions" does not qualify as original. Mosseri called aggregators "an important part of the ecosystem" but told them to remix and "make it your own." — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator); [PetaPixel, Apr 30, 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- [VENDOR, unconfirmed number] A "10 or more reposts in 30 days" threshold circulates; some sources attribute it to the 2024 Reels rule, others to 2026. — [Status 200 Uploads](https://status200uploads.com/blog/instagram-originality-rules-reposts); [Blossom](https://blossai.com/blog/instagram-originality-repost-rule)
- [VENDOR summary of platform statements] Cross-posting a Reel you filmed yourself is not what the rule targets; nothing the platforms have published says cross-posting your own content hurts engagement; the advice is to upload a clean export with no other-app watermark. — [Post Bridge](https://www.post-bridge.com/blog/does-cross-posting-hurt-engagement); [Blossom](https://blossai.com/blog/instagram-originality-repost-rule)

Facebook
- [OFFICIAL] July 14, 2025 (Meta for Creators, "Combating unoriginal content"): accounts that repeatedly reuse others' text, photos or videos lose access to Facebook monetization programs for a period and get reduced distribution on everything they share; detected duplicate videos get reduced distribution so the original creator benefits; Meta is testing links on duplicates pointing to the original. — [Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content); [TechCrunch, Jul 14, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/14/following-youtube-meta-announces-crackdown-on-unoriginal-facebook-content)
- [TRADE] Not penalized: reaction videos, trend participation, transformative edits, commentary. Meta's guidance to creators: post primarily original content you filmed or created; avoid content with third-party watermarks or content visibly recycled from other apps; licensed/sourced footage is fine if meaningfully enhanced. Meta reported removing about 10 million profiles impersonating large creators and acting on 500,000 accounts for spammy behavior or fake engagement (H1 2025). — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/meta-follows-youtube-in-crackdown-on-unoriginal-content/551096/); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/); [Tubefilter, Jul 15, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/15/ai-slop-unoriginal-repetitive-content-monetization-facebook-meta/)
- [TRADE] Meta has since added further measures to credit original creators (article found; details not retrieved). — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-adds-more-measures-to-ensure-original-creators-get-credit/814764/)

TikTok
- [OFFICIAL] For You feed ineligible: "reproduced or unoriginal content imported or uploaded without any new or creative edits, such as content with someone else's visible watermark or superimposed logo." Such content is generally not removed, just not recommended. — [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update)
- [VENDOR quoting TikTok] "Content ineligible for the For You feed will not be eligible for the Creator Rewards Program." Accounts that post a lot of FYF-ineligible content may become harder to find at the account level. Cropping out a watermark does not help if the content is identified as unoriginal. — [TallyTerms](https://tallyterms.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-requirements); [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok); [Tagly](https://trytagly.com/blog/tiktok-for-you-feed-ineligible)
- [TRADE] Creator Rewards "originality" is described as content "unique to the creator" that showcases "their point of view or creative thought process." — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/)

### Inferences
- Posting the same self-made clip natively (clean file, no other-app watermark) to Shorts, TikTok, Reels and Facebook is not "unoriginal" under any of the four rule sets as reported: each is worded around *someone else's* content or mass-produced content. This is an inference from the policy wording and secondary summaries; no platform was found stating verbatim "cross-posting your own video is fine."
- The practical hazards are (a) exporting from TikTok/CapCut/Instagram with a visible app logo, (b) letting a third-party clipper or fan account post the same clip first on Facebook/Instagram, which could make *your* upload the detected "duplicate," and (c) on YouTube, a channel whose Shorts shelf is dominated by dozens of templated sequential chunks being read as "mass-produced/repetitive" in a YPP review.
- TikTok's wording ("imported or uploaded without any new or creative edits") is the least clear about self-reposts; re-editing natively (captions/text added in-app, distinct first frame) is the conservative path, and matters for Creator Rewards "originality."
- If paid clippers are used (section 9), their accounts — not yours — are the ones exposed to Instagram's April 2026 and Facebook's July 2025 repost rules, which may limit how much reach clip farms can deliver on Meta surfaces going forward.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full primary policy pages (YouTube channel monetization policies, Meta's blog post, Instagram's April 30, 2026 announcement, TikTok's current Community Guidelines) because fetching was blocked; wording above is as reported in search summaries.
- No official statement from any platform that explicitly addresses a creator posting the identical self-made clip across all platforms.
- No evidence found on whether platforms detect and downrank the *same* file uploaded to a sibling surface (e.g., identical Reel on Instagram and Facebook via Meta cross-posting); nothing suggests a penalty, but nothing official confirms.
- Whether Instagram's "10 reposts in 30 days" number is real and current is unconfirmed.

## 5. Does posting many clips per day hurt an account? Recommended frequency per platform

### Takeaway
No platform has stated a frequency penalty, and the largest data studies (Buffer) find reach per post rises, not falls, with more posts per week — but with diminishing returns, and the effect is correlational. The risk in a chunk-dump strategy is not "posting too often" per se; it is posting many weak, near-identical clips that compete for the same followers and drag down per-post signals.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] Buffer, 2.1M Instagram posts from 102K accounts (August 2025): versus 1–2 feed posts/week, reach per post was about 12% higher at 3–5/week, 18% higher at 6–9, and 24% higher at 10+. Buffer's Instagram and LinkedIn studies found no point where extra posts lowered reach per post; the first step up gives the largest gain. Correlational; Stories excluded. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/study-shows-posting-more-instagram-leads-to-more-reach/757633/); [Buffer growth playbook](https://buffer.com/resources/creator-growth-playbook/)
- [DATA, secondhand, provisional] Buffer TikTok analysis (11.4M posts, October 2025): 2–5 posts/week up to 17% more views per post; 11+/week up to 34% more (another summary cites 29% for 6–10/week); the gain is described as more shots at virality, not a per-post algorithmic boost. Not confirmed against Buffer's original. — [SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary coverage] Mosseri avoids a fixed number: some successful creators post once a month and others five Reels a day; posting more generally helps reach, but the right cadence is one you can sustain; quality before quantity. The widely repeated "3–5 posts a week according to Mosseri" lacks a primary source. Several Reels on the same day compete for the same followers. — [Kontentino](https://www.kontentino.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/); [Aubrium](https://aubrium.com/blog/post-every-day-myth)
- [OFFICIAL] Rene Ritchie (YouTube creator liaison) wrote in July 2024 that he was running an experiment posting three Shorts a day on his channel; no penalty reported. — [YouTube Blog, Jul 25, 2024](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-july-25-2024/)
- [OFFICIAL, via secondary coverage] YouTube states no daily quota or magic number for Shorts; deleting and reposting can look like spam. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] A YouTube director says subscribers skip roughly 90% of uploads in their feed — more uploads do not translate into more subscriber attention. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [DATA] AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 English-language channels (March 2025–March 2026): channels that kept Shorts at about 25–40% of uploads did best in fitness, food, business and education — roughly 1–2 Shorts per week alongside 3–5 long-form videos. Correlational. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/should-you-chase-shorts-views-or-double-down-on-long-form-for-channel-growth)
- [VENDOR] Practitioner cadence advice: fewer than three Shorts per week is often too slow to learn from; "one Short every 2–3 days beats dumping 10 at once." — [Clipping Agency](https://clippingagency.co/how-to-grow-a-youtube-channel-with-shorts-the-clipping-strategy/); [Quso](https://quso.ai/blog/short-form-content-strategy) (attribution of each line within this cluster not isolated)
- [VENDOR] On TikTok, accounts that post a lot of For You-ineligible content can become less discoverable at the account level. — [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok)

### Inferences
- Publishing 20+ chunks of one video in a burst is far outside the range any study measured as beneficial (Buffer's top bucket is 10+/week), and on YouTube the AIR data associates the best outcomes with Shorts as a minority of uploads, not 20 Shorts per long video.
- Because follower feeds are selective (90% skipped on YouTube; same-day Reels compete with each other), same-day sequential chunks mostly cannibalize one another among existing followers, and each must win cold distribution independently.
- A reasonable evidence-consistent cadence: 1 strong clip per platform per day (up to ~3/day on Shorts/TikTok if quality holds), drawn from curated moments, spread over the weeks between long-form uploads.

### Gaps
- No official TikTok posting-frequency recommendation was found (the commonly cited "1–4 times per day" could not be traced to a TikTok page in this research).
- No study isolates the effect of posting many clips from the *same* source video on account-level reach.
- No Facebook-specific posting-frequency data found.

## 6. Do Shorts help or hurt a long-form YouTube channel?

### Takeaway
YouTube says Shorts do not hurt long-form because the two formats' watch histories are separated in recommendations — which also means Shorts viewers are not automatically fed your long videos. Independent data is mixed and niche-dependent: mixing in a modest share of Shorts correlates with more subscribers in several niches, but not in gaming or beauty, and one academic study found long-form views fell after creators adopted Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, via vidIQ and Social Media Today] YouTube (Todd Beaupre) said it observed that viewers who found a channel through Shorts were not well served by being recommended its long-form videos, so YouTube separated Shorts watch history from long-form watch history; discovering a channel through Shorts does not inform which long videos get recommended outside the Shorts experience. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/youtube-shorts-long-form-viewers-algorithm); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-shares-new-insight-into-how-to-use-shorts-to-boost-your-channel-per/628491)
- [vidIQ, relaying YouTube guidance; single source] "You don't need to isolate your Shorts in a separate YouTube channel... The only exception is if your Shorts align with an interest not central to your long-form content." A separate Shorts channel could hurt growth. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/youtube-shorts-long-form-viewers-algorithm)
- [OFFICIAL documentation as quoted by a vendor] YouTube's recommendations documentation says new formats will not inherently confuse the algorithm or negatively impact a channel's overall performance. A circulated Ritchie quote ("Shorts will not hurt your long-form content...") has no source link and is unverified. — [YTGrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/shorts-vs-long-form); [Market Maker Mgmt](https://marketmakermgmt.com/blog-list2/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-your-channel)
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube's blog presents the Related Video link as the main tool to send a Shorts viewer to a long-form video, livestream or another Short on the same channel; the first 5–10 seconds of the long video should deliver on the Short's promise. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- [DATA] AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 English-language channels, March 2025–March 2026 (correlational, AIR itself calls it niche-dependent and not proven causal): in most niches, channels mixing Shorts with long-form have more subscribers than long-form-only channels; fitness channels that mixed in Shorts had 41% more subscribers; gaming channels *without* Shorts had 85% more subscribers than mixed ones; gaming and beauty did better long-form-only; for food, channels with Shorts led at smaller sizes but at 10M+ monthly views long-form-only channels were 47% ahead; in one 100K–1M comparison long-only and mixed were nearly identical (73,600 vs 72,400). — [AIR: Do Shorts help long-form (18,000 channels)](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels); [AIR: Shorts vs long-form 2026](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/should-you-chase-shorts-views-or-double-down-on-long-form-for-channel-growth)
- [ACADEMIC] "Shorts on the Rise" (Rajendran, Creusy, Garnes; Upfluence; arXiv 2402.18208; 250 creators; Wilcoxon signed-rank on pre/post data): significant decrease in long-form viewership after creators introduced Shorts; more pronounced for predominantly long-form creators, for Lifestyle and Entertainment categories, and for creators above 5M subscribers; Information/Education channels were less affected. Before/after design — does not prove causation. Exact effect sizes not retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18208)
- [VENDOR consensus] Shorts views do not count toward the 4,000 public watch-hour YPP threshold; the alternative Shorts path is 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-to-get-your-first-1000-youtube-subscribers/); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/convert-shorts-viewers-to-subscribers)
- [DATA] AIR study of 274 channels: Shorts RPM is roughly 3–14% of long-form RPM. — [AIR Shorts RPM](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- [Creator forum opinion] Some creators argue Shorts attract a different type of viewer and recommend a separate channel linked by Related Video. Opinion, not measurement. — [Skool community post](https://www.skool.com/creatorboost/do-shorts-help-or-hurt-a-channels-growth-ai-data-i-found-interesting)

### Inferences
- For a channel whose clips come from its own long videos (same topic, same audience), YouTube's guidance favors keeping Shorts on the main channel and attaching the Related Video link to each Short; the separate-channel exception (different interest) does not apply.
- Shorts are a discovery and subscriber tool, not a watch-hour or revenue tool: they don't count toward the 4,000 hours and pay a small fraction of long-form RPM. Subscribers gained via Shorts may not watch long-form, since recommendations treat the formats separately.
- The niche split matters for a games-related project: AIR's data shows gaming channels did better *without* Shorts (85% more subscribers long-form-only). This is correlational, but it argues for testing a small number of high-quality Shorts rather than flooding a gaming channel with chunks.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the original Creator Insider/Beaupre video or its date (the Social Media Today URL suggests 2022); whether watch-history separation is still exactly as described in late 2026 is unconfirmed.
- No YouTube-published data on Shorts-driven long-form lift.
- arXiv paper effect sizes and Tubefilter's 2023 "cannibalization" article content were not retrievable.

## 7. How effectively do short-form viewers convert to long-form viewers/subscribers, and what improves it?

### Takeaway
Conversion is very low — fractions of a percent of Shorts views click through to long-form — and no platform publishes an official rate; third-party figures conflict. The funnel only works at high reach and when the Short makes a specific promise that the linked long video pays off immediately.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA/VENDOR] AIR Media-Tech frames Shorts-to-long-form conversion as moving from about 0.04% to a 0.5% target: "Getting from 0.04% to 0.5% Shorts-to-long-form conversion is worth more than doubling your upload frequency." Methodology for the conversion figure not visible. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- [Reported in the YouTube-blog/related-video result cluster; exact page not isolated] Click-through rate on the Shorts Related Video link "can be as low as 0.05% in some cases," so the approach depends on high Shorts reach. A "5% benchmark CTR" also circulates without a cited origin; claims of "25–40% higher long-form discovery" for channels with a Shorts funnel have no methodology. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/drive-traffic-shorts-to-long-videos); [LongStories (unverified claims)](https://longstories.ai/blog/shorts-boost-long-form-video-performance)
- [VENDOR, conflicting] View-to-subscriber conversion benchmarks: Shorts 0.5–2% vs long-form 3–8%; or 1–3% vs 5–10%; or 0.5–1.5% vs roughly 2–4%. All agree Shorts convert at a fraction of long-form's rate; none is authoritative. — [YoutoWire](https://www.youtowire.com/blog/youtube-shorts-vs-long-videos-subscribers); [GoodSponsor](https://goodsponsor.io/blog/best-youtube-content); [Touhfa](https://touhfa.art/blog/growth/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-subscribers/)
- [Weak source: student/product proposal, India-specific] "62% of Shorts-first viewers never transition beyond 90 seconds to long-form content (>10 min)." — [Nextleap PDF](https://assets.nextleap.app/submissions/IncreasingYouTubeLong-FormViewers-75a70bb6-7231-4d58-8fc3-d589ec5dc5f4.pdf)
- [VENDOR] YouTube does not publish a Shorts-to-long-form conversion rate; the Shorts traffic source in Studio (which Shorts drove long-form views) is the closest proxy. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/convert-shorts-viewers-to-subscribers); [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- [OFFICIAL] Tactics YouTube itself recommends: attach the Related Video link (set in Studio on the Short; target must be on your own channel), and make the opening 5–10 seconds of the long video deliver what the Short promised. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- [OFFICIAL, via vidIQ] Because Shorts and long-form watch histories are separated, a Shorts viewer will not be organically recommended your long videos just for watching your Shorts — the explicit link/call-out is the bridge. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/youtube-shorts-long-form-viewers-algorithm)
- [VENDOR/practitioner] For multi-part clips, a one-line recap, on-screen part number and a real payoff reduce drop-off between parts. — [DEV Community guide](https://dev.to/agentworkshop/how-to-plan-the-next-part-of-a-tiktok-series-so-part-2-viewers-arent-lost-2026-recap-callback-e1a)

### Inferences
- Worked example using the only figures available: at 0.04–0.05% click-through, 100,000 Shorts views yield roughly 40–50 long-form clicks; at the 0.5% "target," about 500. Off-platform (TikTok/Reels/Facebook to YouTube) should be assumed lower still, because it requires leaving the app and those platforms have no in-feed link to YouTube; no data was found.
- Therefore clips should be valued mainly for (a) on-platform audience and monetization on each platform and (b) brand familiarity, not as a meaningful direct traffic source to the long video. A plan that depends on "every chunk sends viewers to the full video" is not supported by any data found.
- The clip types most likely to convert are open-loop moments whose resolution genuinely lives in the long video, paired with the Related Video link — the opposite of a complete sequential upload, which gives the viewer no reason to leave (the whole video is already there in chunks).

### Gaps
- No reliable published conversion data for TikTok/Instagram/Facebook viewers to YouTube long-form or subscribers.
- No measured data on the effectiveness of pinned comments, link-in-bio, or spoken "full video on YouTube" call-outs; and no confirmation of whether platforms downrank clips that mention a competing platform (commonly claimed, not sourced here).
- AIR's 0.04% baseline definition (click vs. view vs. subscriber) could not be verified.

## 8. Vertical reframing and format best practices

### Takeaway
All four short-form surfaces are built around 1080x1920 (9:16); horizontal footage should be actively reframed rather than letterboxed, with text and faces kept inside platform-specific safe zones. Guidance here is almost entirely from vendor sources, and the exact safe-zone numbers conflict.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR consensus] Standard canvas is 1080x1920, 9:16, for TikTok, Reels and Shorts; horizontal video gets letterboxed and underperforms in short-form feeds. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/social-media-video-aspect-ratios-and-sizes-the-2025-guide/); [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/video-aspect-ratios-for-social-media)
- [VENDOR] TikTok, Instagram and Facebook place interface elements in different areas, so safe zones differ per platform; a common rule of thumb is to keep essential content in the "middle 60%." — [Ignite Social Media](https://www.ignitesocialmedia.com/content-creation/what-are-the-safe-zones-for-tiktoks-and-instagram-reels/); [Reap](https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones)
- [VENDOR, conflicting numbers] One source reports that as of March 2026 Meta Reels and Stories share a unified safe zone: top 14%, bottom 20–35%, and 6% on each side (on a 1440x2560 canvas); another reserves the top 15%. Meta provides a Reels ad safe-zone checker. (Exact page within the safe-zone result cluster not isolated; candidates: [TryMyPost](https://www.trymypost.com/blog/social-media-safe-zones-templates-2026), [Wild and Free Tools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/vertical-video-safe-zones-guide-2026/), [Strike Social](https://strikesocial.com/?p=264470).)
- [VENDOR, single unconfirmed claim] One vendor says that since 2026 AI-generated captions are on by default on TikTok, Reels and Shorts — if true, platform captions can overlap burned-in captions placed low in frame. (Page not isolated within the same cluster; e.g. [Postmypost](https://postmypost.io/resources/vertical-video-2026-safe-zones-text-and-editing-how-to-create-content-that-doesn-t-cut-through-algorithms).)
- [OFFICIAL] Instagram moved the profile grid from square to vertical (announced with the 3-minute Reels change, January 2025), so covers should be designed for a vertical crop. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-announces-3-minute-reels-and-vertical-grids/); [Mosseri on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DE-efFqyStv?hl=en)
- [OFFICIAL] Watermarks/logos from other apps reduce recommendation on Instagram and make content For You-ineligible on TikTok when it is someone else's mark; Meta advises against third-party watermarks on Facebook. — [dot.LA](https://dot.la/instagram-tiktok-content-2657200198.html); [TikTok FYF standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update); [Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/)
- [VENDOR] TikTok search ranks on query match, so spoken keywords, caption keywords and on-screen text matter for discovery. — [Sorank](https://www.sorank.com/blog-geo-seo/tiktok-algorithm)
- [VENDOR] AI clippers automate reframing/speaker tracking and captions; reported caption accuracy around 92–97% on single-speaker talking-head content (vendor benchmarks, conflicting). — [Montage benchmark](https://montage.app/blog/state-of-ai-video-clipping-2026-benchmark-report); [Techsy](https://techsy.io/en/blog/opusclip-vs-vizard)

### Inferences
- For 16:9 gameplay or screen footage, a center crop loses UI and context; a stacked layout (gameplay top, face/captions in the middle band) or deliberate punch-ins fits the safe-zone guidance better than auto-centering. This is an inference; no data study on layouts was found.
- Export one clean master per clip (no app watermark, captions inside the middle band), then add platform-native text/cover/sound in each app — satisfying both the watermark rules (section 4) and TikTok's "new or creative edits" wording.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 statistic for the share of short-form viewing with sound off was found.
- No official safe-zone specifications were retrieved from TikTok, YouTube or Meta pages; all numbers are vendor-reported and conflicting.
- No data on performance of burned-in vs. native captions, cover choices, or trending-sound use for clipped long-form content.

## 9. Additional reach levers beyond clipping

### Takeaway
The best-documented extra levers found are YouTube multi-language audio/auto-dubbing (official: 25%+ of watch time from non-primary-language views for creators using multi-language tracks), paid clipper bounties (market rate about $1 per 1,000 views), and extra short-form surfaces with their own >1-minute monetization rules (Snapchat Spotlight). Several levers named in the brief were not researched to a citable standard — see Gaps.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] YouTube (September 2025) expanded multi-language audio to millions of creators; creators uploading multi-language audio tracks saw on average over 25% of watch time come from views in the video's non-primary language. In 2023 YouTube had reported 15%+. Self-reported, from early adopters, and about creator-uploaded tracks rather than auto-dubbing specifically. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-multi-language-audio-options/759834/)
- [OFFICIAL] February 2026: auto dubbing available to everyone with 27 languages, plus a viewer Preferred Language setting and "expressive speech"; in December (2025) more than 6 million viewers per day on average watched at least 10 minutes of auto-dubbed video. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/unlocking-a-global-audience-with-auto-dubbing/); [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-auto-dubbing-expressive-speech/)
- [DATA/VENDOR] Paid clipping ("content rewards" on Whop-style marketplaces): typical rate about $1 per 1,000 views; an analysis of 708 campaigns (October 14, 2026) found half pay $1 or less; another October 2026 sample of 100 campaigns found $0.20–$5.00 with a $1.30 median; Whop reportedly says clippers average $1 per 1,000 and streamer campaigns pay around $0.50. Platform fee reported as 10% (8% if verified) — sources conflict on who pays it. Campaigns are open to any number of clippers. — [ClipDocker (708 campaigns)](https://clipdocker.com/blog/whop-clipping-rates-2026); [viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/guides/clipping-campaigns); [Reap](https://reap.video/blog/whop-clipping)
- [VENDOR] Snapchat: monetization program shares revenue from Spotlight and Public Stories; one guide gives a threshold of 15,000 hours of view time in 28 days with at least 3,000 from Spotlight; Spotlight videos must be longer than one minute to monetize; as of May 7, 2026 at least 100 hours of Spotlight view time in 28 days is needed for maximum rewards. Payout-per-view estimates conflict by two orders of magnitude ($0.01–$5 per 1,000). — [Storika](https://www.storika.ai/guides/snapchat-creator-monetization-2026); [ShortSync](https://www.shortsync.app/resources/snapchat-spotlight-monetization-2026); [Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/research/social-media-monetization-requirements)
- [VENDOR] X pays ad-revenue share gated on Premium, 500 followers and 5 million post impressions in three months (one source); Threads has no confirmed public payout program; LinkedIn has no per-view payout (BrandLink, advertiser-funded, in beta); Pinterest's creator-rewards status is unclear. — [Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/research/social-media-monetization-requirements); [Elev8or](https://www.elev8or.io/blog/creator-monetization-requirements)
- [OFFICIAL feature] Instagram's sanctioned ways to share others' content are its repost, Story-share and Collab features (relevant to collaborations: a Collab post appears on both accounts without triggering repost rules). — [Blossom](https://blossai.com/blog/instagram-originality-repost-rule); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)
- [OFFICIAL, via trade press] Ritchie's advice to low-reach streamers: clip livestreams into Shorts. — [Tubefilter, Aug 26, 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)
- [VENDOR] TikTok search: keywords in captions and on-screen text drive search ranking; Creator Rewards explicitly scores "search value." — [Sorank](https://www.sorank.com/blog-geo-seo/tiktok-algorithm); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/)

### Inferences
- Clipper bounties at about $1 per 1,000 views buy reach on *other people's* accounts; given sub-0.5% click-through to long-form (section 7), 1 million bounty views (about $1,000) might send on the order of hundreds to low thousands of viewers to YouTube. Their value is awareness, not efficient traffic; and Meta's 2025/2026 repost rules make clip-farm reach on Facebook/Instagram less dependable.
- Auto-dubbing is close to free and applies to the long-form video itself, the asset that actually earns watch hours and RPM — likely a better marginal return than uploading 20 chunks.
- Snapchat Spotlight and TikTok both require >1-minute clips for monetization, so a library of self-contained 61–90 second clips is reusable across both.

### Gaps
- Not researched to a citable standard in this session (no reliable sources retrieved): YouTube search/SEO and title practice, community posts, playlists/series, trends, Reddit/Discord/forum seeding, livestreams, podcasts (audio/video podcast distribution), paid promotion (YouTube Promote, TikTok Promote/Spark Ads, Meta boosts), and LinkedIn/Pinterest/X/Threads reach statistics for repurposed video.
- No independent data on ROI of clipping campaigns for the creator (as opposed to earnings for clippers).
- Auto-dubbing-specific uplift (as opposed to creator-uploaded multi-language tracks) is not published.

## 10. AI clipping tools (OpusClip, Vizard, Descript, CapCut, etc.)

### Takeaway
AI clippers are competent at mechanics (transcription, captions, reframing) and mediocre at judgment: their "virality scores" are not validated against real views, so they are best used as a first-pass candidate generator with human selection and re-editing. Almost all evidence is from vendor-adjacent review blogs; no independent controlled test was found.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR/review] OpusClip scores each clip 0–99 across Hook, Flow, Value and Trend, but publishes no study tying the score to real-world views; a hands-on tester found a 92-score clip could be worse than a 64-score clip and called the score more a marketing feature than a reliable signal. — [Javis Review](https://javisreview.com/review/opusclip-review-2026/); [Techsy](https://techsy.io/en/blog/opusclip-vs-vizard)
- [VENDOR/review] Consensus that OpusClip's moment selection is sharper while Vizard offers better value/upload volume and "gives you a clean clip"; reviewers disagree on which has better captions (OpusClip cited at about 97%; a 2026 benchmark gives OpusClip 92% on single-speaker talking-head content). — [Forkoff buyer's guide](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/clipping-tools-comparison-2026); [Montage benchmark](https://montage.app/blog/state-of-ai-video-clipping-2026-benchmark-report); [AI Alleyway (Vizard review)](https://aialleyway.com/vizard-review/)
- [VENDOR/review] Pricing: OpusClip entry tier around $15/month (review titles cite $15–$19), Vizard Creator tier around $29/month; one review headline cites 10M OpusClip users. — [The Planet Tools](https://theplanettools.ai/tools/opusclip); [LaunchToolsAI](https://launchtoolsai.com/tools/opusclip); [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/opus-clip-vs-vizard-2026)
- [OFFICIAL context] YouTube's inauthentic-content policy targets mass-produced, templated content at channel level, and Meta/Instagram/TikTok all look for "meaningful"/"creative" edits rather than light touches like borders or subtitles (for others' content). — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)

### Inferences
- Risks of fully automated clipping: (1) clips that start/end mid-thought or lack context (the same failure as mechanical chunking); (2) a channel full of same-template auto-clips resembling "mass-produced" content; (3) tool watermarks on free tiers, which would trigger the cross-posting watermark problems in section 4; (4) caption errors on names/jargon at 92–97% accuracy.
- Best use supported by the evidence: let the tool propose 10–20 candidates per video, keep the handful that stand alone, rewrite the first line/hook, fix captions, and export watermark-free.

### Gaps
- No independent measurement of how often AI clippers cut mid-sentence, nor of typical view outcomes for AI-selected vs. human-selected clips.
- Nothing citable was found on Descript or CapCut clip-generation quality specifically, or on CapCut export watermarks/terms in 2026.
- No platform statement on AI-clipped content specifically (beyond general inauthentic/unoriginal rules).

## 11. Realistic growth benchmarks

### Takeaway
Reaching 1,000 subscribers is common among channels that persist but slow: vidIQ's data puts the median at about 16 months and usually more than 40 uploads; only about 8% of channels reach 10K and about 1.3% reach 100K (of channels with at least one subscriber). Figures depend heavily on the denominator and come from a tool vendor's dataset, not from YouTube.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA] vidIQ, July 2026, 61.2M channels with at least one public subscriber: 40.6% had reached 1,000 subscribers, 7.9% had reached 10,000, 1.3% had reached 100,000, 0.13% had reached 1 million. — [vidIQ Subscriber Benchmarks 2026](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-subscriber-benchmarks-2026/); [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-subscriber-growth-statistics/)
- [DATA, different denominator] Older estimates using all channels (including zero-subscriber ones): about 10.1 million channels have 1,000+ subscribers = 8.86% of all channels (about 1 in 12); 10,000+ is about 1.75%. — [Tim Queen](https://timqueen.com/youtube-number-of-channels/); [Indiy](https://indiy.com/how-many-youtube-channels-have-over-1k-subscribers/)
- [DATA, different denominator] ChannelCrawler (October 2026 index of 38.6M channels with 500+ subscribers): 1K–10K is 54.3% of indexed channels; 10K–100K is 11.1%. — [ChannelCrawler](https://channelcrawler.com/insights/youtube-channel-statistics)
- [DATA] vidIQ analysis of 9,414 channels created after 2023 that recently crossed 1,000 subscribers: median time about 16 months; fastest 25% under 8.5 months; slowest 25% nearly two years or longer; 68% needed more than 40 videos; the most common path was 150 or more uploads. Survivorship-biased (only channels that got there). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-to-get-your-first-1000-youtube-subscribers/)
- [VENDOR, weaker] Blogs commonly claim 6–12 months for consistent creators and 12–18 months to full YPP (1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours); a claimed "vidIQ study of 5.3 million channels" could not be confirmed. — [Touhfa](https://touhfa.art/blog/growth/how-long-to-get-1000-youtube-subscribers/); [Growwly](https://growwly.co/blog/how-long-does-it-really-take-to-grow-on-youtube-2026-roadmap/)
- [VENDOR consensus] YPP full monetization thresholds: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts views do not count toward the 4,000 hours. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-to-get-your-first-1000-youtube-subscribers/)
- [TRADE] TikTok Creator Rewards threshold: 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-creator-next/)

### Inferences
- For planning (and sponsorship pitching), a new channel should assume roughly 1–2 years and 40–150+ uploads to reach 1,000 subscribers at the median, with 10K a top-8% outcome and 100K a top-1.3% outcome among channels that have any subscribers. Short-form can accelerate the subscriber count (the "150+ uploads" modal path likely includes Shorts-heavy channels) but does not supply the 4,000 long-form watch hours.
- The vidIQ video-count figure cuts both ways for the chunk strategy: high upload counts are typical of channels that reach 1K, but that says nothing about whether sequential chunks are an effective kind of upload.

### Gaps
- No YouTube-published statistics on share of channels by subscriber tier or time-to-YPP.
- No benchmark for time to 10K or 100K, or for TikTok/Instagram follower growth timelines, was found.
- No benchmark distinguishing Shorts-led vs. long-form-led paths to 1,000 subscribers from a neutral source (vendor claims conflict).
