# Short-form video and cutting clips from a long video

For Shorts, TikToks and Reels written from scratch, and for turning a long video into clips. Platform details are dated November 2026 and came largely from secondary reporting; confirm limits and policies on each platform's own help pages before a user relies on them.

## Why short-form needs its own approach

In long-form the viewer already chose the video through its title and thumbnail, so the opening only has to confirm the choice. In a swipe feed nothing was chosen and leaving costs nothing. The first frame has to stop the thumb, state the question and show there's something worth staying for, all at once. Staying versus swiping is also the first thing the platform learns about the video.

## Writing a short from scratch

Write in this order:

1. **The last line.** Know the payoff before anything else exists.
2. **The hook** — about one second. Start mid-action or on the most striking image. Put the hook on screen as text in frame one so it works muted. Say the question or the result in the first sentence. No greeting, no logo, no "so today".
3. **Two foreshadowing lines** — three seconds or less — that tell the viewer what is coming and why it's worth waiting for.
4. **The body as "but" and "so" beats.** Every line creates or resolves a conflict. Delete any line that only connects ("and then", "let's get started").
5. **Stop on the payoff.** No outro. If the last line can flow back into the first, viewers rewatch without noticing the loop.

Checks:

- One idea per short.
- Does the hook raise more than one question, even though the short answers one?
- Would someone watch it twice?
- Is the payoff as late as possible, but definitely there?
- Does it make sense with the sound off?

Length follows the idea. Platforms describe time watched and completion as signals; none names a target duration.

## Cutting clips from a long video

**Do not chop a video into consecutive one-minute pieces.** Each platform scores every clip on its own, by whether viewers stay in the first seconds, finish, and share. A piece that begins mid-thought, depends on what came before, or ends without a payoff fails those tests however good the full video is. "Part 7 of 23" clips lose most viewers, and a feed of near-identical chunks is the pattern closest to what platforms describe as mass-produced, low-variation content. No controlled study compares chunking with curated clips, but platform guidance and practitioners all point the same way.

**Instead, pick moments that stand alone.**

Where to look:

- **Spikes in the long video's retention graph.** These are moments viewers already rewatched; they are pre-tested hooks.
- The video's high points: the reveal, the biggest laugh, the failure, the surprising result.
- Any self-contained mini-story with its own setup and payoff.
- A single strong claim or tip that makes sense with no context.

For each candidate, check:

- **Hook:** does it grab in the first second or two? If the moment starts slowly, open with its most striking second and then go back.
- **Context:** can a stranger follow it? Add one line of on-screen text if not.
- **Payoff:** does something resolve before it ends?
- **Standalone:** would it be worth watching if the long video didn't exist?

A typical 10–15 minute video yields perhaps three to six clips that pass, not fifteen. A few strong clips beat many weak ones, because weak clips compete with your strong ones for the same followers.

**Re-edit, don't just trim:**

- Reframe to vertical (9:16) around the subject; don't letterbox horizontal footage.
- Add captions, keeping text and faces away from the top and bottom edges where each app's interface sits.
- Write a new first line of on-screen text for each clip.
- Export clean. **Never post a file carrying another app's watermark**; that is the one cross-posting practice the platforms explicitly demote.

Posting your own clip natively on several platforms is not what originality rules target; they are aimed at re-uploads of other people's content and at mass-produced, templated material.

AI clipping tools are useful for transcription, captions and reframing, and for a first list of candidates. Their "virality scores" are not validated against real results, so a human should choose and re-edit.

## Pointing viewers to the long video

Expect very little. Reported click-through from Shorts to long-form is a small fraction of one percent, and YouTube separates Shorts and long-form viewing history, so Shorts viewers aren't automatically shown your long videos. Short-form mostly builds awareness and its own audience.

What helps at the margin:

- Make a specific promise the long video pays off immediately ("the full build, including the part that failed").
- Use YouTube's related-video link on the Short.
- Pin a comment and keep a link in the profile on other platforms.

Treat short-form as worth doing for its own reach, and treat any long-form lift as a bonus.

## Platform notes (late 2026, verify before relying on them)

| Platform | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | up to 3 minutes | Public view counts include every start and replay; judge by engaged views and "viewed vs swiped away". Shorts don't count toward long-form watch-hour thresholds. |
| TikTok | up to 10 minutes recorded | Its creator payout programme requires videos over 1 minute. In one large 2026 dataset, median views rose with length up to 2–3 minutes. |
| Instagram Reels | up to 3 minutes | Reach reported strongest around 30–60 seconds. Sends per reach is one of the signals Instagram's head names. Repost-heavy accounts lose recommendations. |
| Facebook | all video is now Reels, no length cap | Originality enforcement targets repeated reuse of others' content. |

No platform has stated a penalty for posting often, and no platform publishes numeric benchmarks for any short-form metric. Compare each clip against your own previous clips of similar length and topic.

## Short-form review checklist

- [ ] Frame one works as a thumbnail and carries the hook as text
- [ ] The question or result is stated in the first sentence
- [ ] No greeting, intro or wind-up
- [ ] One idea only
- [ ] Every line is a "but" or a "so"
- [ ] Understandable with sound off
- [ ] Payoff is present and lands at the very end
- [ ] No outro, no "follow for more" after the payoff
- [ ] Vertical, captioned, no other app's watermark
- [ ] Makes sense to someone who has never seen the channel
