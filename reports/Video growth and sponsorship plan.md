# Keep the Videos, Rebuild the Rest

The plan's first step holds up, its second needs reworking, and its third should be replaced. Long-form YouTube videos are the right foundation: they are the only format that pays dollars rather than cents per 1,000 views, and they are what sponsors price. Posting every one-minute chunk of each video everywhere is the wrong way to clip, because every platform scores each clip on its own with each viewer and a chunk that opens mid-thought gets swiped; three to eight standalone moments per video, posted natively on all four platforms, is the version the evidence supports. The sponsorship step fails twice. No brand or marketplace found in the research uses a summed cross-platform view count as a threshold, and a bot that emails as many companies as possible picks the lowest-reply design on record (2.1% for campaigns of 500+ recipients against 5.8% under 50), collides with mailbox providers' 0.3% spam-complaint ceiling, needs consent it will not have in Canada and Germany, and spends each brand's one cold first impression on a template. The better route to profit is a short, human-approved pitch list of brands already sponsoring similar channels, layered on income that unlocks earlier and pays more per viewer: affiliates, fan funding and, above all, a product of the creator's own, which for a game-dev channel is the game itself. Viewership grows from ideas, packaging and retention rather than upload count, and the psychology behind that is consistent: people click to close a specific curiosity gap, stay for emotional change and story, and return for a person. Two cautions apply throughout. Nearly all the underlying research was gathered from web-search summaries rather than full-page reads, and several 2026 platform rules cited here (YouTube's new view counting, the February 2027 Partner Program thresholds, Instagram's originality rules) need checking at the official source before any decision depends on them.

## Three verdicts: keep the videos, curate the clips, retire the bot

The plan treats views as the product and sponsors as the only buyer. Each step looks different once it is measured against how platforms rank content, how sponsors buy, and what email law and mailbox providers allow.

| Part of the plan | Verdict | Main reason | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Make videos and post them on YouTube | **Keep** | Long-form is the asset that earns ad dollars and that sponsors price | Add a packaging-first workflow; plan for a one-to-two-year runway |
| 2. Post every one-minute clip on TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and Shorts | **Rework** | Cross-posting your own clips is fine; mechanical chunking is not | Three to eight standalone clips per video, cut at natural boundaries, re-edited natively |
| 3. Reach a combined-view threshold, then bot-email as many companies as possible | **Replace** | Wrong trigger and wrong method | Per-platform staged triggers; 30 to 150 targeted brands; human-approved sends; other revenue first |

### Part 1 holds: long-form is the asset that earns and sells

The best dataset found, about 300 monetized channels tracked from May 2025 to May 2026, puts the **all-niche median long-form RPM near $2.30 per 1,000 views and gaming at $2.05** ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels)). Six long-form creators told Digiday their **Shorts RPMs sat under $0.20** against $3 to $6 for long-form ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/how-youtube-shorts-revenue-compares-to-long-form-video-revenue-for-creators)). Sponsors use the same asset: the standard pricing formula is recent average views per video multiplied by a niche CPM, not subscriber count or lifetime totals ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)). Step 1 is therefore correct, with two expectation corrections.

The first is time. Among 9,414 channels created after 2023 that recently crossed 1,000 subscribers, the **median took about 16 months and 68% needed more than 40 videos** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-to-get-your-first-1000-youtube-subscribers/)); of 61.2 million channels with at least one subscriber, 40.6% had reached 1,000 and only 7.9% had reached 10,000 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-subscriber-benchmarks-2026/)). These are a tool vendor's figures and the first is survivorship-biased, but they are the only benchmarks found. The second is the ad bar. YouTube announced on August 10, 2026 that from **February 1, 2027 new applicants for ad revenue need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours in 365 days, or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days**, double the current requirement ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)). Given the median timeline, a channel starting today should plan on the higher bar. The implication is that a revenue plan cannot wait on ads or on a view threshold.

### Part 2 needs reworking: cross-post, but stop chunking

A 20-minute video cut into consecutive 60-second chunks produces about 20 clips, or 80 uploads across four platforms, and roughly 19 of the 20 open mid-thought. That matters because none of the platforms rewards a clip for belonging to a complete set. YouTube describes its system as "pulling" videos for an individual viewer and using satisfaction surveys alongside watch time ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)); TikTok builds a per-video prediction score in which time spent watching weighs heavily ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/recommendation-system)); Instagram's head names watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top signals ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). Clipping practitioners agree that a clip must make sense alone, with its own hook, context and payoff ([Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/how-to-repurpose-long-form-video-into-clips)), and the one data point on sequential parts is an anecdote in which a Part 1 drew about 80,000 views in a day and its Part 2 drew 21,000 ([DEV Community guide](https://dev.to/agentworkshop/how-to-plan-the-next-part-of-a-tiktok-series-so-part-2-viewers-arent-lost-2026-recap-callback-e1a)). **No independent controlled study compares consecutive chunks with curated moments.** The verdict rests on consistent platform statements and practitioner consensus, not on a measured experiment, and it is the cheapest gap for the creator to close with a test of their own.

The plan also assumes clips feed the long video. YouTube separated Shorts watch history from long-form watch history, so watching a channel's Shorts does not cause its long videos to be recommended ([vidIQ, relaying YouTube](https://vidiq.com/ru/blog/post/youtube-shorts-long-form-viewers-algorithm)). The only conversion figure found is a third-party one: **about 0.04% of Shorts views converting to long-form, against a 0.5% target** ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)). At that rate 100,000 Shorts views send 40 to 50 people to the full video. No data exists for TikTok, Reels or Facebook viewers crossing to YouTube, and a complete chunk-by-chunk upload removes the one reason a viewer would cross, since the whole video is already in the feed.

Cross-posting itself survives scrutiny. Every originality rule found targets other people's content or mass-produced content: Meta penalizes accounts that repeatedly reuse someone else's material ([Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)), and Instagram's April 30, 2026 rules make accounts that primarily post unoriginal content ineligible for non-follower recommendations ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)). The documented self-inflicted risk is another app's watermark, which Instagram has downranked since 2021 ([dot.LA](https://dot.la/instagram-tiktok-content-2657200198.html)) and which TikTok lists among For You-ineligible content ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards?cgversion=2024H1update)). One caveat applies on YouTube: the "inauthentic content" policy targets mass-produced, repetitive, templated uploads and is judged at channel level ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)), and dozens of sequential chunks per video is the pattern closest to that wording. No platform was found stating outright that posting your own clip everywhere is fine; that conclusion is an inference from policy wording.

Neither posting frequency nor the one-minute length carries the weight the plan gives them. Buffer's study of 2.1 million Instagram posts found reach per post rising with weekly frequency, with no penalty point, though the result is correlational and its top bucket is ten or more posts a week, far below a chunk dump ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/study-shows-posting-more-instagram-leads-to-more-reach/757633/)). On YouTube, an 18,000-channel study associates the best outcomes with Shorts at about 25 to 40% of uploads, and finds that **gaming channels without Shorts had 85% more subscribers than gaming channels that mixed them in** ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/should-you-chase-shorts-views-or-double-down-on-long-form-for-channel-growth); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)); AIR itself calls this niche-dependent and not causal. As for length, a clip over 60 seconds matters only on TikTok, whose Creator Rewards Program pays only on original videos longer than one minute ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/)) and where median views rise with length ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-are-tiktok-videos/)). Reels reach peaks around 30 to 60 seconds ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics)), and Shorts now run up to three minutes ([Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be)). Clip boundaries should follow the moment, not the clock.

### Part 3 should be replaced: the trigger and the method both fail

The trigger does not match how the market works. Every eligibility threshold found is measured per platform: TikTok One asks for 10,000 followers ([TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/tiktok-one/tiktok-one)), Instagram's Creator Marketplace has a functional floor near 1,000 ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/instagram-creator-marketplace-eligibility)), Collabstr and Aspire start at 1,000 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/companies-matching-youtubers-with-brand-deals/)), and YouTube BrandConnect requires Partner Program membership ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9385307)). None counts summed cross-platform views. Buyers look at projected 30-day views for the specific upcoming video and at the channel's sponsor history ([ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/what-are-the-key-youtube-influencer-metrics-for-brand-sponsorships)), and vendor benchmarks price a **sponsored Short at roughly 30 to 60% of the same creator's long-form integration** ([InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-pricing/); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-sponsorship-rates)). A combined total also inflates on its own now: since March 31, 2025 a Shorts view counts whenever a Short starts or replays ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)), and trade coverage reports YouTube extended start-based counting to all formats on August 24, 2026 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-how-it-counts-views-on-long-form-live/586166/)). A headline number built mostly from swipe-feed starts answers a question sponsors do not ask.

The method selects the worst-performing design on every available measure. A Belkins analysis of 16.5 million cold emails found **5.8% reply rates for campaigns under 50 recipients against 2.1% for 500 or more**, and personalization roughly doubles replies ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)). Brand-side commentary says mass-produced pitches "are often ignored before they're even opened" ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pitching-brands-via-email/)) and that brands "can usually tell immediately" when a creator mass-sends a template ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pr-email-for-influencers)). The prize is also small: Collabstr's 2026 report puts the **average paid collaboration at $255 on YouTube, $193 on Instagram and $186 on TikTok** ([Collabstr](https://collabstr.com/2026-influencer-marketing-report)). These reply rates are B2B sales benchmarks from email-software vendors; no measured reply rate for creator-to-brand pitches was found.

Deliverability and law turn underperformance into damage. Gmail and Yahoo's sender rules cap spam complaints at 0.3%, and Google recommends staying under 0.1% ([Bird](https://bird.com/explained/deliverability/what-are-the-gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sender-requirements)), which is about three "report spam" clicks per 1,000 delivered messages. Cold-email practitioners hold volume to about 30 emails per inbox per day after two to four weeks of warm-up ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/slow-ramp-plan-for-new-cold-email-domains-deliverability/)), so 3,000 companies would take 100 inbox-days, and they keep cold volume off the primary domain because one failed campaign damages every other message sent from it ([Instantly](https://www.instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-software-mistakes-to-avoid-12-costly-errors-that-tank-deliverability-roi)). In the United States unsolicited commercial email is legal on an opt-out basis, but each non-compliant message carries a penalty of **up to $53,088** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)), and automated address harvesting and scripted creation of multiple sending accounts are listed aggravators that allow treble damages ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/inbox/can-spam_act_problematic_spamming_techniques)). Canada's anti-spam law requires consent, with penalties up to C$10 million for organizations ([CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)), and Germany generally requires prior consent even for business-to-business email ([Morrison Foerster](https://media2.mofo.com/v3/assets/blt5775cc69c999c255/blte030b4975e725a10/642305842cf9c710750b34cb/230328-a-lawyers-guide-to-marketing-compliance.pdf)). No authority was found ruling on whether a creator's sponsorship pitch counts as commercial email; the safe assumption is that it does. Enforcement against one small sender is unlikely in the United States, so the realistic near-term cost is a spam-flagged domain and wasted prospects. This is research, not legal advice.

The reputational evidence supports dismissal rather than blacklisting: no source documents shared blacklists of creators. The real cost is simpler. Each brand can be pitched cold for the first time only once, and a threshold-triggered blast spends that attempt on a template before the channel has its strongest numbers.

### The weaknesses, ranked by the damage they do

The ranking puts first the weaknesses that actively destroy value, then those that misdirect effort, then those that slow the plan down.

| Rank | Weakness | Consequence | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mass bot outreach to "as many companies as possible" | Lowest reply rates, spam-flagged sending domain, per-email legal exposure, consent violations abroad, burned first impressions | Moderate: official mailbox rules and statutes via summaries; reply rates from vendors |
| 2 | Summed cross-platform views as the selling metric | The number sponsors are shown is not the number they buy; short-form views are discounted and now counted as starts | Moderate: consistent vendor and platform sources; no independent valuation study |
| 3 | Sponsorship as the only income, deferred until a threshold | Months or years with no revenue; dependence on the stream with the highest bar and least control | Moderate: surveys and vendor data |
| 4 | Mechanical one-minute chunking | Most clips fail the first seconds; account-level signals fill with weak uploads; closest pattern to "mass-produced" policy wording | Weak to moderate: no controlled test |
| 5 | Assuming clips funnel viewers to the long video | About 0.04% conversion on YouTube; unknown and likely lower off-platform | Weak: one third-party figure |
| 6 | Short-form reach earns almost nothing directly | Shorts under $0.20 per 1,000; Instagram has no open Reels revenue share; TikTok payouts unstable | Moderate |
| 7 | No method for making videos worth watching | Upload count substitutes for idea, packaging and retention, where most growth comes from | Moderate: practitioner testimony |
| 8 | Timeline and threshold risk | Median 16 months to 1,000 subscribers; ad bar doubles for new applicants in February 2027 | Moderate: vendor data, official announcement via trade press |
| 9 | No disclosure or contract layer | Undisclosed sponsorship is a deceptive practice; unpriced usage rights and exclusivity give away value | Moderate: law-firm summaries |

Weakness 3 deserves a note because it is easy to miss. Brand deals are the main income source for 77% of surveyed creators ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://www.influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report/)), yet total dollars paid to creators rose 59% in 2025 while the number of creators in campaigns grew 183% ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/industry-trends/creatoriq-creator-compensation-report)). More creators are dividing a pool that grows more slowly than they do, and gaming sits at the bottom of the price range.

## Reach pays more through owned products than through sponsor emails

### Sponsorship stays in the plan as a small, targeted pipeline

Sponsorship remains worth pursuing; it needs a different metric, a different list and a different sending discipline. The metric to build is the median views per long-form video over the last ten uploads, with top-country share, age band and niche attached, because integrations are quoted as recent average views times niche CPM ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)). Niche moves the price more than size does at small scale. Published 2026 ranges put **gaming integrations at roughly $3 to $15 CPM** ([YTMoneyCalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/); [DEV/Spansa](https://dev.to/tryspansa/youtube-gaming-sponsorship-rates-2026-the-6-15-cpm-niche-13nb)) and **B2B software and developer tools at $40 to $80** ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)). A channel averaging 20,000 views per video quotes about $160 per integration at an $8 gaming CPM and about $500 at $25. All of these tables come from vendors with undisclosed samples and disagree by a factor of two or more, so they are ranges, not benchmarks. For this creator the practical reading is that developer-facing content, such as how the snowboarding game is built, sits in a far better-paid sponsor category than gameplay does. That reading is an inference: no game-dev-specific rate was found.

The lead list should come from brands already buying. Justin Moore, the sponsorship coach whose method is the best documented in this research, tells creators to study comparable channels and note which sponsors recur ([Circle](https://circle.so/de/blog/podcast-sponsorship-guide)), and ThoughtLeaders scores channels on repeat sponsors, which makes early small deals a pricing asset later ([ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/blog/why-brands-should-look-at-a-youtubers-sponsorship-score)). His pitch structure asks for relevance to the brand's current campaigns, organic content the creator has already made about the product, proof of results, and a deliverable that is easy to execute ([Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/justin-moore-sponsorship-strategy/)). Three of those four elements are specific to each recipient, which is why a bulk mailer cannot produce the pitch. Follow-up norms are one nudge after five to seven business days and a stop after three unanswered messages ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/pr-email-for-influencers)).

The bot still has a job. Automation carries no legal or deliverability risk when it compiles which brands sponsor the 20 to 50 closest channels, ranks them by repeat count, drafts a per-brand pitch for human approval, schedules follow-ups and tracks status in a simple CRM. The sending decision and the message body stay with the creator, at 5 to 20 emails a day from an authenticated domain with a real name, postal address and opt-out line, with Canadian and German brands routed to inbound forms and marketplaces instead of cold email. This design is synthesized from separate findings; no source tested it end to end. Using the vendor reply ranges for small personalized campaigns, 100 well-chosen brands would plausibly produce 6 to 18 conversations, which is more usable conversation than a 2,000-company blast at 2% minus spam-folder losses.

On timing, creator-education sources place the start of outreach near 1,000 engaged subscribers in a defined niche, with paid offers easier past 10,000 ([Sellfy](https://sellfy.com/blog/sponsorships-for-youtubers/); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel)). Nano-tier integrations pay about $100 to $500 ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-to-get-brand-deals-small-youtube-channel)). This is consensus advice from sites with an interest in onboarding small creators, not measured outcome data. Once deals arrive, two obligations follow. The FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides require a disclosure that is "difficult to miss" ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/locke-lord-quickstudy-new-ftc-endorsement-guides-what-advertisers-and-influencers-need-to-know/)) and say a platform's built-in label might not be adequate alone ([The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/what-to-know-now-that-the-ftc-has-updated-its-endorsements-guides/)), so a sponsored video needs both the platform toggle and a spoken or on-screen statement. Contracts should itemize the content fee, exclusivity and usage rights as separate lines ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/usage-rights-for-creators)), with paid usage commonly priced near 25% of the base fee per 30 days ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/organic-vs-paid-usage-rights/)), and view guarantees deserve caution on a channel whose views swing widely ([ThoughtLeaders](https://www.thoughtleaders.io/sponsorship-glossary/view-guarantee)).

### Seven income lines unlock before or alongside a first sponsor

The alternatives differ in when they open and what they pay per viewer. The table orders them by how early they are available; thresholds are as reported by the sources cited and belong on the verification list in the final section.

| Income line | Opens at | What it pays | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid video work for brands (UGC) and services | No audience | Median about $175 per video ([Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-price)) | Labor income, not leverage on viewership |
| Affiliate links | No minimum found for software programs; YouTube Shopping affiliate at 500 subscribers since March 2026 ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shopping-expansion-500-subscribers/)) | Software programs typically 20 to 30% recurring ([Refgrow](https://refgrow.com/blog/affiliate-program-benchmarks-2026)); Amazon pays 1 to 2% on video games ([Amazon Associates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)) | Needs content with purchase intent; no per-view benchmark found |
| Email list, Discord, Steam wishlist | Day one | Rule of thumb of about $1 per email subscriber per month if products are sold to the list ([Nathan Barry interview](https://membership.io/blog/myb-nathan-barry)) | Source sells email software; unverified heuristic |
| Fan funding | YouTube memberships and Supers at 500 subscribers plus 3,000 watch hours ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en)); Twitch subs and Bits for most streamers since May 2026 ([Twitch Blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2026/05/13/monetization-for-all/)) | Roughly 0.5 to 3% of subscribers convert to membership ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-memberships-earnings-guide)) | Conversion figures are third-party estimates |
| Owned products (course, assets, templates, tools, a game) | Any size | Margin set by the creator; a vendor survey reports creators with owned products earning about 25% more than social-first creators ([Kajabi](https://kajabi.com/state-of-creator-commerce-25)) | Survey of a product-tool vendor's own customers |
| YouTube long-form ads | 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 hours now, 8,000 for new applicants from February 2027 ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)) | About $2 per 1,000 views in gaming ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels)) | Verify the 2027 thresholds |
| Short-form programs | TikTok Creator Rewards commonly reported at 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in 30 days ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/)) | Cents per 1,000; creators reported sharp TikTok payout drops in 2026 ([EURweb](https://eurweb.com/tiktok-payout-drop/)) | Anecdotal; Instagram has no open Reels revenue share ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-reels-monetization/)) |

The mechanism behind the owned-product row is arithmetic, not a sourced benchmark: at a $2 RPM, 100,000 views earn $200 in ads, while 0.1% of those viewers buying a $20 product earn $2,000. Merchandise is the weakest owned product, netting most sellers $3 to $8 a shirt by a print-on-demand vendor's own account ([Printful](https://www.printful.com/blog/how-much-money-can-custom-t-shirts-make)). Short-form income will shrink further for small channels: YouTube's help page, as summarized, says that from February 1, 2027 Shorts ad revenue requires at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the previous 90 days, including for existing members ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)). If that holds, a small channel's clips earn nothing from Shorts ads, which makes "clips as audience acquisition for something else" the only coherent role for them.

### For a gaming or game-dev channel, the game is the product

The repository's snowboarding game changes the calculation if the videos are about it. Gaming is the lowest-paid category on both ad RPM and sponsor CPM, and affiliate rates on games are 1 to 2% at Amazon and a developer-set share with a 5% minimum on the Epic Games Store ([Epic](https://sac.epicgames.com/overview/)). The upside sits in selling the game, with the channel feeding Steam wishlists. Chris Zukowski's dataset shows sales per wishlist at six months falling from 0.82 for 2020 to 2022 releases to **0.43 for 2023 to 2025 releases**, while wishlists still predict launch performance ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27273/steam-wishlist-inflation-value-of-1-wishlist-cut-in-half-but-predictive-power-remains-unchanged)), and he suggests about 7,000 wishlists before launch ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/03/14/frequently-asked-question-about-marketing-your-game/)). At that ratio 7,000 wishlists imply about 3,000 copies in six months, or about $45,000 gross at a $15 price before the store's cut, refunds and taxes. A modest wishlist count out-earns years of small-channel ad revenue.

The documented cases cut both ways. Thronefall's co-developer said the team "received a lot of the early players from the devlog", but the large spike came from a Steam Next Fest demo picked up by YouTubers ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-how-thronefall-went-minimal)), and Zero Sievert's earliest traffic was led by a YouTube devlog ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/18/how-a-pixel-art-shooter-achieved-a-million-dollar-launch-the-zero-sievert-story/)). Against that, Zukowski calls devlogs a near-full-time time sink ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/03/14/frequently-asked-question-about-marketing-your-game/)), reports that viral social moments traced back to roughly twenty wishlists ([PressKit](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/how-to-build-steam-wishlist)), and one developer argues devlog audiences are mostly other developers who do not buy ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/indie-marketing-is-a-fool-s-errand)). Streamer coverage is the lever he credits most for wishlists ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/tips-strategies-for-marketing-indie-games)). No controlled data isolates wishlists per 1,000 views of a developer's own channel. The decision this forces is which audience the channel serves. Players buy the game; developers buy tutorials, assets and tools and attract better-paying sponsors. A game-dev channel can monetize both, but video views and wishlists should be tracked as separate goals because one does not reliably produce the other.

### The base rates argue for diversifying early

NeoReach's 2025 survey of more than 3,000 creators found **50.71% earning under $15,000 a year** and only 5.69% over $200,000 ([NeoReach](https://neoreach.com/creator-earnings-report)). "Audience first, sponsors later" is the path most creators are on, and it is the path producing that distribution. Creators who publish stronger numbers show a different mix: Jay Clouse's Creator Science reportedly earned about $830,000 in 2024 with roughly half from a paid membership, a figure available here only through secondary reporting ([Niche Pursuits](https://www.nichepursuits.com/jay-clouse-creator-science/)). Platform payouts are also the least controllable line in any plan: within 2026 alone YouTube announced a doubled entry bar and X retired its revenue-sharing program in favor of a new one ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/11/x-monetization-original-content-rewards-program-creators/)).

## Views grow from ideas, packaging and curated clips, not upload volume

The plan's growth engine is volume: more uploads, more surfaces. YouTube's own staff reject that mechanism. The Shorts lead said posting many Shorts a day does not by itself affect performance ([Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/34020532)), and a YouTube director is reported as saying subscribers skip about 90% of uploads in their feed ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)). Large correlational datasets do show more frequent uploaders growing faster, with a vidIQ study of 10.2 million channels finding that moving from one to three videos a month to four to seven lifted median view growth by about 50%, but its authors state the data does not prove weekly uploads cause growth ([Vlix](https://www.vlix.me/digital-marketing/quality-vs-quantity-on-youtube)). The supportable cadence is roughly weekly long-form, at whatever pace still allows a deliberate idea and packaging pass on each video.

The largest lever is the idea and its packaging. Paddy Galloway, the strategist with the deepest client record among those researched, says the vast majority of a channel's growth comes from one idea that is then turned into a repeatable format ([Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1874513765762531734.html)), and that his clients often make ten or more thumbnails per video because packaging is "50% of the game" ([Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/2117334892375896413)). YouTube provides the testing tool: Test & Compare runs up to three titles or thumbnails on one video and **picks the winner by watch time share, not click-through rate** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)). A thumbnail that wins clicks from the wrong viewers loses the test. The post-publish routine the sources support is to compare click-through and early retention against the creator's own previous video, which YouTube's creator liaison gives as the definition of a good CTR ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/08/26/rene-ritchie-shorts-creator-faqs/)), and to map each retention dip to the script segment that caused it.

Clips then become a selective distribution layer. The evidence-consistent version takes three to eight moments per long video that each carry a hook, context and payoff, re-edits each one natively with a new first line and captions, exports a clean file with no app watermark, and posts about one clip per platform per day between long-form uploads. On YouTube, each Short should carry the Related Video link, and the first five to ten seconds of the linked long video should deliver what the Short promised ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)). AI clipping tools help with captions and reframing but not with judgment: a hands-on review found a clip scored 92 by OpusClip could be worse than one scored 64 ([Javis Review](https://javisreview.com/review/opusclip-review-2026/)), so the tool proposes and a person selects. Because public view counts now include starts and replays, clip tests should be judged on engaged views and on the "viewed versus swiped away" figure in YouTube Studio ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)). For a gaming channel, the AIR finding argues for a small number of strong Shorts, tested, over a flood.

Three further levers are documented well enough to recommend. YouTube reports that creators who upload multi-language audio tracks see on average over 25% of watch time from views in a non-primary language ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/)), and auto dubbing became available to everyone in 27 languages in February 2026 ([Google](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/unlocking-a-global-audience-with-auto-dubbing/)); it applies to the long video, the asset that earns. Paid clipper bounties buy reach on other people's accounts at about $1 per 1,000 views, with half of 708 analyzed campaigns paying $1 or less ([ClipDocker](https://clipdocker.com/blog/whop-clipping-rates-2026)); that spend pays back only when the views lead to something that earns, such as a Steam page or a product. On Instagram, sends per reach is the signal Adam Mosseri singles out for reaching non-followers ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)), which favors clips built around one moment worth forwarding. Several other levers were not researched to a citable standard and this report takes no position on them: search optimization, community posts, playlists, livestreams, podcasts, forum and Reddit seeding, and paid promotion.

## People watch to close a gap, feel something and follow a person

The psychology and the practitioner playbooks converge more than creator lore suggests, and the research corrects several pieces of that lore. The table pairs each mechanism with the documented practice that uses it and states how far the evidence reaches.

| Why people watch | What the research found | What successful creators do with it | Evidence reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity about a specific, closable gap | Curiosity peaks at intermediate knowledge ([Kang et al.](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf)); across 27,616 headline experiments, too-vague and too-revealing wording both lost clicks ([Aubin Le Quere and Matias](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11704130/)) | Titles built on curiosity plus fear or desire ([Jake Thomas](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jake-thomas/)); the first five seconds confirm the title and thumbnail ([Aprilynne Alter](https://www.netinfluencer.com/aprilynne-alter-reveals-how-to-make-a-killer-youtube-intro/)) | Strong for news headlines; transfer to video titles is inference |
| Tension that gets resolved | Creating then resolving uncertainty beats giving everything at once ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/how-hook-customers-first-tease-them)); people tend to resume what they started, but the Zeigarnik memory effect failed a 2025 meta-analysis ([Ghibellini and Meier](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)) | Setup, tension and payoff loops every one to two minutes ([George Blackman workshop recap](https://recapio.com/digest/guest-workshop-with-george-blackman-by-creator-crew-workshops)); re-engagement beats near minutes three and six ([MrBeast memo](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)) | Moderate; do not cite Zeigarnik as proof |

| High-arousal emotion worth passing on | Awe, amusement, anger and anxiety raise sharing while sadness lowers it ([Berger and Milkman](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)); in YouTube ads, inspiration, excitement, amusement and warmth raised sharing, while information-heavy content and early, prolonged branding lowered it ([AMA summary of Tellis et al.](https://www.ama.org/2019/06/07/the-hidden-secrets-of-creating-a-viral-youtube-ad/)) | One "wow" moment no similar channel offers ([MrBeast memo](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)); a click-worthy wrapper around substance ([Mark Rober](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/how-mark-rober-hides-science-vegetables-in-viral-videos-transcript)) | Strong for sharing; not tested on watching |
| Story with stakes | Narrative transportation is supported by a meta-analysis of 132 effect sizes ([van Laer et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/673383)); viral ads with four or five dramatic acts averaged over four times the shares ([Quesenberry and Coolsen](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joinma/v48y2019icp1-16.html)) | A stated mission restated every episode ([Ryan Trahan's editor](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/game-changer)); "but" and "so" in place of "and" ([Jenny Hoyos summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/entrepreneurship/the-formula-to-break-100-million-views-on-shorts-ft-jenny-hoyos)) | Strong for transportation; structure finding is correlational |
| Limited working memory | Cutting extraneous material, signaling what matters and segmenting improve learning ([Mayer](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)) | Remove anything that does not serve the title's promise; signpost each segment | Strong for instructional video |
| A person to return to | Parasocial relationships are robust across 120 studies and are not explained by loneliness ([Tukachinsky et al.](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/40aDeVml)); direct address intensifies the experience ([Hartmann and Goldhoorn](https://typeset.io/papers/horton-and-wohl-revisited-exploring-viewers-experience-of-2x3qk052xt)) | Speak to one viewer through the lens; "more personality" ([Tubefilter on MrBeast](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/)) | Strong that the bond exists; return viewing not measured |
| Memory of the peak and the end | A large peak-end effect on how experiences are remembered, with the overall average mattering about as much ([Alaybek et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v170y2022ics0749597822000334.html)) | "Don't ever signal the end of the video" except to build toward the payoff ([MrBeast memo](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)) | Strong in general; applied to video by analogy |

Practitioners agree on an order of operations that follows from this. The idea is filtered first, then title and thumbnail are settled, then the video is scripted to pay off the packaging. Ed Lawrence describes roughly 70% of his process as planning before any content is made ([Passionfroot](https://www.passionfroot.me/creators-on-air/mastering-youtube-strategies-with-ed-lawrence)). The leaked MrBeast production memo, the one primary document in this research that was read in full text, reduces performance to click-through rate, average view duration and average view percentage, calls the first minute "the most important minute of each video", tells staff to stop describing and start showing between minutes one and three, and explains the packaging rule through viewer expectation: a thumbnail that shows something the video does not contain makes the viewer feel lied to and leave ([MrBeast memo](https://github.com/echore/blog/blob/HEAD/content/Main/Mrbeast%20production.md)). Galloway's thumbnail rules are three or fewer major elements and a design that reads in a glance ([Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1450463472907657220); [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1811083499044044951)).

Short-form compresses the same logic, because a swipe feed has no title or thumbnail and the opening frame does all three jobs. Jenny Hoyos, the best-documented Shorts specialist, writes the hook and then the last line before filming, follows the hook with two lines of foreshadowing in three seconds or less ([Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/)), and spends about 20 hours on a single Short ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/09/27/creators-on-the-rise-jenny-hoyos/)). That last figure is the sharpest contrast with the plan: the most successful short-form practitioner researched engineers each clip, where the plan produces twenty by cutting on the minute.

Several widely repeated tips are now wrong. MrBeast publicly reversed his own fast-cut style in March 2024, crediting slower pacing, storytelling and longer videos for higher views and saying of overstimulating content, "It doesn't even work" ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/03/04/mrbeast-editing-style-number-of-cuts-per-video/)). The eight-second attention span traces to an unsourced graphic, and the BBC found no evidence attention spans are shrinking ([BBC, hosted copy](https://internet.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/532-Master/532-UnitPages/Unit-09/Goldfish_MythBusting.pdf)). No research supports a magic cut interval. The shocked-face thumbnail is not a law either: vidIQ's study of 500 breakout videos found exaggerated expressions on about 5% ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/)), and another analysis found faces on 84.2% of breakout thumbnails and 82.2% of underperformers ([Eden](https://eden.so/blog/what-makes-a-good-youtube-thumbnail/)). No peer-reviewed causal evidence on thumbnail features was found, so testing beats rules.

The ethical line doubles as a performance line. Curiosity pulls people toward a payoff regardless of its quality, which is why empty clickbait wins clicks, but YouTube measures post-view satisfaction through surveys and trains its recommendations on the answers ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)). The working test is whether the video delivers the literal promise of its title and thumbnail at a findable timestamp. Out-group and outrage framing reliably raises shares, with each out-group term adding about 67% to the odds of a share in political posts ([University of Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/viralpolitics)), and it is the lever most associated with lost credibility.

Gaming and game-dev craft advice is the thinnest area of the research: no first-hand guidance from named gaming or devlog creators was retrieved. What exists is blog-level opinion that devlog titles should lead with the most interesting moment in place of labels like "Devlog #14" ([GTStudio](https://gtstu.com/indie-game-devlog-audience-growth-youtube-tiktok/)) and that updates should cover a problem and how it was solved rather than list features ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/4969270/how-do-you-write-an-interesting-dev-blog)). The general rules above are the safer guide.

### Where the evidence is weak

This report synthesizes five sets of research notes, and every one records that page fetching was blocked during research, so the findings come from web-search result summaries of the linked pages. The one exception is the MrBeast memo, quoted from a full text copy. Exact policy wording, statutory figures and direct quotations should be treated as "as reported" until read at the source. A second weakness is the kind of source: sponsorship rates, cold-email reply rates, per-inbox limits, short-form payout estimates, clipping advice and most growth benchmarks come from vendors that sell rate calculators, outreach software or creator tools, with undisclosed samples and frequent disagreement. A third is transfer: the strongest psychology findings come from news headlines, advertisements, online courses and laboratory tasks, and their application to creator videos is inference. The specific holes that bear on decisions are the absence of any controlled test of chunked versus curated clips, any measured reply or close rate for creator-to-brand pitches, any conversion data from TikTok, Instagram or Facebook to YouTube, any game-dev-specific ad or sponsor rate, and any first-hand craft advice from gaming or devlog creators. The legal material is research rather than legal advice, and the tax and business-structure side of earning was only briefly researched and is left out here.

## Conclusion

The plan assumes that views are the scarce thing and that a buyer appears once there are enough of them. The research points the other way. Views are an input, and cheap ones: a dollar buys a thousand of them from a clipper. What is scarce is a per-video audience that a specific buyer wants and something of the creator's own to sell to it. That reframing changes where the creator's two real assets belong. The willingness to automate belongs in research, drafting and tracking, where a bot multiplies a person's judgment, instead of in sending, where it multiplies a template. And the game in the repository is plausibly the most valuable thing the channel could sell, which makes the niche question the first decision instead of an open detail: a player-facing channel should be judged on wishlists and a developer-facing one on tool affiliates, products and better-paid sponsors.

The timing also matters more than the plan allows. From February 2027, if the announced rules hold, a small channel's short-form views earn nothing from YouTube and its long-form needs twice the watch time to earn ads, so the old sequence of audience first and money later gets longer for everyone starting now. The plans that survive that change are the ones that earn from the first few hundred viewers. The single most useful thing the creator can do this month costs nothing: run the chunk-versus-curated test on their own first videos, because it is the one question this research could not answer and the one the whole distribution step depends on.
